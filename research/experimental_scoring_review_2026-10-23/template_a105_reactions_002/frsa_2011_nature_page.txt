Abstract
The interaction between fermentation-respiration switch (FrsA) protein and glucose-specific enzyme IIA
Glc
increases glucose fermentation under oxygen-limited conditions. We show that FrsA converts pyruvate to acetaldehyde and carbon dioxide in a cofactor-independent manner and that its pyruvate decarboxylation activity is enhanced by the dephosphorylated form of IIA
Glc
(d-IIA
Glc
). Crystal structures of FrsA and its complex with d-IIA
Glc
revealed residues required for catalysis as well as the structural basis for the activation by d-IIA
Glc
.
