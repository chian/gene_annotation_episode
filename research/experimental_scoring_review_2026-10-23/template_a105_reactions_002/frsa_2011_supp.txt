                Construction of the frsA-deleted mutant

                An 838-bp DNA containing the frsA upstream region was amplified from the genomic DNA

                of V. vulnificus MO6–24/O1 using two primers, frsA-upF (5’-

                ACATGCATGCAATAATCGTTTGCGCAGCTCGATACCC-3’; underlined sequence

                denotes an SphI restriction site) and frsA-upR (5’ -

                GCTCTAGATCGGCATGTATTGAGTCAATGCCGAGG -3’; underlined sequence denotes

                an XbaI restriction site). The PCR product was then cloned into pBluescript SK II(+) to

                produce pSKfrsAup. A 1,118-bp DNA fragment containing the downstream region of the frsA

                gene was made using the primers frsA-downF (5’-

                GCTCTAGAAGGGGATCCGGTCTCGCCATATTCGGA-3’; underlined sequence denotes

                an XbaI restriction site) and frsA-downR (5’-

                GGACTAGTATCCGCTCGAGTGAGCAACATTTGGCC-3’; underlined sequence denotes

                an SpeI restriction site) and cloned into the corresponding sites of pSKfrsAup to result in

                pSKfrsAup/down. A 1,956-bp DNA fragment of pSKfrsAup/down digested with SphI and

                SpeI was ligated into a suicide vector, pDM42, to generate pDM4-frsA. The E. coli

                SM10pir strain3 carrying pDM4-frsA was conjugated with V. vulnificus MO6–24/O, and

                the exconjugants were then selected on thiosulfate citrate bile sucrose medium as described

                previously4. The selected colony was further confirmed by PCR using primers frsA-upF and

                frsA-downR and frsA mutant was named with SM201. For complementation of the mutant,

                a 1,731-bp DNA containing the V. vulnificus frsA gene, which had been amplified using two

                primers, frsA-comF (GCTCTAGAAACTGCGTGAAATGTACCCTAAAGCG-3'; underlined

                sequence denotes an XbaI restriction site) and frsA-comR (5'-




Nature Chemical Biology: doi:10.1038/nchembio.589
                GGGGTACCTTGCCAATAAACGATTGTGGGTAGGG-3'; underlined sequence denotes a

                KpnI restriction site), was cloned into the broad-host-range plasmid pRK415. The resultant

                plasmid, pRK415-frsA, was transformed into E. coli SM10 λpir and then transferred to

                SM201 by conjugation.



                Cloning and purification of FrsA

                Two oligonucleotides, FrsAexp-FK (5’-

                GGGGTACCCCGAATATGTCAGAAGAAGTCAGC-3’; underlined sequence denotes a

                KpnI restriction site) and FrsAexp-RH (5’-

                CCCAAGCTTGTCACCTTAAGAGTTCATCTTCCAGC-3’; underlined sequence denotes a

                HindIII restriction site), were used to amplify a 1,255-bp DNA fragment containing the

                complete open reading frame of the frsA gene from V. vulnificus genomic DNA. KpnI and

                HindIII sites located at both ends of the resultant frsA DNA were used to clone this DNA into

                the pQE30 expression plasmid (Qiagen), to generate a plasmid pQE-frsA. Recombinant FrsA

                was overexpressed in E. coli JM109 (Promega) by adding isopropylthio--D-galactoside at a

                concentration of 0.2 mM, and purified using Ni+-nitrilotriacetic acid affinity column and size

                exclusion chromatography (Supplementary Fig. 1).

                Cloning and purification of IIAGlc

                Two oligonucleotides, Crrexp-F (5’- CGGGATCCGACACAATGGGTCTGTTTGAC -3’;

                underlined sequence denotes a BamHI restriction site) and Crrexp-R (5’-

                AACTGCAGTAGTAATTACTTAGTTACGCG -3’; underlined sequence denotes a PstI

                restriction site), were used to amplify a 522-bp DNA fragment containing the complete open

                reading frame of the crr gene encoding IIAGlc from the genomic DNA of V. vulnificus.

                BamHI and PstI sites located at both ends of the resultant crr DNA were used to generate a

                plasmid pQE-crr. Recombinant phosphorylated IIAGlc or dephosphorylated IIAGlc were

                overexpressed in E. coli JM109 grown in the medium containing 1.0 mM

                phosphoenolpyruvate or 1.0% glucose, respectively, and purified using an Ni+-nitrilotriacetic

                Site-directed mutagenesis of FrsA

                Amino acid residues were mutagenized by overlap extension5 using the primers that include

                the altered nucleotides to substitute alanine for the 53rd arginine, the 203rd aspartic acid, or

                the 272nd arginine. To amplify the DNA encoding R53A FrsA, two sets of primers, FrsAexp-

                FK/ FrsA R53AR (5’-CCACACCCATTGCAAGCGAGCAAGGTTGCG-3’) and FrsAexp-

                RH /FrsA R53AF (5’-CGCAACCTTGCTCGCTTGCAATGGGTGTGG-3’), were utilized.

                Two PCR products were then used as templates for the next PCR using FrsAexp-FK and

                FrsAexp-RH. The resultant mutagenized frsA DNA was digested with KpnI and HindIII, and

                ligated to KpnI/HindIII-digested pQE30 to produce pQE-frsAR53A. Similarly, other sets of

                primers, FrsA D203AR (5’-GGTTTGCAGACTGGCTAATCCCGCACTCAC-3’)/ FrsA

                D203AF (5’-GTGAGTGCGGGATTAGCCAGTCTGCAAACC-3’), FrsA D203NR (5’-

                GGTTTGCAGACTGTTTAATCCCGCACTCAC-3’)/ FrsA D203NF (5’-

                GTGAGTGCGGGATTAAACAGTCTGCAAACC-3’), and FrsA R272AR (5’-

                GTTACCGCCAAAAGCAAAGCCGATCAAACC-3’)/ FrsA R272AF (5’-

                GGTTTGATCGGCTTTGCTTTTGGCGGTAAC-3’), were used to produce pQE-frsAD203A,

                pQE-frsAD203N, and pQE-frsAR272A, respectively. For double mutation of R53A and

                R272A, the primer set, FrsA R272AR/FrsA R272AF, was utilized to amplify the DNA using

                pQE-frsAR53A as a template to produce pQE-frsAR53A/R272A. Nucleotide sequences of all

                the resultant plasmids were confirmed by DNA sequencing and then mobilized into E. coli

                JM109 to purify the recombinant proteins as described above.



                Estimation of enzyme kinetic parameters

                FrsA enzyme assay was performed in 50mM sodium phosphate solutions (pH 7.0) containing




Nature Chemical Biology: doi:10.1038/nchembio.589
                1 mM dithiothreitol and various concentrations of pyruvate. To measure acetaldehyde

                produced from pyruvate, 2 mM NADH and 3.7 U of yeast alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH)

                (Sigma Co.) were included in the reaction mixtures6. Reaction was initiated by adding the

                recombinant FrsA (1 nM) and the reaction mixtures were incubated at 37 oC for 1 min. The

                remaining NADH in the reaction mixtures was continuously determined by

                spectrophotometry at 340 nm. To measure CO2, the enzyme reaction was performed in 50

                mM Tris-HCl buffers (pH 7.0). Carbon dioxide dissolved in the reaction solutions was

                quantified by a spectrophotometry after the reaction solutions were treated with 10 mM

                calcium hydroxide7. Concentrations of pyruvate and acetaldehyde in cell lysates or reaction

                mixtures were measured by the pyruvate assay kit (BioVision) and the acetaldehyde UV-

                method kit (Roche), respectively. To increase the sensitivity of the acetaldehyde UV-method

                kit, the reaction mixtures were further treated with a color reagent (40 mg

                iodonitrotetrazolium chloride and 10 mg phenazene methosulfate in 50ml of 1.0% Tween 20)

                and then spectrophotometrically measured at 490 nm8.



                NMR spectroscopy

                Five millimolar pyruvate, -ketoglutarate, or -ketobutyrate was added to a solution (50 mM

                sodium phosphate and 1 mM dithiothreitol in 95% D2O). Reaction mixtures were incubated

                at 20 oC for 90 min in the absence or presence of 2 M FrsA. After keeping reaction mixtures

                on ice for 2 hrs, they were subjected to the NMR experiments. The spectra were recorded at a
                1
                    H resonance frequency of 500 MHz by use of a Bruker DRX500 spectrometer.



                Mouse mortality tests

                All mice were housed in specific pathogen-free conditions at Yonsei University College of

                Medicine, and all animal experiments were performed in accordance with Institutional




Nature Chemical Biology: doi:10.1038/nchembio.589
                Animal Care and Use Committee guidelines. For determination of LD50, specific pathogen-

                free, 7-week-old, female ICR mice were used without pretreatment with iron dextran.

                Bacterial cultures grown in LBS medium at A600 of 1.0 were harvested, washed in PBS, and

                then resuspended in PBS. One hundred microliters of serial dilutions of the bacterial

                suspension were injected intraperitoneally into six mice per each dilution group. The numbers

                of dead mice were determined at 24 h after the injection, and the LD50 values (colony-

                forming units: CFU) were calculated as described previously9.



                Crystallization, structure determination and refinement of FrsA

                Crystals were obtained at 23 °C by the batch crystallization method with mother liquors of

                1.8 M ammonium sulfate, 0.1 M Tris-HCl (pH 8.5), 0.2 M sodium acetate, and 5 mM

                oxamate. The crystals belonged to the space group P21 with cell parameters a = 60.834, b =

                88.201, c = 79.629 Å and = 106.619º. For data collection, crystals were frozen at -170 °C

                after a brief immersion in a cryoprotectant solution containing 15% ethylene glycol in the

                same mother liquor. A 2.2 Å data set (Supplementary Table 2) was collected at beamline

                17A at Photon Factory, Japan.

                         The structure was solved by the MAD method using three-wavelength data

                sets of SeMet-labeled FrsA (Supplementary Table 2). The 26 selenium positions of

                crystals of SeMet-labeled FrsA were located, and phases were calculated using the

                program Solve. The subsequent solvent flattening by Resolve gave rise to an

                interpretable map, based on which the de novo model building of FrsA was easily

                completed. The model building was performed using Quanta and refinement was

                done with maximum likelihood algorithm implemented in CNS (Supplementary

                Table 2).




Nature Chemical Biology: doi:10.1038/nchembio.589
                Model building of the FrsA/puruvate complex

                It is well established that the oxyanion hole that exists commonly in / hydrolase structures

                accommodates the carbonyl oxygen of substrates in ester or peptide hydrolysis reactions10.

                Based upon the “solid docking" module in QUANTA (Molecular Simulation, Inc.), pyruvate

                is positioned to locate its carbonyl oxygen between the backbone -NH groups of Leu202 and

                Phe273 that correspond to the oxyanion hole in / hydrolases. The module allows a

                successful docking only when an electrostatic and geometric complementarity is

                accomplished between a host and a guest molecule.



                Crystallization, structure determination and refinement of the FrsA/d-IIAGlc complex

                Purified FrsA and IIAGlc proteins were mixed with 1:1 molar ratio and the mixture was used

                for crystallization. Crystals were obtained at 23 °C by the batch crystallization method with

                mother liquors of 20 % polyethylene glycol monomethyl ether 550, 0.1 M MES (pH 6.5), and

                0.01 M zinc acetate. The crystals belonged to the space group C2 with cell parameters a =

                322.87, b = 62.07, c = 126.36 Å and = 110.8º. For data collection, crystals were frozen at -

                170 °C after a brief immersion in a cryoprotectant solution containing 15% ethylene glycol in

                the same mother liquor. A 2.2 Å data set (Supplementary Table 3) was collected at beamline

                17A at Photon Factory, Japan.

                         The structure was solved by the molecular replacement method using MolRep in the

                CCP4 program suite. The crystal structures of FrsA and IIAGlc (PDB ID; 1O2F) was used as

                search models. The model building was performed using Quanta and refinement was done

                with maximum likelihood algorithm implemented in CNS (Supplementary Table 3).
