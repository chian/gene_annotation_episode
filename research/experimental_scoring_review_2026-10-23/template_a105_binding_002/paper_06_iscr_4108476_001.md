Research proposal, 2026-10-23. Giel et al., *Regulation of iron-sulfur cluster
homeostasis through transcriptional control of the Isc pathway by [2Fe-2S]-IscR
in Escherichia coli*, Mol Microbiol 87:478–492 (online 2012; issue 2013),
DOI 10.1111/mmi.12052, [PMC4108476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4108476/).
Primary binding Results, DNase-footprinting Methods, fluorescence Methods and
Fig. 4 were inspected; the latter was viewed as an image. Source text/image are
`iscr_4108476_primary.txt` and `iscr_4108476_fig4.jpg`. Reporter expression and
organism perturbation results are not A105 binding measurements.

## Experimental definition and source values

E. coli WT IscR and IscR-C92A/C98A/C104A were separately purified by previously
published protocols; this article does not fully restate all expression/tag/
purification details. WT and mutant are in separate tubes. WT preparations for
all in vitro experiments are ≥50% occupied with Fe-S clusters, so WT protein
itself contains coexisting cluster states. The intended comparison is cluster
dependence of type-1 DNA binding; sequence substitution and purification/state
history differ as well. Those effects cannot all be attributed solely to the
absence of the cluster.

The affinity assay has 5 nM Texas Red-labelled 30-mer dsDNA containing either
PiscR site A or site B, 40 mM Tris-Cl pH 7.9, 150 mM KCl, 10 minutes at room
temperature under anaerobic conditions. All protein concentrations are reported
as monomers, although IscR is mainly dimeric. WT input spans 20–718 nM; mutant
35–1000 nM. Thus the highest doses differ intentionally to examine weak binding;
the two full dose ranges are not point-matched comparisons.

WT apparent Kd values from Hill fits are **93 ± 5 nM for site A** and
**112 ± 2 nM for site B**. Fig. 4C bound fractions were corrected for fluorescence
quenching and display standard errors of triplicate experiments; Methods specify
three independent occasions. The text does not prove independent protein
purifications or that the fitted Kd ± values are the same statistical object as
the individual plotted error bars. The values describe the mixed WT preparation,
not pure fully active holo-IscR.

For the triple mutant, saturation was not attained at the highest tested
concentration, 1000 nM, and the authors did not determine Kd. Fig. 4B visibly
shows increasing mutant anisotropy at high concentrations; this is not zero
binding. The mutant saturation amplitude is not measured. Consequently neither
`Kd=1000 nM` nor `Kd>1000 nM` follows automatically from "not saturated at 1000".

DNase I protection used a separately labelled promoter fragment, −161 to +38
relative to the transcription start site, with 10-minute protein preincubation.
WT DNA and individual site mutations were compared. Mutating site A removed
protection at −67 to −41, while B/C protection remained; mutating B removed B
protection while A/C remained; mutating C removed C protection while A/B remained.
This tests spatial specificity of binding/protection. Raw band intensities,
cleavage normalization and quantitative uncertainty were not extracted here.
Footprinting is complementary localization, not another Kd replicate. Whether
binding subsequently changes transcription is a separate inference.

## Numerical conversion into the user's components

**Magnitude and selectivity.** For the two WT sites, the derived midpoint ratio
`Kapp_B/Kapp_A=112/93=1.204301`, and `d=ln(112/93)=0.185900830`. This measures
relative apparent affinity under this assay, not evidence strength by itself.
If the reported ± values were independent SEs, conditional propagation gives
`SE_d=sqrt((2/112)^2+(5/93)^2)=0.0566475`; their interpretation and covariance
must be verified before treating this illustration as inferential uncertainty.
No WT/mutant affinity ratio can be populated from an unidentifiable mutant Kd.

**Comparability outside intended variables.** In the A/B comparison, DNA
sequence is the intended variable, while length, label chemistry, buffer and
incubation are shared. A sequence-dependent optical endpoint is still a possible
nuisance. Use per-DNA free and saturated anisotropies plus quenching calibration,
not raw anisotropy subtraction across sequences. For WT/mutant comparisons,
preparation and active-concentration differences remain explicit nuisance inputs.
Use measured response models to compute `b_cmp` and its uncertainty, not ratios
of pH, minutes or protein range endpoints as comparability grades.

**Isolation of effect.** Under a deliberately simplified model in which only
holo protein binds, all holo protein is active, apo does not compete or form
mixed oligomers, and depletion is negligible, the active concentration is
`P_active=f_holo P_total` and `K_active=f_holo Kapp`. The measured lower bound
`f_holo≥0.5` would yield illustrative ranges **46.5–93 nM** (A) and **56–112 nM**
(B). These are conditional transformations, not corrected paper affinities.
Mixed dimers, residual apo binding and cooperative species invalidate that
simple conversion. Measured cluster occupancy informs the species model rather
than supplying a 0.5 reliability score. Active-protein quantitation and purified
state comparisons would address this isolation limit.

**Validity and uncertainty.** The response is anisotropy, not direct molecule
counts. The bound-fraction calibration and quenching correction are measurement
components already used by the source. Propagate their uncertainty once.
Unknown mutant saturation amplitude creates a parameter-identifiability problem,
which an arbitrarily larger error bar on an invented Kd does not fix. Keep the
raw response curve, concentration range and profile-likelihood region if it can
be fitted. Additional saturation or an independent bound-state signal calibration
would resolve the amplitude/affinity tradeoff.

**Explicit proposed compilation.** A response-level formulation preserves these
distinctions without inventing missing affinities. For experiment e and dose i,
define calibrated expected signal `μ_ei(θ,x_e,z_e)`, where θ describes the
binding parameters, x the nuisance assay conditions, and z the coexisting
protein-state fractions/active concentrations. Comparability enters through x;
effect isolation enters through z. Fit the joint data including references and
controls, with measured covariance Σ_e. For an explicitly defined binding claim
H versus a specified alternative H0, a continuous evidence component is

`S_e = ln L_e(data | H, x_e,z_e) − ln L_e(data | H0,x_e,z_e)`.

For independent experiment blocks, `S_A105=Σ_e S_e`, equivalently the likelihood
ratios multiply. Shared calibration/preparation parameters remain in a joint
block rather than making each concentration or derived ratio independent.
Parameters/nuisance integration or profiling must be specified identically for
the compared models; this paper alone does not choose those models or supply
the raw covariance. A target relationship requires an explicit measured mapping
from studied binding parameters to target predictions. Without it, retain
source-system evidence and missing target contribution. This is a proposed
binding-family interface, not an adopted probability/credit scale.

For a well-identified scalar endpoint, the equivalent local contrast formulation
is `d_target=d−b_cmp−b_other+b_correspondence` with summed variances/covariances;
`E=d_target/sqrt(V_total)` is an uncertainty-standardized component. Never add E
to the response likelihood from which it was derived as another independent
piece of evidence. Neither formulation assigns points for the paper count.

**Consistency and target/system relevance.** Direct titration, site-mutated
footprints and reporter expression answer different questions. The latter does
not repair a missing in vitro Kd and is kept outside A105. Multiple IscR studies
may use different state compositions, sequences, glycerol and incubation;
numerical differences are not automatically replication failures. Relevance to
a new target can use identity, measured sequence/structure/site correspondence,
interchangeability or other applicable evidence. There is no invented relation
to ybhP or a generic homology multiplier here.
