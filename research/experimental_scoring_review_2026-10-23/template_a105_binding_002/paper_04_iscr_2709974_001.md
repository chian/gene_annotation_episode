Research proposal, 2026-10-23. Source: Nesbit et al., *Sequence-specific binding
to a subset of IscR-regulated promoters does not require IscR Fe-S cluster
ligation*, J Mol Biol (2009), [PMC2709974](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2709974/).
Primary binding Results, Table 1, Figures 3/5/6/8 and binding/purification Methods
were inspected. Parsed primary text is retained in `iscr_2709974_primary.txt`.
This is a new review of the assigned source, not a review of old categorical

## Actual comparisons and measurements

The subjects are E. coli WT IscR and separately purified IscR-C92A/C98A/C104A.
They are not mixed together. The WT preparation used in the principal binding
comparison is 60% Fe-S occupied, so its tube contains an unresolved mixture of
cluster states. The mutant lacks the cluster. The intended comparison is the
ability to bind the type-2 hyaA site without cluster ligation; mutation and
different preparation histories are additional differences, not a clean
chemical removal of only one cofactor from identical molecules.

WT was purified from PK7599 and the triple mutant from PK7881; mutant
purification was aerobic at 4°C. Other experiments used anaerobic C92A
preparations or dipyridyl-treated apo protein. These should not be collapsed
into one identical protein preparation. Protein concentration used a
colorimetric assay standardized by amino-acid composition analysis.

Equilibrium titrations use 5 nM Texas Red-labelled 30-mer double-stranded DNA
containing the 26-bp hyaA type-2 site, 7–200 nM IscR, 40 mM Tris-Cl pH 7.9,
150 mM KCl, and 10 min at room temperature. Concentrations are IscR protomers.
Main experiments are aerobic; analogous anaerobic behavior is described without
shown measurements. Randomized site DNA is a specificity control. Low salt
(30 or 70 mM KCl) caused nonspecific binding in unshown experiments, so salt is
an empirically important nuisance variable; 70/150 is not a comparability score.

Affinity assays were repeated on three independent occasions/separate days.
This does not identify three independent protein purifications. The read text
does not identify the meaning of the reported ± quantities, so they cannot
automatically be entered as standard errors. The fitted curve is

`A = A_free + (A_bound−A_free) P^n/(K_app^n+P^n)`.

`A_free` is measured without protein; `A_bound` is measured at 200 nM for this
titration. The use of total input concentration, cooperative binding and mixed
WT cluster states all qualify `K_app`. It is not a monovalent thermodynamic Kd.

Separate high-DNA experiments used 250 nM DNA. Methods give 268–1600 nM protein;
the Fig. 5 caption gives 269–1000 nM. Retain this discrepancy instead of silently
choosing one range. WT fluorescence quenching was measured as
`q = total fluorescence at 1600 nM protein / total fluorescence at 0 protein`,
with q = 0.87–0.91. The paper corrects its stoichiometric bound fraction:

`f = (A−A_free)/[(A_bound−A)q + A−A_free]`.

The mutant did not quench and uses `f=(A−A_free)/(A_bound−A_free)`. Here saturation
is the 1600 nM condition. A shared calibrated fluorescence endpoint must not be
treated as a separate independent observation just because it yields both f
and a stoichiometry.

Competition assays held labelled DNA at 5 nM and triple-mutant IscR at 55 nM,
with 8–1000 nM unlabelled 30-mer competitors. Protein was added last. The
reported curve is `f = f_max[1−C/(IC50+C)]`, where `f_max` is the 55 nM protein
condition without competitor. IC50 is an inhibition midpoint under these
specific ligand/protein concentrations; it is not automatically Kd.

## Conversion into numerical template components

**Signal and specificity.** The measured signal is fluorescence anisotropy,
and the measured specificity comparisons are the competition curves. A
dimensionless effect for a declared competitor is
`d_j=ln(IC50_j/IC50_WT)`; larger positive d indicates a weaker competitor under
this assay. The random-site comparison supplies `d_random>ln(1000/29)`.
This is a lower bound on selectivity against this tested random sequence, not
selectivity against every genomic sequence. Raw traces, error terms and shared
control covariance are missing for uncertainty-aware compilation.

**Comparability outside the intended variable.** Identical buffer, DNA,
incubation and calibration can share nuisance parameters in a joint fit. A
measured response model `g(x)` for a nuisance x supplies an endpoint correction
`b_cmp=g(x_test)−g(x_reference)` and its variance. Without that response model,
salt ratios or room-temperature similarity do not yield a numerical penalty.
Separate purification/cluster histories require explicit nuisance operands.

**Isolation of effect.** A 60% holo WT preparation is not an isolation score
of 0.60. Its curve combines interacting species, and DNA binding involves about
four protomers. The authors report <1.4-fold variation among preparations with
42%, 60% and 70% cluster occupancy, but do not supply individual curves/errors.
That limits a mixture effect within the tested range; it does not identify the
affinity of pure holo protein. A simple `0.6 f_holo+0.4 f_apo` model additionally
requires no species competition, no heteromer effect and a valid signal mixture
model. Those requirements are not established here. Fit species-specific
concentrations/affinities in a justified mass-balance model, or retain the
mixed-preparation estimand. Do not multiply all evidence by 0.60.

**Validity and calibration.** Quenching is a measured assay-specific effect
with a defined correction. At a synthetic exact midpoint in anisotropy, the
source's q range changes f from the naive 0.5 to 0.52356–0.53476. This illustration
uses source q values with an invented midpoint. Once q is included in the fit,
its uncertainty enters propagated variance; a second generic quenching penalty
would count the same correction twice.

**Depletion and active concentration.** Five nM DNA has approximately 20 nM
protomer capacity under a four-protomer-per-DNA approximation. This is not
negligible beside a 34 nM apparent midpoint. Under an explicitly hypothetical
all-or-none four-protomer model, half-occupancy binds 10 nM protomer. Simply
subtracting 10 nM from the fitted midpoint is not justified without the actual
binding-species model, free concentrations and active protein fraction. Keep
the paper's apparent midpoint and flag the missing fit operands.

**Consistency/uncertainty.** Derived WT-to-mutant midpoint ratio is
`44/34=1.294118`; log ratio is `0.257829`. If and only if the ± values were
independent standard errors, propagation would give
`SE_log=sqrt((3/44)^2+(2/34)^2)=0.090053` (conditional illustration, not a reported
confidence interval). Shared calibration would add a covariance term. Three
days quantify repeatability only to the extent the replicate unit is known.
An equivalence claim also needs a declared biologically meaningful margin;
failure to detect a difference alone is insufficient.

**Explicit combination proposal.** For a stated binding comparison, retain a
continuous estimated effect d and its corrected target estimate
`d_target=d−b_cmp−b_other+b_correspondence`. The nuisance correction for conditions
and the correction for contaminating/coexisting molecular contributors remain
separate operands. Compile the uncertainty using

`V_total=V_measurement+V_cmp+V_other+V_correspondence+2Σ_covariances`,

then `E=d_target/sqrt(V_total)` when the sign of d defines support for the
specific claim. This is a proposed uncertainty-standardized evidence component,
not a calibrated annotation probability. A binding-detection model can instead
retain the raw curve likelihood; the empirical Kd and its derived ratio must
not both enter as independent factors. Unknown nuisance and correspondence
terms remain unknown, not zeros. A final annotation-credit mapping and target
correspondence are not supplied by this paper, so this file does not complete
the common sheet.

**Dependence and correspondence.** For competition log-ratios, all values
share WT IC50: `Cov(d_i,d_j)` contains `Var(ln IC50_WT)` and any shared protein/
calibration terms. A joint likelihood or covariance matrix preserves that;
adding many ratios as independent evidence exaggerates support. The same
sequence, a measured relevant site, structure or demonstrated interchangeability
could connect this observation to a target; no target relationship is invented
for ybhP. Binding at hyaA does not itself prove transcriptional repression.
