# IscR redox comparison: binding similarity needs an uncertainty bound

Fleischhacker et al., *Characterization of the [2Fe-2S] cluster of the
Escherichia coli transcription factor IscR*, Biochemistry (2012),
[PMC3447993](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3447993/).
Research conversion for A105; no production scoring change.

The direct binding experiment is Figure 9. The subject is wild-type
*E. coli* IscR isolated anaerobically from strain PK8581, exposed to oxygen
for five minutes, and tested anaerobically with dsDNA containing the iscR
site from −42 to −12 relative to the transcription start. The same protein
sequence is compared under 0 versus 10 micromolar dithionite. Protein
concentration is reported per monomer. There is no deliberately mixed
WT/mutant protein preparation. Oxidized and reduced cluster populations may
coexist within each treatment; exact fractions in each binding tube are not
given. The intended variable is cluster oxidation state. The redox reagent
is an additional chemical difference which could also affect the optical
reporter or protein state, and must be tested rather than assumed harmless.

The endpoint is fluorescence anisotropy plotted against nominal IscR
concentration. Figure 9 shows binding curves for both treatments. Visually,
both span approximately 0.128 to 0.181 anisotropy units, a response change
of approximately 0.053. These are approximate visual readings from the
published image, not reported tabular measurements, digitized raw data, Kd
fits or a quantified treatment difference. The source describes similar
affinity. It supplies no fitted numerical Kd, binding-curve replication
count, error-bar definition, active-protein fraction or quantitative
equivalence limit in this paper's binding Methods/caption. The binding
protocol is referenced to an earlier paper; its full buffer, DNA
concentration and incubation details are not restated here.

The three-independent-isolate statement applies to the lacZ assays, not
Figure 9. It cannot be used as the binding replicate count. Likewise the
8% iron contaminant discussed for a dithionite-reduced Mössbauer sample
must not become an 8% inactive-protein correction to the separate binding
samples. Fe-atom fraction and IscR-molecule fraction have different
denominators. Cluster spectroscopy and proposed ligand contacts belong
to their actual structural/chemical interpretation, while genetic
reporter effects remain outside this direct-binding A105 row.

Proposed quantitative cells in the user's template:

| User consideration | Exact proposed computation and missing operands |
|---|---|
| Signal | For each concentration c, delta_r(c)=r_with_protein(c)−r_DNA_only in anisotropy units. The plotted low-concentration plateau is not automatically a separately measured DNA-only blank. |
| Measurement validity | If bound and free fluorophores have equal brightness and only two fluorescence states exist, bound fraction f=(r−r_free)/(r_bound−r_free). Otherwise use the measured brightness ratio q and the intensity-weighted two-state model; no unconditional linear occupancy conversion. |
| Comparison outside intended variable | Correct each curve for separately measured reagent-only optical effects and any changed active concentration. Dithionite concentration itself is not a comparability score. A reference reporter exposed to both reagents provides b_reagent(c) and its uncertainty. |
| Isolation | A binding tube's holo/apo and oxidized/reduced fractions require measured state abundances and a mixture binding model. Total monomer concentration is not automatically active site concentration. State-purity uncertainty is separate from optical comparability. |
| Consistency/equivalence | Fit a defined model to independent preparations, retain d=ln(Kd_reduced/Kd_oxidized), SE(d) and covariance. d near zero without a narrow interval is weak evidence of equivalence. |
| System relevance | A redox result for this IscR/site cannot by itself establish redox-insensitive binding to other sites, other IscR homologs, or an unidentified target. A measured mapping must predict the target contrast and propagate its error. |

An explicit proposed combination for a treatment effect at fixed concentration
is delta_corrected=delta_r,reduced−delta_r,oxidized−b_reagent−b_state_mix.
Its total variance is a' Sigma a, where Sigma includes the two anisotropies,
shared blank, reagent calibration, mixture estimate and target prediction.
Then E=delta_corrected/sqrt(a' Sigma a) is a signed uncertainty-standardized
effect. This evaluates a redox difference, not the separate claim that
binding occurs; the latter uses the protein-versus-blank contrast. Treating
the same curve as independent evidence for both claims would double count
its measurement uncertainty in a combined annotation score.

For an emerged claim of practical equivalence, the proposed numerical cell is
M_eq=(epsilon−abs(d))/SE_total(d), where epsilon is an explicitly justified
maximum tolerable log-affinity difference. Positive M_eq measures how far
the estimate lies inside that margin in SE units; it is not an annotation
probability. **Synthetic illustration:** d=ln(1.1), SE_total=0.10 and a
chosen two-fold margin epsilon=ln(2) gives M_eq=5.97837. This two-fold
margin is an illustration, not a proposed universal rule or paper value.
The real paper cannot populate this cell without fitted estimates,
uncertainty, covariance and a claim-specific margin. A zero effect score
from a difference test must not erase evidence of binding in both states.

Complementary measurements would separate reagent effects from redox state:
DNA-only fluorescence with/without dithionite; total fluorescence/brightness;
state occupancy measured in the actual binding preparations; reversible
redox treatment with cluster recovery; and independent preparations fitted
jointly. These address named biases, not automatic extra independent votes.
