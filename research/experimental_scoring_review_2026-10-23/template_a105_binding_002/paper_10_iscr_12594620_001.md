## Define the experiment first

The assayed molecule is purified *E. coli* IscR. Wild-type preparations contain a mixture of cluster-bound and cluster-free protein: the paper's iron/protein assay yields average cluster occupancy between 50% and 70%. This is not a measured 50–70% fraction of correctly folded, DNA-binding-active molecules. The source determines iron after TCA precipitation with TPTZ, subtracts buffer iron, and assumes two Fe atoms per holo-IscR monomer. Protein concentration is measured by Bradford Plus. The C92A apo variant is separately isolated; it is not mixed with wild-type protein in the control binding reactions. A His6-tagged C92A variant is also prepared for Western-blot standards; do not transfer that tag assignment to the binding construct. Exact expression strain/complete construct boundaries for the purified wild-type material are referred to earlier protocols rather than fully specified here.

The intended variable in the direct wild-type binding comparison is PMS exposure or oxygen/redox treatment of the same protein species and DNA motif. In the mechanistic comparison, the question becomes whether the cluster's oxidation state explains that change. Redox state is dynamic: the condition at purification is not necessarily the state at DNA measurement.

**Fluorescence anisotropy, Figure 4c and Methods 4.6.** Anaerobically isolate IscR, reduce a chromatography fraction with 1 mM dithionite for 15 min, then remove dithionite through BioRex-70 chromatography. Use 30-bp Texas Red-labelled type-1 iscR B DNA (sequence specified in Methods). Incubate varying IscR concentrations in 500 µl of 40 mM Tris-Cl pH 7.9, 30 mM KCl and 5% glycerol, with or without 2 µM PMS for 15 min in a Coy chamber, then add DNA and incubate another 15 min. Seal assay tubes before removal for measurement. The final DNA concentration is not stated in the inspected Methods or Figure 4 caption. The plotted protein range extends to about 100 nM; the textual apparent-affinity bound above 1000 nM is the authors' reported bound, not a concentration range directly tested to 1000 nM in the displayed curve.

The oxygen branches expose protein to air for 15 min, followed by measurement in air, or return it to the anaerobic chamber with a 15 min room-temperature incubation before measurement. These branches differ in the environment maintained during the assay, not just in a prior label 'oxidized'. Triplicate assays have plotted SD between replicates. Independence of purification preparations, fitted-parameter uncertainty, fit model/Hill coefficient, fluorescence-brightness corrections and detailed raw replicates are not given in the inspected section.

Source values: untreated anaerobic apparent Kd = 22 nM; PMS-treated anaerobic apparent Kd >1000 nM; air-exposed then returned to anaerobiosis apparent Kd = 26 nM. Air-exposed and assayed in air is similarly impaired in Figure 4c, without a separately supplied numeric fitted Kd in the inspected text. The authors describe the PMS effect as at least 50-fold. Arithmetic using their printed values establishes `>1000/22 = >45.4545`, or `log ratio >3.816712826`; a mathematically exact ≥50 bound does not follow from the rounded displayed numbers alone. Preserve both the source wording and this derived bound without silently substituting one for the other. The restored/untreated ratio is `26/22 = 1.18181818`, log ratio 0.167054085. No error on that contrast can be computed from the displayed Kd values alone.

**EMSA, Figure 4a,b and Methods 4.7.** Wild-type IscR 15, 30 or 60 nM is incubated with/without 240 nM PMS for 15 min anaerobically in 40 mM Tris-Cl pH 7.9, 150 mM KCl, 10 mM MgCl2 and 10% glycerol; then add 4.5 nM type-1 erpA DNA (−300 to +40 relative to start codon), final volume 10 µl, 30 min at 37 °C. Run a 6% native gel for 4 h anaerobically, using the specified MOPS/Tris/glycerol running buffer, stain with SYBR Green, image with Azure 600. PMS diminishes the shifted material. The separate apo-C92A control uses 3.75, 7.5 or 15 nM protein with 6 nM type-2 hya DNA (−200 to +40 relative to transcription start) under the same stated treatment procedure; PMS does not visibly impair its shifted material. This helps exclude a completely nonspecific PMS inhibition of all DNA binding. However, mutant, DNA identity/length/type, DNA dose and protein dose all change between the holo/type-1 and apo/type-2 experiments. Their difference is not a single-variable quantitative test of cluster state. No calibrated lane fractions or uncertainties are supplied.

**Complementary cluster assay, Figure 3 and Methods 4.5.** At 5 µM IscR, 20 µM PMS, 10 mM potassium phosphate pH 7.4 and 200 mM KCl, anaerobic PMS does not give the increased ferene A593 signal interpreted as released iron; PMS plus air does. SOD inhibits the latter. Normalization uses approximately complete release of cluster iron. This concerns cofactor loss, with different concentrations and buffer from the 2 µM-PMS binding assay. A420(t)/A420(0) is a spectroscopic relative-cluster measure under the stated interpretation, not DNA occupancy or active-protein fraction. Neither these time courses nor the chemical cluster measurements automatically provide contemporaneous oxidation-state fractions in each anisotropy cuvette.

Transcriptional reporter, abundance and growth/viability responses remain outside this binding calculation. They cannot be added as extra affinity replicates or used to turn binding into proof of transcriptional regulation.

## Fit the findings into the user's components

### Comparability outside the variable being tested

The controlled ±PMS anisotropy pair shares wild-type protein identity, type-1 DNA, buffer and timing as reported. PMS presence is the intended variable; its mere difference must not reduce a comparability score. If the narrower claim is specifically redox-mediated binding loss, the redox state becomes the intended mechanistic variable and direct PMS photophysics, residual reductant, protein aggregation, effective active dose and elapsed time become possible alternative contributors that need their own measured terms.

A numerical comparability component can be a measured predicted response difference from nuisance conditions, not a count of equal metadata. For log-affinity contrast `d = ln(K_treat/K_control)`, use `b_cmp = sum beta_k Delta_x_k`, excluding the intended variable, with sensitivity beta_k measured in the same response units and region of the assay. Propagate `v_cmp` using the full covariance of the sensitivities and condition measurements. To compare a 30 mM KCl anisotropy result with earlier 150 mM KCl results, the relevant quantity is a measured `d ln K / d[KCl]` or a validated salt-dependence curve. `30/150=0.2` would be an arbitrary comparison score and is not used. No such salt-response model is supplied by this paper, so a standardized cross-protocol Kd is not calculated.

The air-to-anaerobic branch shows why two nominally 'oxidized' samples are not demonstrably comparable when their states during readout differ. Time-dependent state measurements and a state-transition model would quantify this difference; a 15 min/5 min time ratio would not.

### Separate isolation of the measured effect

The treatment contrast estimates a change in the preparation's DNA-binding response. Attributing that change specifically to a transition in cluster oxidation requires excluding cluster loss, changed active concentration, direct DNA effects and altered fluorophore response. Iron release helps address cluster loss; the C92A/type-2 EMSA helps address generic impairment of binding; a second optical/nonoptical readout helps with anisotropy artifacts. Each control addresses a distinct alternative and does not supply a universal numeric 'validity' multiplier.

An explicit mixture model would use `P_red = P_total*f_holo*f_red(t)`, `P_ox = P_total*f_holo*(1-f_red(t))`, and `P_apo = P_total*(1-f_holo)`, with binding and dimer assembly represented as appropriate. This is a proposed model, not a fitted result. `f_holo` is cofactor occupancy and `f_red(t)` is the proportion of holo subunits reduced at measurement. Neither is automatically the active fraction; heterodimers and different state affinities can matter. If and only if apo protein is inert, holo protein is uniformly active, oligomerization/depletion complications are absent, and total-dose Kapp follows a simple one-component model, `K_active = f_holo*K_app`. Applied conditionally to 22 nM and the reported 0.5–0.7 range, this yields 11–15.4 nM. It is an illustrative active-dose range, not a corrected paper Kd. Do not multiply the resulting evidence a second time by 0.5–0.7.

For quantitative isolation at response level, define `d_iso` using appropriate same-protein/same-DNA controls or a fitted mixture model. A difference-in-differences between wild-type/type-1 and C92A/type-2 curves cannot be interpreted as pure cofactor specificity without a model for the changed DNA and doses. Source input sufficient to estimate that model is missing. The source's cluster-oxidation attribution is an evidence-supported interpretation; directly measured redox fractions in the exact binding mixtures would reduce its remaining uncertainty.

### Measurement validity, signal, uncertainty and explicit arithmetic

The preserved affinity values and censoring are numerical sheet entries. An apparent Kd from a heterogeneous preparation is not automatically a thermodynamic state-specific Kd; a low signal or nonsaturating curve is not zero binding. PMS may change the anisotropy baseline or fluorophore brightness. A binding fraction calculated from anisotropy requires free/bound endpoints and, when brightness differs, the relevant brightness correction. The Figure 4c ordinate is anisotropy, not an already calibrated fraction occupied.

A proposed log-effect cell is `d_corr = d - b_cmp - b_iso`, with `b_iso` the estimated bias from additional contributors (e.g. unequal active dose) that are distinct from nuisance assay-condition differences. Full variance is `v = Var(d-b_cmp-b_iso)`, including cross-covariances; under independence it becomes the explicit sum `v_d+v_cmp+v_iso`. Report bounds when d is censored. The log-ratio bound >3.8167 cannot be promoted to a z score because uncertainty and the fitted censoring likelihood are unavailable.

A proposed explicit compilation into evidence is `ell_b = log p(y_b | H1, x_b, z_b) - log p(y_b | H0, x_b, z_b)`, where x holds measured comparable conditions, z holds mixed-state/contributor measurements, and y is the actual binding readout. Independent experimental blocks add, `L_binding = sum_b ell_b`; equivalent likelihood ratios multiply. Shared preparations, dose calibration and blank/endpoints remain joint operands within a block, not duplicated evidence. If only a censored fitted log K is available, the appropriate likelihood contribution is a tail probability under each model, `log Pr(log K > log 1000 | H1) - log Pr(log K > log 1000 | H0)`, conditional on the validity of the reported censoring mechanism and parameter model. Use raw curves when available, since the plotted dose range and unknown asymptote can make a parameter-only bound model-sensitive. No raw noise model or model predictions are supplied here, so no numeric likelihood score is invented.

The ratios, original values and uncertainty are not additional independent contributions alongside that likelihood: they are visible numerical components from which it is calculated. An extra fixed 'triplicate' multiplier would count precision twice if replicate variance already entered the likelihood. Three assays do not establish three independently purified preparations.

### Consistency and additive correction to earlier interpretation

The 2025 paper explicitly challenges the earlier conclusion that oxidizing IscR leaves type-1 binding unchanged. It reproduces high binding after oxidized protein is returned to anaerobic conditions and infers auto-reduction. Therefore `paper_03_iscr_3447993_001.md` remains an accurate record of what the earlier source reported, but that report must not be treated as established equal affinity of continuously oxidized and reduced IscR. The newer evidence says the actual state during the earlier assay is uncertain. Do not erase the 2012 result, count the 2025 citation of it as another replication, or average unlike state histories into a single Kd. This paragraph is an additive interpretive qualification based on the new primary paper, not a new reading of the prior packet.

A consistency statistic across genuinely comparable preparation-level contrasts could be `Q = sum_i (d_i-d_bar)^2/v_i` with d_bar computed by generalized least squares when covariance exists. Unknown comparable-state identity and unavailable variances prevent a meaningful Q for the 2012/2025 contrast. Protocol/state effects should be estimated before assigning unexplained heterogeneity. Contradictory well-matched evidence can enter the same likelihood with its sign; it must not simply be discarded through a maximum or erased through a reliability label.

### Target/system relationship and missing operands

This study is about *E. coli* IscR DNA binding under specified redox and buffer conditions. Target relevance needs a demonstrated relationship for the source molecule, partner motif, cofactor machinery and state during the proposed function. The relationship may involve identity, measured molecular features, structural/evolutionary correspondence or functional substitution; it is not a sequence-only weight. No such relationship to supplied ybhP material is established. A target prediction would require a function relating these measured inputs to target binding parameters and associated residual uncertainty; without it the target-contribution calculation is unfinished, not assigned a generic 0.5.

Essential numerical gaps: final DNA concentration; active and redox-state fractions during each binding readout; fit form/asymptote and parameter covariance; replicate preparation identities; same-condition photophysics and cluster-loss controls; a measured cross-buffer response model; relationship model to a supplied target; and calibrated competing predictions for score compilation. These are specific unfilled operands, not reasons to replace the user's requested components with an overall categorical grade.
