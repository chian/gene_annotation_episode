## Actual subjects, variables and measurements

P. aeruginosa PAO1 GcsR (PA2449) was expressed as N-terminal His6-GcsR from
pBRL417 in E. coli BL21(DE3), induced at 0.1 mM IPTG for 12 hours at 16°C,
purified by Ni-NTA and quantified by Bradford assay. Purity was monitored
visually by SDS-PAGE; numerical purity, active fraction and tag-cleavage control
are not reported in the inspected methods. There is one recombinant construct
in each binding tube, not a native/mutant protein mixture. Other co-purifying
proteins have not been quantitatively excluded.

Fig. 5 compares a 200-bp gcvH2 promoter probe with a 200-bp PA5530 promoter
probe. DNA identity is the intended variable. Both are 2 nM Cy5-labelled DNA
with 200 nM His6-GcsR, incubated for 30 minutes at 30°C in 25 mM Tris-acetate
pH 8.0, 8 mM magnesium acetate, 10 mM KCl and 1 mM DTT. Protein-free lanes for
each probe define their unshifted migration. Different sequence can still affect
DNA conformation/label brightness; matching length alone does not remove those
measurement differences.

The source reports complete shift of gcvH2 and no shift of PA5530 at 200 nM.
A dilution series uses 0, 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, 100 and 200 nM protein, with a
detectable shifted band reported at 6.25 nM. No fitted Kd, bound-fraction table,
detection threshold or binding replicate uncertainty is supplied. Thus the
source's description of high affinity cannot become `Kd=6.25 nM` or an exact
occupancy measurement. A concentration at first detectable shift is a detection
observation under this gel assay.

Competition holds 200 nM protein and 2 nM labelled gcvH2 DNA, adding 0/25/50/100
nM unlabelled gcvH2 or 100 nM unlabelled PA5530. Cognate competitor depletes the
shift, whereas the nonspecific competitor does not. Derived competitor/labelled
DNA ratios are 12.5, 25 and 50. These describe the challenge applied, not
selectivity factors or binding affinities. The nominal protein/DNA input ratio
is 100 at 200 nM protein and 3.125 at 6.25 nM; it is not a measured stoichiometry.

Three shifted species appear with the promoter. Mutation of one proposed site
changes three shifts to two; isolated sites 2 and 3 bind separately and their
mutated probes do not shift at the tested dose. These support sequence-dependent
complex formation at three regions. A count of three bands does not by itself
measure a hexamer or prove simultaneous occupation by three dimers. Fig. 6C's
caption lists lane 3 in both absent/present groups; quantitative lane extraction
would need the actual labelled figure to resolve that inconsistency. No values
are assigned to its lanes in this review.

The source also reports absence of shift for the mexE promoter at 200 nM and an
unshown hcnA upstream assay. Neither is a universal absence-of-binding claim;
assay concentration, sensitivity and preparation remain part of each result.

## Numerical components and combination

**Signal and specificity.** A dataset reader can integrate each lane's free
band F and all shifted bands B1…Bk after local background subtraction. Under
linear equal-brightness detection and recovery,

`f_shift = Σ_j B_j/(F+Σ_j B_j)`.

If complex and free dye brightness differ, a measured calibration q_j is needed:
`f_shift = Σ_j(B_j/q_j)/(F+Σ_j(B_j/q_j))`. Saturation of the imager, unobserved
well-retained material and differential loss require their own measured
corrections. The visible publication image is not treated as a calibrated raw
gel dataset, so this packet does not invent fractional values from visual words.

For probe-specific signal use the difference of differences

`d = [f_gcv,+P−f_gcv,0P] − [f_PA5530,+P−f_PA5530,0P]`.

For the competition comparison, use at the same 100 nM competitor concentration
`d_comp=f_nonspecific_comp−f_specific_comp`, optionally corrected by matched
protein-free competitor controls if they alter migration. A missing positive
numerical signal threshold cannot be supplied by translating "complete" to 1
and "no shift" to 0. Nondetection is represented by an assay-derived bound when
the noise/detection calibration is available.

**Comparability excluding intended variable.** Same protein lot, temperature,
time, length and label concentration can cancel shared nuisance effects in a
paired model. DNA-specific migration and fluorescence require probe-specific
blank/brightness operands. A measured response correction `b_cmp` predicts any
remaining difference caused by nuisance conditions. Raw ratios of incubation
time, salt, pH or probe length are not meaningful comparability multipliers.
Changing DNA sequence is deliberately tested and must not be penalized as a
nuisance difference.

**Isolation of effect.** Purified-protein addition, cognate competition,
nonspecific DNA and site mutation address alternative explanations for the
shift. They do not numerically identify the active GcsR fraction or eliminate
every possible co-purifying DNA-binding protein. A tag-only control, additional
purification with activity tracking, immunosupershift or orthogonal binding
measurement could quantify specific remaining alternatives. Assign observed
background once through a subtraction or response model, not a second generic
"specificity" penalty after subtracting it.

**Validity and uncertainty.** EMSA is an endpoint after electrophoretic
separation; a measured shifted fraction need not equal equilibrium solution
occupancy if complexes dissociate during the run. Active concentration,
multisite binding, ligand depletion and survival during electrophoresis belong
in the response model. A first visible band cannot identify these. The inspected
binding sections do not report independent-preparation count, raw replicate
intensities or error estimates. Three shifted bands, seven protein doses and
several probes do not constitute independent preparation replication.

**Explicit compilation proposal.** For a specified DNA-binding/selectivity
claim, let a=(1,−1,−1,1) for the four fractions in d and let Σ be their measured
covariance. The primary uncertainty is `V_measurement=a'Σa`. Use

`d_target=d−b_cmp−b_other+b_correspondence`,

`V_total=V_measurement+V_cmp+V_other+V_correspondence+2Σcovariance`,

`E=d_target/sqrt(V_total)` when the fraction contrast is identifiable and its
approximate uncertainty model is appropriate. b_other isolates unwanted signal
contributors separately from b_cmp. These are proposed sums and a normalized
evidence component, not a final calibrated annotation probability. Unknown
operands stay unknown. With fully recorded lanes, a joint response likelihood
can combine probe, competition and mutation controls through their common
preparation/calibration parameters rather than assuming independence.

Across genuinely independent preparations, combine their joint likelihood
ratios by multiplication, or sum their log ratios under a stated common binding
model. Do not multiply the original titration likelihood by a Kd, dose-ratio
or specificity summary derived from the same lanes. Complementary controls
identify parameters of one experiment; their number is not a quality score.

**Target/system relevance.** The assayed sequence is PAO1 GcsR and the tested
partner is promoter DNA. A quantitative mapping to another supplied target
requires a relevant sequence/site/structure/interchangeability or other measured
relationship. Broad TyrR-family membership cannot alone infer the same DNA or
effector specificity. This assay does not measure GcsR–glycine binding and does
not by itself show what DNA binding does to transcription. No target relationship
or organism-independent relevance grade is invented.
