## Experimental definitions and source values

The source protein is MalR, Saci_1161 of *Sulfolobus acidocaldarius*. Two separately prepared sources of the protein are examined. These preparations do not coexist in the same binding mixture.

1. **MalR_Saci:** PCR-derived malR in pSVA1470 is expressed in *S. acidocaldarius* MW001 with a C-terminal Strep tag and 10×His tag. The host contains its native malR; the purified preparation enriches the tagged product, but the main text does not quantify native untagged MalR carryover or possible mixed oligomers. Maltose/dextrin induces expression in the host. Purification uses ammonium sulfate and Ni affinity, followed by dialysis into 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 100 mM NaCl.
2. **MalR_Ecoli:** codon-optimized malR in pSVA937 under a T7 promoter is expressed in *E. coli* Rosetta DE3 pLysS with a C-terminal His6 tag. Cultures contain 0.5% glucose, induction at OD600=1 with 0.5 mM IPTG, 2.5 h expression. Initial purification attempts suffered degradation; DTT and EDTA stabilized the protein. Final buffer is 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 120 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 1 mM DTT, 2 mM EDTA. This comparison changes host, tag, expression conditions and final buffer as well as possible sugar carryover. Codon optimization itself is not evidence of amino-acid change; exact construct sequence equivalence is not verified in this inspection.

**EMSA:** 15 µl reactions contain 30 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 75 mM NaCl, 1×BSA, 10% glycerol, labelled malE promoter DNA stated as 5 nM (8 ng), and 100 ng herring DNA. The binding fragment is described as 250 bp. Incubate 60 °C for 30 min; cool on ice, then run 6% native PAGE at room temperature, 15 V/cm, in 45 mM Tris-borate. Detect 32P-labelled DNA with Storm 840 phosphorimager. The measured endpoint is the distribution of labelled DNA between unshifted and retarded material after cooling and electrophoresis. It is not a direct maltose-binding assay, a transport measurement or a transcription rate.

Figure 4A–D displays MalR_Saci at 0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.4 and 2.0 µM, with no added sugar or 10 mM maltose, xylose or glucose. The source describes complete shifting at high protein and similar binding with and without those sugars. No fitted Kd, IC50, fraction-bound table, binding replicate count or uncertainty is supplied. Triplicate/median statements for reporter experiments do not supply EMSA replication.

Figure 4E contains increasing specific unlabelled promoter competitor with labelled:competitor ratios 1:0, 1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, 1:20; nonspecific competitor lanes are 1:10 and 1:20. Specific promoter competes for the shifted labelled material more effectively than the nonspecific competitor. The displayed panel/caption does not identify a protein concentration for this competition experiment. The ratio is labelled DNA:DNA; the molar versus mass convention for nonspecific DNA of unspecified length remains unresolved. For the same promoter fragment mass and molecule ratios coincide, but this does not license a molecule count for heterogeneous herring DNA.

**Explicit denominator discrepancy:** the Methods say both 5 nM DNA and 8 ng in 15 µl, while Results describe a 250-bp fragment. Using the approximate dsDNA conversion 660 g/mol/bp, `5e-9 mol/L × 15e-6 L × 250 bp × 660 g/mol/bp = 12.375 ng`, whereas 8 ng implies approximately 3.2323 nM. The source's '10-fold' nonspecific competitor language also differs from the stated mass ratio `100/8 = 12.5`. These are derived consistency checks using an approximate molecular-mass convention, not corrected source doses. Preserve all reported values and resolve fragment length/loading convention or original records before calculating precise depletion or an affinity. With the nominal 5 nM denominator, the assayed protein:DNA ratio spans 10 to 400; it describes excess total protein and is not a stoichiometry or active-concentration measurement.

## Conversion into the user's numerical components

### Experimental comparability excluding the intended variable

For MalR_Saci ±10 mM sugar, sugar identity/presence is the intended variable. The protein preparation, promoter, stated buffer, incubation and readout are shared. Do not penalize the presence of sugar merely because the experiment intends to test sugar dependence. Instead quantify unintended changes in ionic strength, temperature, effective protein concentration, solvent composition or recovery if those were measured and could change the endpoint.

Host-source comparison is different. It addresses the possibility that maltose carried over from archaeal expression explains apparent sugar independence. Different tags, stabilization buffers, host modifications or copurified proteins are nuisance changes if the intended comparison is residual maltose. They cannot be collapsed to 'same amino-acid identity therefore fully comparable'. A nuisance-response component is `b_cmp = sum_k beta_k Delta_x_k` in the units of the chosen binding contrast, excluding the deliberately tested factor. beta values must come from measured response sensitivities or a validated response model. No such buffer/tag response measurements are available here. Raw pH, time or salt ratios do not measure comparability. In particular external growth-medium pH 3 is not the protein's measured intracellular pH and cannot be compared numerically with assay pH 8 as an activity-relevance grade.

### Isolation of the effect

The protein-dependent shift plus specific competitor addresses sequence-selective DNA association. Herring competitor constrains some nonspecific association; it does not prove the specific promoter is the only binding substrate. The E. coli preparation is a complementary attempt to address carried-over maltose, but its different preparation and unmeasured residual sugars mean no numeric 'zero maltose' correction follows. Direct assay of residual maltose or binding of maltose to purified MalR would address those distinct questions. Protein purity, active fraction, untagged host-protein carryover and oligomeric composition are not quantified.

The biological inference must be kept precise: sugar-insensitive malE gel shifting is evidence about this DNA-binding readout. It does not establish absence of maltose binding, absence of sugar-dependent conformational changes, or whether bound MalR activates transcription. The paper's cellular deletion/restoration, gene-expression and promoter-mutagenesis consequences belong to A106 and are not extra A105 binding measurements. The PFK activity used to demonstrate expression-vector improvement is not MalR's catalytic activity.

### Numeric response and specificity cells

Given raw calibrated phosphorimager counts, define background-corrected counts `U` in unshifted probe and `B_j` in each retained shifted region, with matched integration areas and local backgrounds. If label recovery and counting response are comparable across bands, `f_shift = sum_j B_j / (U + sum_j B_j)`; include smear/well material according to a predefined recovery rule. `recovery = (U+sum B)/input_count` detects losses. Cooling and electrophoresis can change complexes: f_shift is the retained-shift fraction and can be converted to equilibrium occupancy only with an appropriate stability/equilibration model. Do not fit a thermodynamic Kd from a visual full-shift threshold.

At a common protein and competitor dose, a specific-competition contrast is `s = f_shift,nonspecific - f_shift,specific`. Alternatively, for several competitor doses fit the dose-response curves and report each assay-dependent IC50 and their log ratio with covariance. Unspecified protein dose and nonspecific DNA concentration units prevent a quantitative competitor-affinity inference here. Do not use the number of bands as a count of binding sites without stoichiometric identification.

For the sugar effect at each protein dose, define `d(P) = f_shift,+sugar(P) - f_shift,no-sugar(P)`. Estimate d across independent preparations, with shared gel/calibration covariance. Similar-looking gels only imply no obvious difference at this resolution. A numerical equivalence component requires an explicit biologically useful tolerance epsilon and a confidence interval or standard error on d. One proposed continuous cell is `M_equiv = (epsilon - abs(d_corr))/SE(d_corr)` where `d_corr = d-b_cmp-b_iso`; b_iso represents separately measured remaining contributor/carryover bias. Positive M means the estimate is that many SE inside the chosen tolerance boundary; it is not a confidence probability or an adopted universal score.

**Synthetic example only:** let a matched-dose effect be 0.02 fraction units, SE=0.03 and tolerance epsilon=0.10. Then `M_equiv=(0.10-0.02)/0.03=2.6667`. Reducing epsilon to 0.01 makes the same result fail that narrower equivalence claim. This illustrates why a zero-looking effect without precision cannot earn maximum evidence for independence. None of these values is extracted from the gels.

### Explicit combination, consistency and target relation

Use measured calibration to convert counts to response once. Apply nuisance-condition and unwanted-contributor corrections once: `d_corr = d-b_cmp-b_iso`. Full `v_corr = Var(d-b_cmp-b_iso)` includes covariance; under independence it is the sum of the three variances. A hypothesis-specific Gaussian likelihood contribution can then be computed as `ell = ((d_corr-delta_0)^2-(d_corr-delta_1)^2)/(2*v_corr)` for explicitly specified predictions delta_1 and delta_0. It is a proposed score in log-likelihood-ratio units, not a paper's value. With independent preparation blocks, `L_binding=sum ell_block`; equivalent evidence ratios multiply. Do not additionally multiply this by signal/noise or replicate-count grades already represented by v_corr. For an interval equivalence hypothesis, integrate or otherwise specify predictions over the interval; a point prediction of zero is not interchangeable with all effects below epsilon.

Actual error, calibration, independent-preparation and alternative-prediction inputs are missing, so the paper cannot fill a numerical equivalence or log-likelihood component on its own. The native observation, dose series and competition ratios remain useful numeric entries without pretending they supply a full score. Contrasts from the same gel or shared no-sugar curve must be modeled jointly; three sugars against one baseline do not yield three independent controls. Across the two hosts, estimate nuisance/preparation effects before calling agreement independent replication or disagreement biological inconsistency.

The relationship to a supplied target requires measured identity/correspondence of the protein, promoter and regulatory context, not only a family label. Structural features, evolutionary evidence, molecular identity or functional substitution may inform it. This study itself cautions against converting a conserved putative sugar-binding motif into a measured sugar response. There is no established correspondence to ybhP here. Missing target correspondence and its quantitative transformation remain explicit unfilled operands; no generic transfer multiplier is assigned.
