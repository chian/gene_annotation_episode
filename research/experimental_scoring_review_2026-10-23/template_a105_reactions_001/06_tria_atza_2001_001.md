## Exact experiment and native numerical comparisons

TriA is from the organism called Pseudomonas sp. strain NRRL B-12227 in this paper. The cloned triA construct pJS3 in pUC18 was expressed in E. coli DH5α. AtzA is from Pseudomonas sp. ADP; its expression clone is DH5α(pMD4). TriA and AtzA are assayed separately, not coexpressed or mixed as two competing enzymes. The intended variables are substrate identity within each enzyme's panel and protein identity for the reciprocal comparison. They differ by nine residues of 475: the calculated global identity is 466/475 = 0.9810526. This sequence fraction is not a measured fraction of shared chemical function.

TriA was partially purified by 0–20% ammonium sulfate precipitation; AtzA was highly purified by an earlier protocol. Both preparations underwent iron-sulfate dialysis followed by metal-free MOPS pH 7. Specific activities use different denominators: total protein in the partial TriA fraction versus highly purified AtzA protein. This prevents interpreting raw cross-protein specific-activity ratios as intrinsic catalytic ratios without active-enzyme abundance and contaminant contributions.

Table 1 reports TriA activities in nmol/min/mg total protein: melamine 245 ± 2; CAAT 135 ± 1; ammeline 17 ± 1; aminoatrazine 17 ± 1. Means are from duplicate samples, n = 2. The table does not explicitly define its ± values; Figure 4 separately says SEM, which is not automatically the table's definition. Initial rates were measured for positive substrates. The negative panel was examined by HPLC after 16 and 24 hours using 8 µg total protein/ml. Reported non-detection corresponds to rates below 10 pmol/min/mg = 0.01 nmol/min/mg; atrazine, ammelide and several other substrates are explicitly below that limit. No infinite selectivity or exact zero is warranted.

For melamine versus ammeline, 245/17 = 14.4118 is the reported specific-activity ratio in this preparation. The separate crude-extract time-course experiment used 100 µM substrate at 22°C, with 12.4 µg/ml total protein for melamine but 124 µg/ml for ammeline, selected to give 8–10% conversion over 30 minutes. That tenfold enzyme amount is an additional difference; absolute product-time curves cannot be compared as if enzyme concentrations were equal. These are reaction-rate changes; they are not independently measured binding Kd values.

## Conversion into numerical sheet components

**Specificity with a censored alternative.** For an exactly declared two-substrate preference under the stated assay conditions, use `D = v_melamine/(v_melamine + v_atrazine)`, where each v is a nonnegative blank-subtracted rate measured per the same active-enzyme denominator. Conditional on comparable substrate exposure and the published total-protein normalization, the observed alternative bound gives `D > 245/(245 + 0.01) = 0.9999592`, and a rate ratio `v_melamine/v_atrazine > 24,500`. This is a bounded within-panel preference component, not 99.9959% annotation confidence. A full panel uses the sum of its positive and bounded alternative rates in the denominator; one must not choose the easiest negative competitor retrospectively and call it universal specificity. The capability claim “TriA deaminates melamine” does not require multiplying by D, because other real substrates do not invalidate that capability.

This censored comparison concerns the observed long-assay screening bound. Stability over the 24-hour interval, initial substrate concentrations across the panel, product recovery, and linearity are needed before treating its limit as an initial-rate limit strictly commensurate with 245. Positive-rate uncertainty and uncertainty in the detection bound also affect the lower bound. The simple numerical result is therefore a conditional calculation, not a completed experimental-relevance score.

**Protein normalization and distinct isolation.** Let `f_TriA = mass(TriA)/mass(total fraction)` and `v_contam` be matched contaminant/background activity per mg total fraction. Intrinsic activity is `(v_total − v_contam)/f_TriA`, subject to the active fraction of TriA. A measured mass fraction can correct the denominator; it cannot replace `I = 1 − v_contam/v_total`, the separate measured fraction of activity attributable to TriA. For example, a synthetic 20% TriA mass fraction would convert 245 to 1,225 nmol/min/mg TriA only if all activity were attributable to TriA. If 20% of observed activity were instead from a contaminant, the corrected number would be 980 and I = 0.8. These are invented illustrations, not reported purification measurements. A matched empty-vector preparation constrains host background; purification-series coelution, proteomics and contaminant-specific rates address residual contribution more directly.

**Comparability outside the intended variable.** Substrate identity is intentionally changed and is not itself a comparability penalty. AtzA/TriA identity is intentionally changed in that comparison, but purification, active-protein abundance, substrate exposure, metal loading, and assay window are nuisance differences. If proportionality of initial rate to enzyme amount is demonstrated, divide by active enzyme concentration before comparison; do not also multiply by 12.4/124 as a second penalty for a difference already normalized. A residual nuisance response g gives proposed `C_g = min[g(x_a),g(x_b)]/max[g(x_a),g(x_b)]`; multiply independent nonredundant C_g terms or use a joint measured response. The required calibration curves and active-protein fractions are not available in the inspected passages.

**Precision and consistency.** Retain n = 2 duplicate activity samples and n = 3 competition samples with their actual meanings. Do not label them independent protein preparations. For a positive net rate with documented variance, proposed `R_prec = 1/[1 + Var(v_net)/v_net²]`. Were ±2 the relevant SD, a conditional value would be `1/[1 + (2/245)²] = 0.9999334`; the source's unspecified table uncertainty prevents filling that as an actual score. Replicate estimates and blank covariance, plus between-preparation variance, would supply the proper variance. This multiplier reports relative precision, not absolute confidence; low assay noise cannot compensate for unresolved contamination or an inappropriate target relationship.

**Complementary product identification.** Agreement of HPLC retention and mass with standards addresses identity; it is not two independent activity experiments. For a calibrated simultaneous reaction, proposed productive fraction is `q = mol identified intended first product/mol substrate consumed`, corrected for the declared stoichiometry. A transient intermediate requires time-resolved recovery and loss modeling. Endpoint ammelide formed after spontaneous CAAT chemistry cannot be entered as a complete one-step enzyme product fraction. Native retention differences, mass errors, recovery and intermediate decay rates are missing here, so no numerical identity/coupling multiplier is fabricated.

**Relation to the target system.** The native sequence identity 0.9810526 can populate an original alignment measurement cell. This actual counterexample shows it cannot be used as a 0.9810526 probability of transferable specificity. A separate measurable relationship would examine correspondence at experimentally resolved specificity determinants, structural site correspondence or directly measured functional interchangeability; those measurements are not established for an unknown supplied target here. No 0.25/0.5/0.75 fallback is substituted, and the relation is not restricted to sequence identity.

## Explicit combination example and remaining inputs

For the narrowly defined positive preparation activity, a candidate combination is `E_activity = R_cal × R_prec × C × I × q`, retaining the actual rate and uncertainty beside it. `R_cal = min(recovery,1/recovery)` comes from a held-out known standard after quantitative calibration; C concerns nuisance comparability; I concerns attributable activity; q concerns direct productive chemistry when a proxy or downstream product is used. These nonredundant components require distinct measurements. For the specific preference claim, `E_preference = E_activity × D`. Correction of a native rate for a known loss must not be penalized again by that same loss.

Synthetic arithmetic: assuming D equals its displayed bound, recovery 0.98, measured net-rate CV 0.10, C = 1, I = 0.8 and independently established direct-product q = 1 gives `E_preference > [245/(245 + 0.01)] × 0.98 × [1/1.01] × 1 × 0.8 × 1 = 0.7762059`. Only the rate and screening bound originate in the paper, and even their joint use remains conditional; the other values are invented to show compilation. This is not the paper's score or an annotation credit. Missing calibration, impurity activity, active fraction, common-condition response, uncertainty definitions, intermediate recovery and target correspondence prevent a completed actual score.
