## Experimental definitions and native numbers

The selected subject is the reaction catalyzed by purified CofC together with CofD. Mycetohabitans sp. B3 CofC (KQH55_09515) was produced with an N-terminal His tag in E. coli; the general production method permits BL21(DE3) and LOBSTR-BL21, without assigning this particular preparation explicitly in the passages inspected. Ni-NTA purification is described. The initial comparison used the same downstream Methanocaldococcus jannaschii CofD with different upstream CofC proteins. The two proteins coexist in each reaction: they are successive catalysts, not separate competing genotypes.

In the direct competition experiment, each substrate—3-phosphoglycerate (3PG), 2-phospholactate (2PL), and phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)—was supplied at 0.5 mM. For Mycetohabitans CofC with M. jannaschii CofD, the reported product-rate proportions were 65%, 26.5%, and 8.5%, respectively. Their denominators are the sum of the three observed final-product rates in that reaction, not the total substrate supplied or a probability that an annotation is correct. The resulting descriptive ratios are 65/26.5 = 2.45283 and 65/8.5 = 7.64706. These are pair-specific product ratios.

The assay method specifies 100 mM HEPES pH 7.4, 2 mM GTP, 2 mM MgCl2, 34 µM CofD, 26 µM CofC, and a 50 µL reaction. The retrieved XML renders the Fo amount as “0.14 nm Fo”; that unit is unresolved here and must not be silently converted. Samples were quenched with acetonitrile/formic acid. Extracted-ion chromatographic peak areas were used to estimate slopes over 0–20 minutes and then normalized. Three biological replicates and SD error bars are described; the inspected material does not resolve whether these were three independent protein preparations, three cultures, or three separately assembled reactions. Standard deviations of these individual percentages were not transcribed from figures.

Changing the downstream partner to the cognate CofD/FbiA changes the final substrate preference: the Mycetohabitans pair becomes nearly exclusive for 3PG, whereas the M. smegmatis pair largely loses the apparent 2PL product. The inspected prose does not give exact cognate percentages. “Nearly exclusive” is not entered as 1.0. CofC generates labile intermediates GPPG, LPPG, and EPPG; downstream selection, intermediate stability, and product inhibition complicate direct measurement of upstream specificity.

## Comparability and effect isolation are different calculations

For the partner-swap comparison, downstream protein identity is the intended changed variable and is excluded from a nuisance-condition comparability penalty. Relevant other differences include active enzyme concentrations, substrate exposure, assay duration, temperature, calibration, and preparation-specific contamination. Equal nominal amounts do not establish equal active fractions or equal downstream capacity. The observed pair preference must not be discounted merely because the intended partner changed.

Isolation is a separate question: a final-product measurement cannot identify upstream activation selectivity without knowing downstream conversion. Let `a_j` be CofC's activation rate for substrate j and `q_j` the fraction of that activated intermediate converted to the measured final product in the sampled interval. With a justified steady-flux approximation, `v_final,j = a_j × q_j`. Then `a_j = v_final,j / q_j`; an upstream selectivity share is `(v_final,j / q_j) / Σ_k(v_final,k / q_k)`. Intermediate loss, accumulation, and time dependence require an explicit kinetic model instead of this simplification. Unknown q values leave the upstream calculation unknown.

This is not a reason to mark the pair measurement unreliable. It identifies the scope the actual measurement supports and the additional operands required for a narrower single-protein claim. An independent direct intermediate measurement or calibrated single-turnover/rapid-quench experiment could supply upstream flux and loss; excess partner concentration alone does not prove q = 1.

## Proposed numerical cells and explicit combination

1. **Calibrated product share, for a pair-preference claim.** Let `m_j` be the blank-subtracted chromatographic-area slope for final product j, and `r_j` its measured area-per-mole response factor in the same matrix. Then `v_j = m_j/r_j` and `D_pair,j = v_j/Σ_k v_k`. This sums molecular rates before normalizing. The paper's reported area-derived share for 3PG is 0.65. If product-specific response factors were not established, 0.65 remains the reported signal share; it is not silently promoted to a molar fraction. Absolute activity and the three native rates remain separate cells. This preference cell applies to “preferentially makes product j among these alternatives,” not to the broader capability claim that the pair can make j.

2. **Rate uncertainty, including the denominator.** With calibrated replicate rates, propagate the full covariance matrix through `D = v_j/Σv`. Shared normalization makes the fractions correlated. For an individual positive net rate, a candidate precision multiplier is `R_prec = 1/[1 + Var(v_net)/v_net²]`, with `Var(v_net) = Var(v_sample) + Var(v_blank) − 2Cov(sample,blank)`. The missing SD values and preparation identities prevent filling it from the inspected text. Three replicates do not automatically mean three independent biological preparations. This multiplier is a proposed relative-uncertainty conversion, not a confidence probability.

3. **Calibration accuracy.** After applying product-specific response factors, use held-out spiked standards to measure recovery `r = measured/known` and propose `R_cal = min(r,1/r)` for positive recovery. Raw response factors are conversion coefficients, not calibration success scores. Joint standards and matrix-specific recoveries can expose ion-suppression differences that alter product proportions. Missing recovery stays unknown.

4. **Nuisance-condition comparability.** For a measured nuisance-response function g and conditions x_a,x_b, use `C_g = min[g(x_a),g(x_b)]/max[g(x_a),g(x_b)]`, excluding the intended partner identity. Multiply only separable nonredundant effects; otherwise fit a joint response. Nominally matched stated conditions support a defined comparison but are not complete quantitative proof that C = 1. No response curves or active-fraction estimates were extracted here.

5. **Pair-to-single-enzyme isolation.** Keep each q_j as a measured flux/recovery component, with uncertainty. Do not replace q_j by a category grade for “coupled assay.” The pair's rate and upstream rate answer different questions. A q correction applied directly to the rates must not also multiply the score as a second penalty for the same loss.

6. **Relevance of a substitute partner to a native pair, if that transfer is being claimed.** Under matched, calibrated conditions, a proposed compositional agreement number is `P = Σ_j min(f_substitute,j,f_native,j) = 1 − 0.5Σ_j|f_substitute,j − f_native,j|`. This measures overlap of product distributions, not mechanism or overall system identity. It belongs to the relationship between the assayed substitute pair and the target pair. It is not a nuisance penalty inside the intentional partner-swap experiment. Native/cognate fractions are missing, so P cannot be filled numerically. No target sequence or target partner has been invented.

For a measured pair-preference claim, an explicit candidate combination is `E_pair_preference = D_pair × R_cal × R_prec × C × I_preparation`, where `I_preparation = 1 − v_contaminant/v_net` requires matched contaminant measurements. The pair capability claim omits D_pair; real alternative activities do not reduce evidence that a capability exists. If transfer to a specified native pair is claimed, P is a possible additional multiplier only when its distinct relationship and measurements are established. It does not absorb the missing target correspondence. These products are scoring proposals, not adopted weights or a final annotation-credit formula.

**Synthetic calculation, not paper data:** suppose properly calibrated D_pair = 0.65, held-out recovery 0.98, net-rate CV = 0.10, matched C = 1, and measured preparation attribution I = 0.95. Then `R_prec = 1/1.01 = 0.990099`, `R_cal × R_prec × I = 0.921782`, and `E_pair_preference = 0.65 × 0.921782 = 0.599158`. The 0.65 is reused from the reported proportion only as an illustration assuming calibration; all other inputs are invented. No actual complete score is calculable from the inspected source.

**Synthetic isolation example:** starting with final rates proportional to 65,26.5,8.5, suppose measured q values were 1,0.5,0.1. The inferred upstream activities would be proportional to 65,53,85; their shares would be 0.320197,0.261084,0.418719. The apparent final-product ranking would differ from the upstream ranking. These q values are invented to demonstrate why the missing operands matter, not to estimate CofC biology.

## Weaknesses addressed by complementary measurements

Product-specific quantitative standards address unequal LC-MS responses. A time-resolved direct intermediate assay addresses upstream versus downstream selection. Matched active-enzyme measurements and a partner-concentration series address capacity differences. Independent protein preparations address preparation consistency. Cognate pair measurements address substituting a different organism's downstream partner. These controls have different functions; several traces from one reaction are not independent confirmations, and two displays of the same LC-MS data are not two experiments.

Remaining inputs include product-specific response and held-out recovery data; actual rate estimates and covariance; exact Fo units; independent-preparation identities; downstream conversion fractions; active enzyme fractions; and the specific target relationship. The paper supplies a useful native competitive preference measurement and a strong warning about assigning a two-enzyme output to the first enzyme. It does not supply every scoring operand.
