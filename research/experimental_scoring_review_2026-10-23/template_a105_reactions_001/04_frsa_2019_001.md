# FrsA: strength of a measured model-substrate result versus native-substrate inference

Source: [Wang et al., Purification and biochemical characterization of FrsA protein from Vibrio vulnificus as an esterase](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6450606/), Methods substrate specificity and kinetics; Results purification, substrate specificity, steady-state kinetics; Discussion; Figures 2 and 4 captions. ## Definitions and native measurements

A synthetic, E. coli-codon-adapted V. vulnificus frsA construct with a C-terminal six-histidine tag was expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3), purified by Ni-NTA and compared with an otherwise matching no-FrsA reaction. There was no intended wild-type/mutant mixture. Residual host esterases were not quantitatively assayed. The substrate panel deliberately varied acyl length at 0.1 mM using 20 µM protein, phosphate pH 7.5, isopropanol/Triton and 37 °C. The separate concentration series used 9.5 µM protein, 0.05–6 mM p-nitrophenyl acetate, pH 7.5 and 25 °C. Thus acyl length is the intended variable within the screen; temperature, dose and matrix differences matter when comparing the screen with the kinetic series.

Net chromogenic product formation gave fitted Km=18.6±1.3 mM, Vmax=6.4±0.4 µM/s, kcat=0.67 s−1 and kcat/Km=36 M−1 s−1. Reactions were triplicate; preparation independence and the ± definition were not established. At the highest measured concentration, Smax/Km=6/18.6=0.32258 and the fitted model predicts v/Vmax=6/(18.6+6)=0.243902. This is model-predicted range coverage, not a measured saturation fraction or an automatic 0.244 reliability grade. The substrate panel reported roughly half the C2 activity for C4 and less than 5% for C8/C12; the C16 nondetection has no numerical detection bound. SDS purity above95% is a protein-mass estimate, not95% catalytic attribution. Background hydrolysis was sufficiently fast at pH9 that kinetics were performed at pH7.5.

## Conversion into the user's numerical cells

**Signal and precision.** Calculate v_net=(dA_sample/dt−dA_blank/dt)/(epsilon l), using epsilon=15,000 M−1cm−1 as the paper's reporting coefficient, path length l and slopes from the same time window. The score-relevant uncertainty combines both slopes, covariance, coefficient/calibration uncertainty and protein concentration. Raw v_net distinguishes signal magnitude from confidence. The proposed R_prec=1/[1+Var(v_net)/v_net²] is populated only once those inputs are known. Do not also reward sample/blank ratio as a second independent signal component if it recycles the same noise/blank contrast.

**Kinetic parameter reliability.** The largest tested concentration being below Km is a specific reason to examine covariance and profile likelihood. It does not prove the fit is wrong: the low-substrate slope kcat/Km may be better determined than kcat and Km separately. For e=kcat/Km, proposed log-uncertainty is u_loge²=Var(log kcat)+Var(log Km)−2Cov(log kcat,log Km). The continuous component R_e=1/(1+u_loge²) evaluates precision of the efficiency that supports the chosen inference. This replaces, rather than multiplies by, a second precision factor derived from the same fit. The printed ± cannot populate it without definitions/covariance. For substantial asymmetry, report profile interval bounds and compute the component from a declared profile-derived uncertainty rather than forcing a symmetric normal approximation.

Synthetic arithmetic only: u_loge=0.20 gives R_e=0.961538; adding an independent between-preparation log-variance of0.09 yields R_e=1/(1+.04+.09)=0.884956. This sum includes inconsistency from genuinely separate preparations without falsely counting repeated measurements as independent. Neither uncertainty is measured in this paper.

**Experimental comparability.** Common-condition per-enzyme initial rates can compare substrates after correcting measured effective concentration, solvent, temperature and product response. Substrate identity itself must be excluded from the nuisance-factor product. A solubility/recovery component could be C_available=min(S_free,A/S_nom,A,S_free,B/S_nom,B)/max(...), using measured free-substrate fractions. Equal nominal doses do not establish equal accessible concentrations for a short-chain versus long-chain ester in a detergent mixture. Those fractions and their effect on activity are missing. For cross-protocol comparisons use measured response factors, not a 25/37 temperature ratio. Reusing concentration normalization already in kcat would double count it.

**Isolation and relatedness.** Let q_P be the fraction of optical product signal confirmed as the intended hydrolysis product after spectral/matrix calibration, and I=1−v_host/v_net the activity fraction attributable to FrsA after quantitatively resolved host contributions. A p-nitrophenol calibration can populate q_P; a no-enzyme blank cannot estimate v_host. Orthogonal product quantification and alternative-expression or quantitative contaminant controls address different weaknesses. A native-substrate relevance factor cannot be manufactured from a high Km alone: the native substrate has not been identified. The measured statement remains “hydrolyses the tested model ester under these conditions.” A molecular comparison with an annotation target also remains unprovided.

**Explicit combination.** Proposed contribution for that bounded reaction is E=C_conditions×R_cal×R_e×q_P×I, with each missing operand visible and R_e containing relevant fit/replication uncertainty once. Synthetic example holding comparability and product isolation fixed at unity for demonstration: calibration agreement .98, u_loge=.20 and quantitatively estimated I=.95 give .98×.961538×.95=.895192. This is a hypothetical component product, not a score for FrsA or probability of native function. If the claim changes to a particular physiological substrate, the missing relationship is not repaired by reusing .895192.

The useful lesson is that weak model-substrate activity can be genuinely measured while the native inference stays unresolved; saturation coverage, assay background and parameter covariance are measurable contributors to reliability.
