## Definitions and comparison

Mycobacterium tuberculosis H37Rv Rv2131c/CysQ was expressed with an N-terminal His6 tag in E. coli BL21(DE3), purified by PAP affinity and size exclusion, and identified by protein mass and tryptic peptides. The experiment compared different substrates on the same named protein. No deliberate wild-type/mutant mixture exists. Substrate identity is the intended changed variable. Additional changes were pH, magnesium, assay readout, enzyme concentration and interval: PAP used 5 nM enzyme, pH 8.5, 0.5 mM Mg, 4 minutes and AMP/GMP mass spectrometry; IMP/FBP used 110 nM enzyme, pH 9, 10 mM Mg, 15 minutes and released-phosphate colorimetry. All were at 30 °C. The alternative substrates were tested under their previously reported optima.

PAP product AMP was distinguished by MS with GMP as internal standard. Colorimetric phosphate can include substrate background hydrolysis, addressed by no-enzyme blanks, but product identity/isomer assignment is a different question. PAP-specific purification and the correct protein identity improve the attribution argument; neither provides a numerical contaminating-enzyme activity fraction. Approximately 83% peptide sequence coverage is identity information, not 83% molecular-function confidence or sample purity.

## Native numerical inputs

Table 1 reports PAP kcat/Km=42±8, IMP=0.0027±0.0015 and FBP=0.025±0.001 µM−1 min−1. The ratios are 42/0.0027=15,555.6 and 42/0.025=1680. Their numerators and denominators are catalytic efficiencies measured under different optimized conditions. They are not matched-condition cellular competition ratios. PAP Km=8.1±3.1 µM and kcat=5.4±1.1 s−1. The table says averages of three independent experiments but does not define the displayed ± or establish three independent protein preparations.

For the MS assay, the AMP/GMP normalization factor averaged 1.5±0.1 across samples measured over three weeks. This is a response correction, not 150% recovery; applying min(1.5,1/1.5) as calibration quality would be wrong. The relative displayed variation 0.1/1.5=0.06667 is descriptive until its error definition is known. An independently measured known-AMP recovery after applying that factor is the calibration-accuracy input still needed.

At 25 µM PAP, the 0–6-minute progress curve had R²=0.993 and 37% substrate conversion by the selected 4-minute endpoint. Low-concentration progress curves could not be confirmed because of Tris ion suppression. R² from the high-concentration curve cannot be a 0.993 universal validity score for all PAP concentrations.

## Concrete component proposals and their meaning

1. **Preference within a declared substrate panel.** For a preference claim, define D=e_PAP/(e_PAP+Σ e_alternative), with e_j=kcat_j/Km_j under a specified common system. It is the fractional efficiency assigned to the candidate within that tested panel under equal low-substrate exposure, not a probability or completeness of substrate discovery. Substituting published values gives a descriptive optimized-condition share 42/(42+0.0027+0.025)=0.999341. The condition mismatch means this is not yet the matched-condition preference cell. An activity-capability claim should not be penalized for additional true activities; D applies only when the annotation asserts preference/specificity. A finite panel also does not establish exclusivity.

2. **Experimental comparability outside substrate identity.** Use pH/Mg response measurements to bring efficiencies to a declared common condition or to calculate their response similarity; do not grade substrate difference itself. Let h_j be measured activity(response in shared condition)/activity(response in substrate-j assay condition). Corrected e_j*=e_j h_j, and D*=e_PAP*/Σ_j e_j*. Uncertainty in h_j enters D* once. The paper supplies response curves for PAP and explains optimized alternative protocols, but no fully quantified cross-condition h_j values were extracted here. A 20-fold Mg concentration difference is not automatically a 1/20 relevance multiplier because response is not linear in Mg. Likewise enzyme concentration normalization already enters kcat; do not apply another 5/110 dose penalty.

3. **Validity of a finite-time rate.** Define L=v_average(0,T)/v_initial for the same substrate concentration, where v_average is net product/T and v_initial is an independently resolved early slope. L measures rate attenuation, not precision. It can enter a reliability component through min(L,1/L), or the fitted rate can be corrected by a justified progress model; do not do both. Conditional calculation only: a simple irreversible Michaelis–Menten depletion model gives L=[(S0−S1)(Km+S0)]/[S0{S0−S1+Km ln(S0/S1)}]. Using S0=25 µM, S1=15.75 µM and fitted Km=8.1 µM gives L≈0.9436. This assumes no product inhibition or other losses and reuses a Km inferred from finite endpoints; it is not independent validation and cannot repair low-PAP points. The measured 37% conversion alone is not a fixed quality penalty.

4. **Calibration accuracy and uncertainty.** For a held-out standard after applying the measured MS response factor, R_cal=min(measured/known,known/measured). Separately propagate uncertainty in response factor, ion intensity ratio, internal-standard concentration, dilution, timing and protein concentration into rate/efficiency. A sum of squared relative uncertainties applies only when the terms are independent; shared calibration covariance must be preserved for comparisons. Report the actual relative uncertainty and use the proposed R_prec=1/[1+Var(estimate)/estimate²] for a declared statistical unit. The factor's 1.5 value is not a validity score; no numeric R_prec is assigned from undefined ±.

5. **Isolation.** Protein-specific product fraction I=1−v_contamination/v_net is separate from h_j and L. A substrate blank measures spontaneous hydrolysis; it does not measure a co-purified phosphatase. Quantitative catalytic attribution remains unfilled. The reported intact mass 30578±1 Da versus expected 30578.5 Da is compatible with the intended tagged protein; its absolute fractional discrepancy is approximately 16.35 ppm. That is not the mass error of the chemical product and is not a contaminant contribution bound.

## Explicit combination, missing inputs and complementary measurements

A proposal for a PAP-preference evidence contribution is E_preference=R_cal×R_prec×R_linearity×I×D*, with experimental condition adjustment contained in D* (or retained as a separate comparability component if no adjustment is made). The narrower activity-only contribution omits D*. This preserves the user's distinct scoring meanings instead of summing a paper-wide grade.

Synthetic illustration only: if D*=0.999341, R_cal=0.98, R_prec=0.99, R_linearity=0.95 and I=0.90, the proposed preference product is 0.829162. Those four reliability/attribution values are invented for arithmetic; the paper does not fill them. The ratio 15,555.6 cannot replace those cells, and the high specificity share does not make missing attribution or low-concentration linearity disappear.

The needed complementary measurements are low-PAP time courses or a validated integrated kinetic fit, matched-condition substrate comparisons, held-out matrix calibration, matched product confirmation for alternatives, and quantitative contaminant bounds. PAPS cannot be assigned a rank from failed kinetics: its instability creates PAP and its lithium salt inhibits CysQ, so a mixed-substrate/time model is required. Genetic complementation and native-deletion conclusions remain A106. No target correspondence or native physiological flux is invented.
