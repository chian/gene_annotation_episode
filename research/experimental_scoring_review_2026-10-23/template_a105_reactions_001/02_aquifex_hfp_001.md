## Experimental definitions

Aquifex aeolicus HFP/PP17 and IMP/PP11 were separately overproduced in E. coli. These were different proteins compared for phosphatase chemistry, not mutants replacing a native allele in an organism. The deliberate changed variable was the added phosphatase. Neither a wild-type HFP plus mutant HFP mixture nor HFP plus IMP was the intended one-pot comparison. Each preparation could include host proteins; E. coli RpiA was actually detected by MS in the HFP preparation. Thus HFP and an unintended alternative catalyst coexist in the relevant sample.

The staged screen first generated unstable ARAPDP/DHBP with RibBA and RibD anaerobically overnight at 37 °C, then added candidate phosphatase for 1 hour at 55 °C, then downstream RibH/RibE for 2 hours. The final optical endpoint was riboflavin, measured at 470 nm to reduce interference from substances absorbing at 410 nm. Two of 22 sufficiently expressed screened proteins supported this staged output. This count is screening coverage, not 22 independent measurements of one protein's activity.

The one-pot comparison used the same pathway mixture, buffer, 55 °C temperature and 4.5-hour endpoint with 8.4 µM HFP versus 8.8 µM IMP. HFP supported riboflavin production; IMP failed. Separate phosphorus NMR showed IMP dephosphorylating NADPH and Ru5P. This explains an endpoint discrepancy without interpreting it as inconsistent replication of one reaction. HFP's FMN dephosphorylation was also observed, but instability of substrates/intermediates at the required temperatures prevented a valid comparative ARAPDP/FM N kinetic assessment.

For phosphorus NMR, nominal candidate dose was 1.4 µM, 1-hour incubation at 55 °C, with matched enzyme-buffer blanks and chemical standards; E. coli YigB controls were incubated at 37 °C for stability reasons. The deliberate phosphatase comparison therefore does not make temperature matched for the YigB contrast. Triplicate phosphorus assays were reported; 512 scans within a spectrum are not 512 replicates. Carbon NMR was performed once. Independent preparation counts remain unspecified.

## Native numerical examples

* HFP-containing preparations established 39.7% Ru5P and 60.3% R5P from either starting substrate. The measured equilibrium R5P/Ru5P ratio is 60.3/39.7=1.51889. Its denominator is the remaining ketose pool, not a blank, wild type or amount of HFP. It is a chemical-equilibrium observation and cannot quantify how much catalysis belongs to HFP.
* After affinity/size purification and 90 °C for 2 minutes, reaching the equilibrium changed from less than 10 minutes to more than 18 hours. The times differ by more than 108-fold, but they are not matched initial rates; protein recovery, sample concentrations and equilibration thresholds must be checked before using that ratio quantitatively.
* Four of five randomly chosen other preparations showed the same isomerisation: 4/5=0.8 is the observed prevalence in those five sampled preparations, not an 80% contaminant activity fraction, a false-positive probability for all assays, or a numerical score for HFP.
* One purification stage yielded 7 mg/mL by Bradford, with HFP estimated visually from a gel as approximately 5 mg/mL. The estimated mass fraction 5/7≈0.714 is preparation-specific, imprecise, and not the fraction of isomerase activity. The paper explicitly used further purification for NMR; do not silently use this mass fraction for every later sample.
* Nominal one-pot dose similarity is 8.4/8.8=0.954545. Its meaning is correspondence of supplied molar dose, outside the intended change of phosphatase identity. At a 4.5-hour coupled endpoint, response proportionality to dose has not been shown, so it is not automatically a 0.954545 correction to product amount or overall relevance.

The primary NMR method later prints 0.67 M for the reversibility experiment whereas nearby preparation details give 0.67 mM for sugar-phosphate assays. This source unit inconsistency is retained explicitly. No inferred correction is used to calculate a rate or catalytic efficiency.

## Proposed components within the numerical sheet

**Experimental comparability.** Use measured response to the non-intended variables, not an overall quality ladder. For an enzyme-linear regime, a dose cell can be C_E=min(E_a,E_b)/max(E_a,E_b); here its nominal value is 0.954545 with linearity unresolved. If concentrations are already used to normalise fitted per-enzyme rates, do not apply C_E again. Different 37/55 °C controls need temperature-response or stability measurements before a quantitative C_T. Proposed comparison combination remains C=C_E×C_T×C_buffer×C_time only where each factor captures a distinct measured effect; coupled differences need a joint model. Intended phosphatase identity is excluded. Unknown factors remain unknown.

**Validity for an unstable substrate.** Let S_0 be initial substrate and S_blank(t) substrate remaining in a no-enzyme assay. A measured substrate-exposure component is A_S=[∫_0^T S_blank(t)dt]/(T S_0), dimensionless. It evaluates how well a nominal constant-substrate interpretation approximates the actual exposure; it does not itself identify the product or the protein. If measured blank loss is first-order, S_blank(t)=S_0e^(−kt), then A_S=(1−e^(−kT))/(kT), where k is fitted from the blank time course. A separate competing-substrate-retention comparison D_j=S_j,with enzyme(T)/S_j,blank(T) quantifies consumption by the added preparation. It addresses assay-system compatibility if that substrate must remain for the coupled readout. Do not multiply the same substrate loss into both A_S and D_j without distinguishing spontaneous from enzyme-associated loss. Neither operand is numerically available from this paper's prose.

**Chemical isolation.** Fit the measured spectrum to calibrated component spectra, A(λ)=Σ_j ε_j(λ)lc_j, with nonnegative concentrations c_j, path length l and extinction spectra ε_j. Then the product-specific signal fraction at the reporting wavelength is q_spec=ε_product(λ)c_product / Σ_j ε_j(λ)c_j. This is a proposed calculation for separating riboflavin from overlapping products, not an assumed q_spec=1 because 470 nm was chosen. Actual spectra, calibration curves, residuals and matrix effects are missing. Independent chemical identification can reduce ambiguity in which spectra belong in the fit, but does not count as an independent replication of pathway flux.

**Protein isolation.** For a co-purified catalyst, v_total=v_HFP+v_RpiA+v_other under an explicitly checked additive initial-rate model. Let v_contam=Σ_j E_j kcat_j S/(Km_j+S), using measured contaminant molar abundance E_j and kinetics measured in the same conditions. Then I_HFP=(v_total−v_contam)/v_total. A confidently bounded I near zero would make strong preparation chemistry contribute little to HFP activity. Mass purity 0.714 cannot fill I: a tiny high-turnover enzyme can dominate the rate. The cited RpiA turnover of 2100 s−1 is a prior paper's value and is not a measured catalytic rate in this 55 °C mixture; it cannot be inserted without qualification. Here contaminant abundance/kinetics and matched total initial rates are missing. Negative residuals are model/measurement diagnostics, not proof of negative enzyme activity.

**Precision and consistency.** Use Var(v_net)=Var(v_sample)+Var(v_blank)−2Cov(sample,blank) and R_prec=1/[1+Var(v_net)/v_net²] for a declared independent unit, as proposed in note 01. Between-preparation variance belongs in that uncertainty; time points and repeated spectra of the same preparation do not create new independent preparations. The staged and one-pot results measure different systems, so do not pool them into a disagreement penalty. A product chemically observed by two nuclei is complementary identification, not n=2 preparation replication.

## Explicit combination and example

For a narrowly stated intrinsic reaction, a proposed combination is E=C×R_cal×R_prec×q_spec×I_HFP, preserving each cell. If an additional substrate-stability component is needed for the intended kinetic interpretation, incorporate the measured exposure in the kinetic model first; use its uncertainty there rather than both adjusting v and multiplying A_S again. For a coupled one-pot endpoint, retain substrate-competition components as relevance limitations of that endpoint; it cannot simply become the intrinsic ARAPDP turnover rate.

Synthetic arithmetic, not paper data: matched-condition comparability C=0.95, calibration agreement R_cal=0.98, net relative standard error=0.10 gives R_prec=1/1.01, spectral product fraction q_spec=0.90, and quantified contaminants explaining 80% of net signal give I=0.20. The product is 0.95×0.98×(1/1.01)×0.90×0.20=0.165921. Keeping all other values fixed but I=0.90 gives 0.746644. This is exactly why a clear product signal and a comparable assay need a separate attribution component. Neither I value comes from the paper's four-of-five finding. The comparison is a proposed response of the numerical sheet, not annotation probability.

The paper adds evidence on interference, substrate destruction, host contamination and ambiguous normalization. It supplies no complete measured aggregate. Native essentiality, deletion/restoration and pathway plausibility do not become A105 scores. No target-to-HFP molecular correspondence has been measured here.
