## Actual experiments and definitions

The assayed proteins are E. coli K12 QueF/YqcD and B. subtilis JH642 (trpC2 pheA1) QueF/YkvM, cloned separately into pET-30Xa and expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3). His6 fusions were purified by nickel affinity; tag-cleaved proteins were also prepared. Purity was reported above 90% by SDS-PAGE, which measures approximate protein mass composition, not the fraction of observed catalytic activity caused by QueF. E. coli FolE and RibA were produced as separate positive-control enzymes for alternative GTP-cyclohydrolase assays. QueF, FolE and RibA do not coexist in one reaction in those comparisons.

The intended activity question changes substrate/cofactor chemistry: GTP cyclohydrolase versus NADPH-dependent conversion of preQ0 to preQ1. Cyclohydrolase tests used radiolabeled formate release, fluorescence and HPLC, with active FolE/RibA controls and many conditions. No QueF activity was detected, but no numeric limit is provided in the inspected text. Three negative readout types are not three independent preparations and do not establish a zero rate.

The positive reaction was followed by NADPH absorbance loss at 334, 340 and 365 nm or reverse-phase HPLC. Routine conditions were 100 mM HEPES pH 7.5, 1 mM DTT, 50–100 mM KCl, 1–100 µM preQ0, 2–150 µM NADPH, at 30°C. Figure 2A instead specifies 100 mM Tris pH 7.5, 100 µM preQ0 and 100 µM NADPH, with E. coli QueF at 0,19,38,95 or190 µg/ml. These dose differences are intentional in the enzyme-dependence experiment. They must not be treated as uncontrolled differences within that experiment. ## What is comparable and what is isolated

The UV assay and the preparative HPLC/NMR experiment differ in buffer, pH, substrate and cofactor concentration, enzyme dose and duration. They are complementary measurements, not identical-condition replicates. Their intended endpoint difference—continuous cofactor consumption versus product identity—is retained; the other changes matter if one tries to assign the same rate or quantitative coupling efficiency to both.

Isolation has two separate parts. No-enzyme signal measures spontaneous cofactor loss, whereas no-substrate plus enzyme measures cofactor loss caused by the preparation without the intended conversion. Neither control alone establishes the fraction of total cofactor loss forming preQ1. Matched quantitative product formation supplies that fraction. Likewise, >90% gel purity does not establish >90% activity attribution. A very small contaminant mass can carry a large activity.

## Proposed numerical cells, with defined arithmetic

1. **Net cofactor and product rates.** Convert absorbance slopes to molar cofactor rates using the measured extinction coefficient and optical path, then subtract matched no-substrate/background rates. Each operand retains its calibration and uncertainty. For the proposed four-electron nitrile-to-amine reaction, chemical accounting predicts two NADPH per preQ1. Let `q = 2 × n(preQ1_net)/n(NADPH_net_consumed)` for a matched interval. q scores the measured productive portion of the cofactor signal; q = 1 is ideal stoichiometric coupling, not something established by observing a product peak. Significant q > 1 indicates a calibration, timing or reaction-model problem and should not be silently clipped. A quantitative product assay can replace the proxy; do not then charge the same uncoupling twice.

2. **Signal uncertainty with native support.** If the reported relative SE <0.10 applies to a particular positive net rate, the proposed mean-precision conversion `R_mean = 1/[1 + (SE_net/mean_net)²]` is bounded above0.990099. This is an actual conditional bound from a reported uncertainty statement, not a numerical category. It measures precision of that estimated mean, not between-preparation reproducibility or confidence that the reaction is correctly assigned. Background uncertainty and covariance must already be included in SE_net; if not, the bound cannot be claimed for the controlled rate. Do not add a separate n-based bonus or count the three wavelengths as three replicates.

3. **Assay-to-assay cofactor comparability.** Under a Michaelis-Menten cofactor response `g(N)=N/(Km+N)`, using the reported Km36 µM gives saturation fractions100/136=0.735294 and500/536=0.932836 at100 and500 µM NADPH. The proposed nuisance-condition comparability contribution is their smaller/larger ratio, `C_N=0.788235`. This is a conditional calculation: Km must apply across the two buffers and pH values, substrate effects must be separable, and the curve must describe the measured interval. Those assumptions are not established. It does not penalize a deliberately titrated cofactor within the kinetic experiment; it concerns transferring a quantitative result between different assays. Other measured nuisance responses would combine multiplicatively only if separable; otherwise a joint model is needed.

4. **Cofactor-limited attainable yield.** The preparative reaction contains equal0.5 mM substrate and NADPH. Under two-NADPH stoichiometry and no regeneration, at most0.25 mM preQ1 can form, or50% of initial preQ0. A proposed reaction-completion number is `Y = measured product / min(initial substrate,initial NADPH/2)`, with measured blank and recovery corrections. This normalizes against the actually attainable amount; it prevents calling50% conversion a50% assay failure. Y is not filled because product amount was not reported. It is distinct from q: Y describes completion relative to the limiting reagent; q describes the fraction of consumed cofactor doing the claimed chemistry. For an initial-rate claim, Y is generally a diagnostic rather than a multiplier.

5. **Quantitative accuracy and attribution.** A held-out product standard with recovery `r=measured/known` can populate proposed `R_cal=min(r,1/r)`. Matching an HPLC peak does not provide r. Activity attribution `I=1−v_contaminant/v_net` needs matched contaminant rates or a validated abundance-and-kinetics model; gel purity alone supplies neither. Product chemical shifts and coelution remain native identity evidence. Calibration of an identity likelihood would need distributions for relevant alternative compounds; none is invented here.

6. **Negative alternative limit.** Retain the cyclohydrolase non-detection and active controls. To score an exclusion or a specificity ratio, obtain the limit on product/turnover under each tested condition and the quantitative positive-control recovery. Neither is printed in the inspected source. “Three different assays found nothing” cannot be assigned a0/0.25/0.5 confidence grade or multiplied as independent negative probabilities.

## Explicit compilation and synthetic illustration

A candidate component product for the cofactor-derived reaction measurement is `E = R_cal × R_mean × q × I × C`, with C the measured comparability outside the deliberately changed variables. These terms represent quantitative calibration, uncertainty, productive chemistry, protein attribution, and nuisance-condition correspondence respectively. They are not overall category grades. Same-source covariances remain part of uncertainty; the product itself is not a probability and has not been selected as an annotation-credit formula.

Synthetic illustration: net NADPH consumption100 nmol and calibrated preQ1 formation45 nmol yield q = 2×45/100 =0.90. Suppose held-out recovery0.98, net relative SE0.10, independently measured I0.95, and use the conditional C_N0.788235 from above. Then `E =0.98 × [1/1.01] ×0.90 ×0.95 ×0.788235 =0.653358`. Only Km and the assay concentrations used for conditional C_N originate in the paper; the product amount, recovery and I are invented. The relative SE uses the boundary of the reported bound for illustration. It is not a completed score for QueF.

Product HPLC/NMR addresses the weakness of nonspecific cofactor disappearance; time-matched quantitative product and cofactor assays address productive coupling. Independent preparations address consistency; varied protein doses address dependence on enzyme amount. Repeating the same reaction at three wavelengths addresses spectral consistency, not biological replication. Missing response calibration, exact rate variance, preparation identities, coupling, impurity activity, buffer effects and target correspondence remain explicit inputs. No present target sequence, candidate annotation or ybhP evidence is assumed.
