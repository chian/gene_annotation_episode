# FolM: optical calibration, substrate preference and unstable substrates

New immutable research note, 2026-10-23. This is an A105 component proposal,
not an adopted scoring sheet, implemented reader or target annotation result.
Primary source: [Pribat et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2805310/),
doi:10.1128/JB.01198-09. Assigned case: `replacement_folM`.

## Experimental definitions and native measurements

The assayed protein was E. coli FolM, expressed from the prior pFolM construct
with an N-terminal His tag in E. coli BL21 CodonPlus(DE3)-RIPL. Protein was
purified by nickel affinity chromatography, desalted, and measured by Bradford
assay with bovine serum albumin as standard. The comparison was between pterin
substrates supplied to this preparation; two different FolM proteins did not
coexist. This is a direct biochemical comparison, separate from the paper's
gene deletion, complementation and organismal synthesis conclusions reserved
for A106. No relationship to the project's supplied target has been measured
by this research note.

Methods, “Biochemical analysis,” specify 100 µL reactions at 22–23 °C in 100 mM
potassium phosphate, pH 6.0, with 100 µM NADPH, 2–20 µg enzyme and 10 mM
beta-mercaptoethanol except for the quinonoid assay. The substrate panel used
50 µM pterin. Components other than NADPH were preincubated for 30 s and
reactions started with NADPH. Pterins were freshly prepared under nitrogen,
protected from light and titrated spectrophotometrically. No-enzyme reactions
corrected substrate breakdown and background NADPH oxidation. “Same protocol”
does not establish the exact protein dose used for every panel member from the
reported 2–20 µg range; rates must be normalized by the actual mass and remain
in the validated linear range.

At 340 nm the pterin itself contributes to the absorbance change. The reported
composite molar extinction changes at pH 6.0 are:

| Substrate | Composite Δε340, M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ |
| --- | ---: |
| Dihydromonapterin (H₂MPt) | 6,028 |
| Dihydrofolate | 12,300 |
| Dihydrohydroxymethylpterin | 10,295 |

Consequently, a common NADPH coefficient is not a valid universal conversion
for these assays. For high H₂MPt concentrations, substrate absorbance required
a discontinuous method: react for 30 s, add 400 µL of 0.5 M Tris at pH 8.0 to
inactivate FolM, then read the 500 µL stopped mixture. The composite coefficient
under the stopped pH 8.0 conditions is 11,040 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹. The pH and dilution are
part of converting signal to amount, not independent confidence factors.

Results, “Recombinant FolM has dihydromonapterin reductase activity,” and Fig. 4
report a 16-fold H₂MPt/dihydrofolate activity comparison at the common 50 µM
substrate concentration. Panel errors are SEM from three replicates; numerical
bar heights and errors were not independently digitized here, and independent
protein preparation count is unknown. H₂MPt kinetic values from this paper are
Vmax = 5.99 µmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ and Km = 147 µM. Dihydrofolate Vmax = 0.083 µmol
min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ and Km = 9.5 µM are quoted from the earlier 2003 paper. That earlier
paper was not newly read here, so these are cross-paper values quoted by the
2010 source, not a freshly verified matched kinetic experiment.

Some H₂hydroxymethylpterin activity was reported; H₂neopterin, monapterin and
NADH replacement did not provide the corresponding positive reaction, with no
quantitative detection bounds extracted here. Quinonoid H₂MPt rapidly
rearranges into ordinary H₂MPt. Its assay generated substrate in situ using
horseradish peroxidase, hydrogen peroxide and H₄MPt, omitted the reducing agent,
and did not demonstrate extra activity beyond reaction with the rearranged
H₂MPt. It therefore does not isolate direct use of the quinonoid species.

## Conversion into numerical components of the user's sheet

These are proposed calculations and combination behavior. The measured values
above are not replaced by a category grade.

1. **Calibrated net molecular rate.** For a continuous assay, let A′sample and
   A′blank be absorbance slopes per minute, l the optical path in cm, Δεj the
   composite coefficient for substrate j, V the reaction volume in L, and m
   the measured enzyme mass in mg. A positive consumption rate is
   `vj = −(A′sample − A′blank) × 10^6 × V / (l × Δεj × m)` in µmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹,
   with sign chosen according to the documented optical reaction. Each operand
   is an instrument reading or calibration, not an assigned score. For the
   stopped assay, replace the slope with the blank-corrected absorbance change
   divided by the 0.5 min incubation and use the **final** 0.0005 L volume and
   stopped-assay coefficient. Equivalently multiply the stopped concentration
   by five and use the original reaction volume; do not do both.

   The optical ratio corresponding to a molar activity ratio of 16 is
   `16 × 6028 / 12300 = 7.8413008` when path, volume and mass are equal. Thus
   the 16-fold paper result cannot be reconstituted by treating a raw optical
   ratio as a molar ratio. Likewise `11040 / 6028 = 1.8314532` is the change
   in conversion coefficient between the specified readout conditions; it is
   not 183% reliability or a penalty. These checks test calculation validity.

2. **Quantitative calibration reliability.** A separate held-out standard
   processed through the complete assay would give recovery
   `r = recovered amount / known amount`. Proposed bounded contribution
   `Rcal = min(r, 1/r)` assesses residual quantitative bias after the native
   conversion above. If recovery were 0.98, Rcal would be 0.98; this is a
   synthetic illustration. An extinction coefficient is not itself a recovery
   measurement. The necessary standard recovery is not reported in the
   extracted results, so an actual Rcal remains unknown.

3. **Uncertainty of a net rate or contrast.** Let `v = vsample − vblank` in
   common rate units. Preserve `Var(v) = Var(vsample) + Var(vblank) −
   2 Cov(vsample, vblank)` when pairing exists. The proposed component
   `Rprecision = 1 / [1 + Var(v)/v²]` decreases as relative uncertainty grows.
   It has no interpretation as a posterior probability. The variance must
   explicitly refer either to uncertainty in the estimated mean or to
   variability across independent preparations. A reported SEM and a
   preparation-to-preparation SD cannot silently substitute for each other.
   “Three replicates” cannot populate between-preparation consistency. Raw
   means, errors and pairing are needed for an actual value here.

4. **Preference at the tested concentration.** For the limited proposition
   that this preparation prefers H₂MPt to dihydrofolate under the stated
   50 µM conditions, the proposed fractional-rate component is
   `D50 = vH2MPt / (vH2MPt + vfolate) = 16 / 17 = 0.94117647`.
   This is a preference component for these two tested substrates, not a
   probability of native function or a general assay reliability score.
   A positive capability claim does not require this preference multiplier.
   Additional substrates expand the denominator only when the scientific
   question explicitly concerns preference across that declared panel.

5. **Kinetic meaning and comparability.** The quoted Vmax ratio is
   `5.99 / 0.083 = 72.168675`, whereas the quoted efficiency ratio is
   `(5.99/147)/(0.083/9.5) = 4.663962`. They answer different questions.
   Applying the two quoted Michaelis–Menten curves at 50 µM predicts a ratio
   of 21.797138, not the matched-panel 16. This is not a demonstrated
   inconsistency: the dihydrofolate kinetic values originate in another
   paper/preparation and uncertainty and cross-assay comparability have not
   been established. The corresponding saturation fractions 50/(147+50) =
   0.253807 and 50/(9.5+50) = 0.840336 are intrinsic consequences of the
   deliberately different substrates. They must not become nuisance
   comparability penalties against that substrate-preference experiment.

   Comparability instead concerns unplanned differences such as pH,
   temperature, actual active enzyme dose, optical method and time interval.
   For a measured nuisance response g(x), a proposed component is
   `Cj = min[g(xa), g(xb)] / max[g(xa), g(xb)]`, excluding substrate identity
   itself. Use separate products only for demonstrably separable effects;
   otherwise fit the joint response. A measured correction to the native
   rate and a penalty for the same rate loss must not both be applied.

6. **Isolation of the molecular effect.** No-enzyme subtraction addresses
   substrate breakdown and uncatalyzed NADPH oxidation, but does not by
   itself identify the enzymatic product or rule out an enzyme-associated
   alternative redox reaction. A matched product measurement can populate
   `q = stoichiometrically scaled product formed / NADPH consumed`, with
   the stoichiometry declared for the reaction. For one two-electron pterin
   reduction, q would compare one product molecule with one NADPH consumed.
   The extracted experiment does not supply a calibrated paired-product
   quantity sufficient to fill q. Orthogonal product identity addresses
   chemical assignment; calibrated product amount addresses productive
   coupling. They are related but different missing measurements.

   For the unstable quinonoid species, an exposure component
   `X = integral(Squinonoid(t) dt) / [T × Squinonoid(0)]` quantifies survival
   over the assay. It does not establish direct FolM use: simultaneous
   stable-intermediate and product measurements are needed to separate
   chemical rearrangement followed by ordinary H₂MPt reduction. Multiplying
   the observed total rate by X would not solve that mixture. A kinetic
   partition model or a direct discriminating measurement is required.

## Explicit combination, with limits

For the two-substrate preference proposition, a possible sheet compilation is
`Epreference = D50 × C × Rcal × Rprecision × q × I`, where C combines measured
nonredundant nuisance-comparability components, q is productive coupling and
I is the fraction attributable to FolM after measured preparation background.
This is a proposed numerical index, not a calibrated probability and not an
adopted common A105 formula. Productive coupling and contamination attribution
must not penalize the same competing process twice. Where the contamination
measurement already partitions the productive flux, one joint fraction may
replace their separate product.

For a **synthetic arithmetic example only**, D50 = 16/17 uses the native rate
ratio, while assumed C = 1, Rcal = 0.98, relative variance = 0.01, q = 1 and
I = 0.95 give `(16/17) × 1 × 0.98 × [1/(1+0.01)] × 1 × 0.95 = 0.86755970`.
The last five inputs are not measured FolM evidence. No actual full score is
filled. Protein/system relevance to an annotation target is a separate set of
unfilled component calculations, not an assumed universal transfer weight.

The native matched-panel result can fill a preference number; it cannot fill
calibration recovery, independent preparation consistency, product-coupling
fraction, contaminant activity fraction or target relationship. Those gaps
remain visible instead of receiving ordinal grades. Genetic evidence in this
paper does not fill them under A105.
