## Experimental definitions before scoring

The coding sequences came from E. coli BW25113 and were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3). CsiD carried an N-terminal six-histidine tag; LhgO a C-terminal six-histidine tag. These are purified recombinant preparations, not a cellular mixture of deliberate wild-type and mutant copies. No separate native protein was deliberately added; the residual endogenous-protein fraction and its catalytic contribution were not quantitatively determined in the text inspected. SDS gel checking is not a measured catalytic purity fraction.

CsiD kinetics measured oxygen disappearance with 180 nM CsiD at 30 °C in MOPS pH 7.2, salt, 400 µM ascorbate and 4 µM iron. The intended variables were glutarate concentration or alpha-ketoglutarate concentration. The companion substrate was held at 1 mM alpha-ketoglutarate or 5 mM glutarate, respectively. Alternative C2–C7 dicarboxylates were tested at 5 mM. NMR instead used 10 mM of each substrate, ammonium acetate pH 7.25, overnight incubation and spectra at 300 K. LC-MS of derivatised product was compared with authentic L- and D-2-hydroxyglutarate. These chemical checks address what oxygen consumption produces, but they were not simultaneous quantitative product/oxygen balances under the kinetic conditions.

LhgO directly reduced 100 µM UQ1, measured by absorbance at 278 nm with 650 nM enzyme (0.03 mg/mL), in HEPES pH 7.5 with salt/EDTA. Initial rate was varied with L-2-hydroxyglutarate concentration. Catalase was included to address peroxide effects on redox dyes. Purified enzyme oxygen consumption was undetected. Membranes and purified enzyme were also compared using DCPIP, with/without added UQ1 or MQ4; membrane results are a multicomponent electron-transfer system. Neither an oxygen terminal endpoint nor DCPIP reduction alone identifies which molecule is the immediate LhgO acceptor.

The paper's knockout/complementation and growth/metabolite-phenotype conclusions are parked for A106 and do not enter the proposed A105 quantities below. Assay blanks, authentic products, calibration and coupling controls remain measurement information for A105.

## Comparability is separate from isolation

**Comparability outside the intended changed variable.** In a substrate-specificity contrast, substrate identity is the intended difference and must not itself lower a comparability cell. Differences in protein amount, active fraction, time window, temperature, buffer and detection chemistry can confound the contrast. A recorded equal nominal dose is useful, but equal doses need not put different substrates at equal saturation. For estimating a preparation's catalytic specificity, compare fitted kcat/Km, with uncertainties, or explicitly compare at the same declared concentration; do not silently exchange those meanings.

For comparisons across the NMR and oxygen assays, the substrate-concentration contribution can be calculated conditionally from measured kinetics. Let f(S,K)=S/(K+S). Proposed substrate-response similarity is C_S=min(f_1,f_2)/max(f_1,f_2). With glutarate K=0.65 mM, f(5)=0.88496 and f(10)=0.93897, giving C_G=0.94248. For alpha-ketoglutarate K=0.1 mM, f(1)=0.90909 and f(10)=0.99010, giving C_K=0.91818. If the response factors were separable and the fitted apparent constants transferred unchanged, C_G×C_K=0.86537 is a proposed concentration-only comparability component. It evaluates similarity of substrate saturation, not overall relevance or a probability. It does not correct the measured activity. The different buffers, time scales, temperature and two-substrate coupling mean those assumptions are unverified here; therefore 0.86537 is a conditional calculation, not a populated overall comparability score. For a substrate titration, the titrated concentration must be excluded from this nuisance-condition product.

Other nuisance-condition cells should use measured response curves where available: C_j=min(g_j(x_a),g_j(x_b))/max(g_j(x_a),g_j(x_b)), with g_j the observed activity response to that condition. pH or temperature differences alone cannot be converted through an invented universal tolerance. In log form the proposed combination is C=exp(−Σ_j |log(g_j(x_a)/g_j(x_b))|), only for distinct, separable effects. Coupled effects need a joint measured response rather than multiplying overlapping factors. Missing response curves leave the corresponding comparison uncomputed.

**Isolation of measured chemistry.** Oxygen consumption can include uncoupled co-substrate oxidation. Define productive coupling q=(net moles of chemically identified L2HG formed)/(net moles of O2 consumed), with both measured over the same interval and expected 1:1 stoichiometry. Values beyond stoichiometric bounds are diagnostics, not silently clipped evidence. The named product fraction p_L=n_L/(n_L+n_D+other identified hydroxylated products) would quantify stereochemical specificity once calibrated product amounts and detection limits exist. L-enantiomer standards address product assignment; they do not supply q. Neither q nor a numeric p_L is populated by the inspected paper text.

For protein attribution, independently estimate the fraction a of net product formation explained by contaminating reactions, then I=1−a. That fraction is not the protein mass purity. It requires matched blank/contaminant preparations and catalytic rates or equivalent experimental resolution. No numeric I follows from “purified.” Membrane oxygen and dye rates measure pathway output and cannot substitute for I for an intrinsic oxygen-oxidase claim.

## Concrete proposed numerical compilation

These are candidate calculations for the user's requested reliability/relevance components, not replacements by overall category grades:

* Calibration agreement R_cal=min(recovery,1/recovery), recovery=measured known standard/true standard. This penalises both over- and under-recovery continuously; its adequacy must be checked against calibration uncertainty. A mass match alone cannot serve as chemical identity.
* Net-signal precision R_prec=1/[1+Var(v_net)/v_net²], with v_net=v_sample−v_blank and Var(v_net)=Var(v_sample)+Var(v_blank)−2Cov(sample,blank). This scores repeatability of the net rate once its statistical unit is specified. It is not a probability that the annotation is correct; it cannot be calculated from an undefined ±. A negative or unresolved net effect retains its sign/bound separately.
* Productive endpoint fraction q converts a proxy rate into the fraction representing the specified chemistry. It addresses a weakness different from calibration and precision. I addresses attribution to the intended protein after background subtraction; the same blank or contaminant correction must not appear a second time as a separate signal bonus.
* The proposed measured-chemistry reliability component is R_chem=R_cal×R_prec×q×I, with the nuisance-condition comparability C kept separately visible. Experimental evidence adjusted for comparison would be E=C×R_chem. A target-transfer multiplier remains a separately justified numerical relationship with the supplied target; it is unknown here. This is explicit arithmetic for feedback, not adoption or W05 wiring.

Synthetic arithmetic only: recovery=0.98 gives R_cal=0.98; net-rate relative standard error=0.10 gives R_prec=1/(1+0.10²)=0.990099; measured productive coupling q=0.90; independently measured contamination fraction a=0.05 gives I=0.95. Then R_chem=0.98×0.990099×0.90×0.95=0.829604. If a separately supported C were 0.865366, E=0.717917. Those invented calibration/uncertainty/coupling/contamination inputs are not measurements from this paper. Native CsiD or LhgO rates alone cannot fill them.

This proposed product deliberately makes unresolved mechanism-specific weaknesses consequential. An optional sum over independent preparations would first pool their native rates with an appropriate variance/covariance model, not sum one score per figure. Identical samples seen by NMR and LC-MS strengthen chemical identification but do not increase independent-preparation count. A precise oxygen trace may coexist with an unresolved q; the missing cell is a named experimental gap, not permission to substitute 0.5.

## What this paper contributes to the sheet, and remaining work

It separates four numerically different questions: nuisance-condition similarity, calibration/precision of the measured signal, productive coupling to the named chemical endpoint, and attribution to the named protein. Complementary product identification addresses proxy ambiguity; calibrated simultaneous product/oxygen rates would quantify the remaining coupling gap. Recombinant active quinone reduction prevents an undetected oxygen signal being dismissed merely as an inactive preparation, while its unreported oxygen limit prevents a numerical intrinsic-oxidase exclusion score. Molecular identity/organism correspondence to an annotation target needs actual mapping data and is not supplied here.
