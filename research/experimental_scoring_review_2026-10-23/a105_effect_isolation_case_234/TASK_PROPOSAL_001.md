# Proposed recurring TASK: mixed contributors to measured molecular activity

Research text for conversation review only. No production prompt is changed, and the fixed user goal remains authoritative. This is an aspect of A105 investigation, not a new mandatory episode or an initial candidate-function requirement.

## TASK

Determine which molecular activities the experimental reports establish for the protein represented by the supplied sequence, selected file contents or snippets. A possible function can emerge during the investigation. Identify the actual assayed molecule, organism, construct, expression host, preparation and experimental system. Preserve whether comparison proteins replace one another or coexist and which partner or host molecules are also present. Separate the change deliberately tested from additional changes in construct, purification, expression, recovered amount, conditions or readout.

Examine the original experiment's chemical or physical endpoint and its resolution. Identify what was measured directly, what was inferred through a coupled reaction or reporter, and which assay or standard establishes the product's identity. Preserve units, denominators, sampling times, substrate availability, calibration, blanks, uncertainty and repetition meaning. Distinguish endpoint composition, approach-to-equilibrium time and initial rate. Do not infer a rate or an active molecular amount from an endpoint fraction or a visually dominant protein band.

Ask separately whether the intended molecule is responsible for the measured outcome. Identify plausible additional contributors from the expression host, co-purifying proteins, partner enzymes and competing reactions. A molecular identification of a contaminant establishes presence at its reported identification resolution; it does not by itself measure a molar amount or share of activity. Inspect the particular limitation of each control: a gel can conceal similarly sized proteins; more extensive purification can alter target amount or activity; a pathway failure can result from loss of an input; an apparently negative readout can be obscured by a competing reaction. Preserve these alternative explanations at the specific functional assertion they affect.

For each comparison, establish whether its endpoint, numerical scale, denominator, preparation, conditions and sampling window support the requested calculation. Inspect matched controls, perturbation selectivity, target recovery, contaminant amount or calibrated activity, spike-back and other available evidence. Do not treat a common name or publication, purification survival, heat treatment or a control label as proof of selective effect isolation. If a component model is nonlinear or the readout is at equilibrium, do not force it into additive rate subtraction. Keep a supported physical relationship separate from a stipulated sensitivity scenario.

Ask the applicable deterministic readers and calculators for the numerical components that the source actually supports. A matched additional rate can enter the existing contaminant subtraction; a product fraction, peptide count, spectral count or estimated protein mass cannot enter that rate slot merely because a number is available. Determine which background, calibration, amount or nuisance corrections were already applied and avoid repeating them. Preserve the original total measurement beside any derived isolated effect. When the source cannot identify or bound an additional contribution, retain the native chemistry and leave the molecule-specific activity unknown with the precise missing input identified.

Track which observations, standards, aliquots and preparations are shared. Complementary chemistry, purification, protein identification and controls can address different weaknesses without becoming independent replicates or confidence multipliers. A ratio, percentage or reconstruction of the same measured quantities is not additional confirmation. Preserve actual biological versus technical replication and the joint uncertainty required for a contrast; do not infer independence or zero covariance from separate tables or different component names.

Return the requested numerical components and the necessary aspect-specific interpretations: the observed chemistry, which contributor identities are established, which quantitative activity attribution is supported, the matchedness and model limitations, the named weakness addressed by each complementary assay and any useful unresolved follow-up. Separate absent source measurements from unimplemented reading or arithmetic. Do not create an overall quality ladder, function probability, target-transfer weight or final annotation credit. The parent retains its requested typed return meanings and decides which uncertainty to investigate next.
