# YjeH: precise extracellular appearance does not isolate a transporter

Source: Liu et al., 2015, https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.02242-15 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4616930/. Original corpus and retained packet reviewed; primary Materials/Methods and Results fully read through Fig. 8, including Tables 1–3. PMC browser access showed a challenge; direct public HTML retrieval succeeded with `?report=xml`. No supplementary data or figure curves digitized. This is a new review of an existing corpus paper, not a new discovery or experiment.

## Experimental definition

*E. coli* W3110 backgrounds: H2 is ΔyjeH plus empty pACYC184; H4 is intact yjeH plus empty pACYC184; H5 is intact yjeH plus pWYE2132 expressing yjeH. H5 contains chromosomal and plasmid sources of the same YjeH sequence, not wild-type and mutated proteins. For the expression comparison, YjeH abundance is the intended change, so unequal abundance must not automatically become an experimental-comparability penalty. Genetic causal contribution, analogue tolerance, CCCP/ATPase perturbation interpretations, mutant effects and fermentation improvement stay in A106. This note evaluates the direct measured amino-acid appearance and its limitations for A105.

In the transport assay, 5 mM Met-Met, Leu-Leu, Ile-Ile or Val-Val supplies intracellular amino acid through peptide uptake and hydrolysis. Cells were used at OD600=2, corresponding to 0.84 mg cell dry weight/ml, at 37 °C. Silicone oil separation and DNFB-HPLC measure extracellular and intracellular amino acids. These are native living cells with other transporters, synthesis, consumption, possible leakage and peptide metabolism, not a purified YjeH preparation. Uptake assays used a different starvation/C−N− protocol, so absence of uptake there is not a same-condition measurement of reversible export.

Table 3 reports extracellular-appearance slopes for methionine of 105.6±1.41 (H2), 127.6±1.21 (H4), and 173.0±1.38 (H5) nmol min^-1 (mg dry weight)^-1. The ± values are SD. Fig. 1 says three replicates, without establishing whether these are independent biological preparations. Intracellular plateaus are approximately 120, 80 and 30 mM, respectively. These plateaus are outcomes during transport; they are not automatically baseline substrate-loading mismatches.

## Quantitative sheet contributions

**Normalization and endpoint validity.** For each preparation, `J_appearance=(dC_out/dt) × V_medium / m_dry_cells`. At the specified 0.84 mg/ml, H5's reported 173 corresponds to `173×0.84=145.32 nmol ml^-1 min^-1=145.32 µM/min` extracellular appearance. This derived conversion is not an independent measurement. Protein-normalized BioY rates and dry-mass-normalized YjeH rates cannot be pooled as if their denominator were identical. For the target-specific membrane transport interpretation, the balance is `J_appearance = J_export − J_reuptake + J_lysis + J_extracellular_generation`. Each correction needs its own measurement, with covariances if shared samples are used. Intracellular concentration also depends on peptide uptake/hydrolysis and metabolism; it cannot be used as a transport flux by itself.

**Consistency and precision.** H5 methionine SD/mean is `1.38/173=0.007977`, or 0.798%. A proposed repeat-precision component `p_repeat=J²/(J²+SD²)` gives 0.999936. This evaluates variation of the reported repeat measurements around their mean, not experiment independence, systematic accuracy, target specificity, or a probability of a correct annotation. A standard error for independent preparations cannot be inferred from 'three replicates' without resolving replicate meaning. No second multiplier from CV should be applied after using the same SD in p_repeat.

**Comparability.** Baseline loading, membrane energy, cell dry mass, viable fraction, accessible intracellular substrate and peptide-hydrolysis supply can differ outside the intended variable. A raw `30/80=0.375` plateau ratio is not a defensible comparability score: the lower pool may be the consequence of stronger export. Even for independently measured initial loading, flux dependence could be `f(C)=C/(K+C)`, so `f(30)/f(80)` ranges from about 0.375 to 1 as K varies; K and the correct pre-readout concentrations are missing. A meaningful score would calculate nuisance-induced predicted flux change from an experimentally supported response model, or normalize flux to comparable starting substrate; it must not penalize the intended transport outcome as an unwanted difference.

**Isolation.** The appreciable methionine appearance in H2 demonstrates that the readout is mixed. The arithmetic `(173−105.6)/173=0.38960` is only the expression/deletion contrast as a fraction of H5 total appearance under those different cellular states. It is not a measured molecular fraction of transport through YjeH, and its causal scoring belongs to A106. It cannot fill A105's target-specific isolation component without assumptions that other transport, pool supply and leakage contributions remain unchanged. No such assumption is silently made. Reconstituted transport, isotope-resolved influx/efflux balances, extracellular peptide-hydrolysis blanks, viability/leak markers and quantified pool/loading time courses would address distinct remaining weaknesses.

**Combination proposal, not an actual score.** For a defined direct-export observation, one possible proposed compilation is `Q_assay=100 × m_loading × c_recovery × f_true_movement × p_repeat`, where m_loading is similarity of *baseline* transport opportunities after excluding the intended difference; c_recovery is measured extraction/HPLC recovery symmetry `min(r,1/r)`; f_true_movement is the measured fraction of extracellular signal due to transmembrane movement after leakage/extracellular generation correction; p_repeat is defined above. The actual paper supplies only p_repeat among these scored cells as extracted. Thus `Q_assay=99.9936 × m_loading × c_recovery × f_true_movement`, with three missing operands. Do not report 99.9936 as the overall score. This explicit multiplication shows why excellent repeat precision cannot repair unisolated readout. Target relevance additionally requires the actual target/system relationship and cannot be inferred from a transporter-family model alone. No target correspondence for ybhP is invented.

Same-sample intracellular/outside pools and fitted rates must not count as independent repetitions. The methionine and leucine rates use different substrates and loading kinetics, so their ratio is not automatically a specificity score. Chemical selectivity needs common accessible-substrate/gradient normalization and comparable detection ranges; negative substrate results require detection limits.
