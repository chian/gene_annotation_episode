## Source and experimental definitions

Original paper: Sunden F, Peck A, Salzman J, Ressl S, Herschlag D (2015), *Extensive site-directed mutagenesis reveals interconnected functional units in the alkaline phosphatase active site*, eLife 4:e06181, DOI [10.7554/eLife.06181](https://elifesciences.org/articles/06181). Publisher version-of-record PDF: [original PDF](https://cdn.elifesciences.org/articles/06181/elife-06181-v2.pdf). Both downloaded byte originals are retained under `sources/`; acquisition receipts identify URLs, status and retrieval time. Plain text is explicitly a derivative of the original. The table and figures are repeated representations, not additional measurements.

The actual studied molecule is *Escherichia coli* alkaline phosphatase (AP), with mutations introduced in defined combinations. The main example is AP D101A/E322Y, compared, when appropriate, with AP D101A/E322Y/K328A. These are different purified constructs assayed separately; neither result is an activity mixture of these two proteins. Both replace D101 and E322 relative to WT; the comparison deliberately changes K328 to alanine. WT is a separate comparator in the paper, not an additional wild-type fraction in either assay. No target sequence or similarity transfer is evaluated.

Methods, PDF pp.18–19, describe MBP–AP–strepII fusion purification constructs expressed in *E. coli* SM547(DE3), induction and separate purification. Exact host genotype beyond the named strain, a complete construct sequence, and an assay-specific tag-cleavage statement are not established by the selected evidence. Do not turn the expression-strain name into a proven endogenous-AP-null genotype. The same Methods report fresh amylose resin for each mutant and >95% purity estimated by stained SDS-PAGE. This is a protein-purity observation; it is not a measured active-enzyme fraction or an attribution percentage.

The endpoint in Table 1 is the catalytic-efficiency parameter kcat/KM for p-nitrophenyl phosphate hydrolysis, in M^-1 s^-1. Product formation is followed optically at 400 nm; initial rates at different enzyme and substrate concentrations supply the parameter. This is an enzyme-normalized kinetic parameter, not an organism phenotype, total protein yield, a raw optical amplitude, or a sample-to-blank ratio. The paper reports at least two enzyme concentrations and seven substrate concentrations, linear dependence on enzyme concentration, low-substrate and full-fit comparisons, and fit R²>0.98. Those concentrations and fit points are not independent enzyme preparations. R² is not assay specificity or a function probability.

The default assay uses 0.1 M MOPS, pH8, 0.5 M NaCl, 100 µM ZnCl2, 500 µM MgCl2, 25°C. **The complete Methods exception matters: MgCl2 is omitted for E322Y-containing variants.** Both selected constructs contain E322Y, so this exception applies on both sides. The table's blanket buffer footnote must not erase it. Their shared protocol supports a reported-protocol K328 substitution comparison. It does not establish equality of active fractions, metal occupancy in every preparation, protein-concentration calibration error or every unreported condition.

## Why this establishes a different sampling unit

Table 1, PDF p.5, D101A/E322Y row marked `#`, gives **3.1 (0.1) M^-1 s^-1**. The hash-mark footnote explicitly attributes that row's SD to activity determinations from **two independently expressed enzyme preparations**. Methods p.19 independently identify D101A/E322Y among the nine constructs re-expressed and re-characterized. This is evidence of physical preparation replication, rather than an inference from triplicate wells, figure labels, times, or assay repeats.

The central number is reported as the kinetic estimate. The table does not explicitly call it the arithmetic mean. The SD ownership and n=2 are explicit; equality of that central number to the equal-weight mean is an additional assumption for a mean confidence interval. The source does not publish individual preparation estimates or the complete technical-readings-to-fit-to-preparation hierarchy in these table/Methods records. Do not reconstruct individual values from mean and SD, invent preparation IDs, or infer a paired covariance matrix.

The within-paper contrast is unusually informative: **D101A/E322Y/K328A is 1.1 (0.1)** but lacks the `#` marker and is absent from the Methods' re-expression list. The generic footnote permits duplicate measurements using the same or independent preparations. Therefore the second row's preparation count and preparation-level SD ownership are **not established**. Two independent kinetic measurements, described in Methods, must not be promoted to two independently expressed preparations for this row. Its point estimate and reported SD remain usable source information.

### Explicit sums and multipliers

The scientific unit determines the denominator. If preparation estimates were available, each preparation would first contribute its estimate x_i; the equally weighted preparation mean would be sum(x_i)/n, and its sample variance would be sum((x_i−xbar)²)/(n−1). The source SD and n allow the conditional **s² × (1/n)** and **s × n^-1/2** calculations above without fabricating x_i. Technical points, concentrations, repeated figures and re-reading do not enlarge n.

The proposed precision-qualified lower endpoint is **x̂ − 12.7062 × 0.1 × 2^-1/2 = 2.20154 M^-1 s^-1** under the stated mean/normal model. This is a specific sum of measured magnitude and a precision term, not an overall assay grade or a probability that the functional assertion is true. It would be inappropriate to give s, n, SE and this lower endpoint four independent weights; these are alternative presentations of one uncertainty contribution. No such cell is silently installed by this research proposal.

For the K328 substitution contrast, D=x̂−ŷ and F=ŷ × x̂^-1; the decrease is 1−F. Preparing proteins independently within a row does not establish independence across rows or independence of shared calibration errors. A proper mean-difference uncertainty needs **Var(D)=Var(Xbar)+Var(Ybar)−2Cov(Xbar,Ybar)**. Here Var(Ybar) at the preparation level and the cross-construct covariance remain unknown. Do not substitute either zero or the technical variance for them. Independent physical preparations solve a specific uncertainty question; they do not establish every correction needed for a contrast.

Shared protein-amount calibration, optical response, buffer/metal stocks and instrument calibration can generate common systematic effects. The selected paper records do not supply a quantitative recovery, error covariance or active-fraction calibration. A multiplicative calibration contribution would remain common, rather than shrink as 1/sqrt(n). No recovery factor of one, zero covariance or assay false-positive/false-negative probability is assigned. The reported n/SD component remains useful partial evidence without those unavailable quantities.

## Comparability, effect isolation, validity and relevance remain different

**Comparability:** the selected two constructs share E322Y and thus the Mg-omission protocol, reported buffer, temperature and optical endpoint. Their deliberately changed variable is K328A. The record does not quantify a complete similarity score across every possible condition; it must not turn matched listed factors into an arbitrary percentage. Differences in active protein fraction, tag handling, preparation age or unreported calibration cannot be numerically corrected without measurements.

**Effect isolation:** separate purified constructs avoid a mixture of these two variants. Fresh purification resin, protein-purity estimates and repeat expression address different contamination alternatives. The extra S102G substitution in the **five-mutation minimal construct**, reported in Methods p.19, removes its nucleophile and produces activity below measured background, with a reported at-least-100-fold separation from the assayed AP-mutant activities. Preserve that bound and its actual construct. It is not a directly matched blank measurement for D101A/E322Y, and it supplies neither a numeric optical blank nor an exact contaminant fraction. No-enzyme controls also report no reaction, without numeric detection limits. Neither control becomes exact zero. Measuring a matched inactive version of the selected construct with quantitative background would more directly address contamination of this particular sample.

**Method validity:** the measured product signal, enzyme dependence and agreement of low-substrate and full-fit estimates address specific validity questions. Metal-dependent activation checks matter because E322Y changes metal-site behavior. The paper distinguishes the observed WT efficiency (3.3×10^7) from an author-estimated chemical-step-limited WT value (6.3×10^8); its fold-decrease column uses the latter. Our numerical example uses neither as an unqualified measured denominator. Do not recalculate an ordinary observed-WT fold ratio and call it the paper's chemical-step comparison. The generic table header also contains a single-mutant label E332Y where the Methods discuss E322Y; preserve the original and do not propagate that label inconsistency into selected unambiguous multi-mutant rows.

**Relation to the functional assertion:** pNPP hydrolysis and preparation-level repeatability support phosphomonoesterase activity of the studied construct under this assay. The K328 substitution comparison concerns a functional distinction within a mutant background. It does not establish a preferred physiological substrate, a native-organism role or specificity against all alternative reactions. Table 3 investigates methyl-phosphate behavior for other named variants; it is not an unreported matched alternative-substrate series for this D101A/E322Y row. Direct alternative-substrate measurements on the selected construct would address that separate specificity question. No target correspondence or A106+ expansion follows.
