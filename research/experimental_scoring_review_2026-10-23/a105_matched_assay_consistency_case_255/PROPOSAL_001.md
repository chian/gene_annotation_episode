The useful distinction is between **a numerical discrepancy in source reports** and **a discrepancy between valid estimates of one activity at common conditions**. Wang et al. supply the former and explicitly explain a readout weakness. ## Experimental definition

The studied molecule is the *Clostridium kluyveri* DSM555 NfnAB complex, nfnA CKL_0459 and nfnB CKL_0460, coexpressed in *E. coli* from source genomic DNA. NfnA has an N-terminal His6 tag and NfnB a C-terminal His6 tag in the successful coexpression/purification construct. Both subunits coexist and form the assayed complex; they do not replace one another. Separate-subunit experiments are ancillary controls and must not be treated as additional measurements of the coexpressed preparation's turnover. The source describes BL21(DE3) expression, Ni-NTA purification under anoxic conditions and FeS-cluster reconstitution. It does not supply residue boundaries, a per-assay preparation identifier, purity percentage or active-enzyme fraction for each compared estimate.

Table1 and the following results describe FeS-reconstituted recombinant complex whose NADPH:TTC specific activity was 8 U/mg. This is a characterization of the preparation used for the reported results; it is not a measured amount-normalization factor that should be multiplied into every other activity. Earlier preparations had different activities. Do not pair the earlier preparation range with Table1 as if independent matched replicates.

Basal activity assays use 37°C, 100mM MOPS-KOH pH7, 10mM 2-mercaptoethanol, 12µM FAD, 0.8ml liquid in stopped anaerobic 1ml cuvettes, with 0.2ml N2 or H2 gas at 1.2×10^5Pa. Both selected assays have an NADPH regeneration system. The ferredoxin/hydrogenase are purified from *C. pasteurianum* DSM525, not native *C. kluyveri* partners. That substitution is a functional context qualification, not a target-homology weight.

No fit errors, SD/SEM, raw saturation curves, per-fit counts, preparation covariance, optical path length or per-assay active amount are supplied for these estimates. Preserve all as unknown. The observed control rate without the required partner is not an instrument blank and cannot silently be subtracted from the full reaction as a separately additive contaminant.

The source reaction5 implies one NAD+ and one two-electron ferredoxin reduced per two NADPH oxidized in the backward direction. This supplies a **stoichiometric connecting model**, not proof that the two assay protocols estimate the same saturated rate. Their substrate/redox arrangements differ. The authors themselves identify an inaccurate NADH rate caused by overlapping ferredoxin absorbance. The functional assertion that the complex couples these reductions can remain supported while the numerical rate comparison stays qualified.

The signed optical model is ΔA380/(εNADH·l)=Δ[NADH]−(εFd/εNADH)Δ[Fdred] for the isolated contributions described by the source, with time, volume and protein normalization retained when converting to rates. Thus h=y+αf. Other changing chromophores and regeneration effects would require their own terms and controls. No numeric path length, raw A380 slope or actual f is invented. This equation explains why the net readout cannot be treated as NADH alone; it is not a universal correction model for every assay.

The author describes8 as about one-third of the expectation from stoichiometry. Our8/23 is a separately labelled arithmetic rendering of rounded source values. The stronger statement that the true rates are23 and8 at one set of common conditions is not established.

## Complementary experiments, controls and dependence

Fig3A/B independently vary the limiting nicotinamide input while measuring final H2 by GC in coupled NfnAB/ferredoxin/hydrogenase reactions. These are endpoint stoichiometry experiments, not rate replicates. The serum-bottle configuration is 0.8ml liquid, 5.7ml N2 headspace, 270µg NfnAB; serial0.1ml gas samples every2min are time points from the reaction, not independent preparations. The GC peak-area standard curve is reported, but calibration recovery, uncertainty and raw calibration observations are not provided.

From caption-native approximate slopes: Fig3A needs1.1µmol NAD+ per1µmol H2 (H2/NAD+≈0.9091); Fig3B needs2.2µmol NADPH per1µmol H2 (H2/NADPH≈0.4545). Dividing these two slopes gives an approximate2NADPH perNAD+ under the declared coupling/completion interpretation. This uses a sum/multiplier model with chemically meaningful coefficients; it is not a 90.9% validity score or independent confirmation of23U/mg. The two panels share the GC/H2 methodology and could share standards and protein; covariance and preparation overlap are unknown. A shared multiplicative H2 calibration could cancel from the ratio of slopes, while residual assay-specific biases would not.

Fig4 instead measures terminal **NADP+ reduced minus NADH oxidized** at380nm under H2/hydrogenase, with40µg NfnAB, in the opposite reaction direction. These data address stoichiometry under their own conditions; they are not a second380nm measurement of the backward8U/mg rate. Raw slopes cannot be compared with Fig3 or the Vmax values as identical endpoints. Captions and graphs are repeated representations of the same observations. The two28U/mg entries in Table1 describe the two necessary donors of the same forward coupled reaction and are not established independent repetitions merely because they occupy different rows. Do not infer duplication just from equal28 values either: retain the shared reaction/protocol link and unknown numerical fit custody.

The source says stoichiometric results were reproducible with different enzyme batches. That supports the qualitative claim of between-batch repetition for **stoichiometry**; the exact batch count, per-batch values and numerical dispersion remain unknown. It cannot supply n or SEM for the two Vmax estimates.

## Explicit sums/multipliers and a segregated illustration

For a genuinely common corrected flux x, use x_i=v_i/ν_i, retaining covariance from shared physical measurements. If correction includes h=y+αf, its first-order variance uses J=(1,α,f) over(y,f,α), including signed cross terms. Reusing that h in a consistency comparison does not create a second independent measurement.

**Synthetic only:** suppose two unbiased common-condition estimates are20 and22 in the same flux unit, with *known estimator* covariance C=[[4,1],[1,9]]. Then D=−2, Var(D)=4+9−2×1=11, z≈−0.603023 and z²≈0.363636. The latter two are alternate descriptions of this same discrepancy; neither is a p-value or function probability. Under an explicitly supported common-mean model, generalized least squares gives weights8/11 and3/11, combined mean226/11≈20.545455 and variance35/11≈3.181818. This precision-weighted sum is justified by that stipulated joint covariance/model, not normalized arbitrary quality grades. It is not calculated for the paper. Same-estimand agreement cannot detect a bias shared equally by both methods, and combining their estimates does not remove it.
