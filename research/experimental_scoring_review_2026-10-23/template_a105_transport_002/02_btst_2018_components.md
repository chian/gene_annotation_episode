# BtsT: transport activity, nuisance comparability and effect isolation

Source: Kristoficova et al., *BtsT, a Novel and Specific Pyruvate/H+ Symporter in Escherichia coli*, J Bacteriol 200:e00599-17, DOI [10.1128/JB.00599-17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5738728/). Main text and Fig. 5 image inspected; supplementary data were referenced in the paper but not opened in this review. All calculations below are proposals or arithmetic derived from explicitly reported quantities, not new paper measurements.

## Subjects, intended differences and mixtures

Whole cells: E. coli MG1655 ΔbtsT with pBAD24-btsT encoding BtsT-6His versus the same deletion strain with empty pBAD24. Native BtsT is absent; the plasmid protein is not deliberately mixed with WT BtsT. Other transporters and metabolism remain. Intended change: presence of BtsT. Both assay suspensions have 0.35 mg/mL total cellular protein, 10 µM pyruvate, pH 7.5 and 15°C (transport Methods). Total protein normalization is not BtsT normalization. Differences in active BtsT abundance, other transporters or metabolism induced by expression are not quantitatively resolved. Cells were assayed without added arabinose using leaky expression.

Purified-protein arm: BtsT-6His isolated from E. coli LMG194, reconstituted in E. coli polar-lipid vesicles at nominal lipid:protein 100:1, extruded through 400-nm pores. Assays use 40 µM [14C]pyruvate at 25°C. The same preparation is exposed to separately imposed ΔpH, ΔΨ, combined proton motive force, sodium conditions or no gradient. Here the energetic gradient is the intended variable. Empty liposomes address passive permeability (supplement Fig. S10, not digitized). Orientation and active fraction of reconstituted BtsT are not quantified in the inspected text.

Endpoints: filtration-retained radiolabel per mg protein versus time; fitted initial slope; concentration-response K_m. Neither retained radioactivity nor whole-cell endpoint alone resolves unchanged pyruvate from metabolites or surface-bound label.

## Combination proposal in the requested numerical sheet

Keep nuisance comparability distinct from isolation. A background subtraction answers what remains after measured background; a response correction answers whether measurements made under different non-target conditions estimate the same quantity.

Define `Jstd = (U − B) × cS × cT × cpH × cgradient / (r × a × o)`, where U and B have the same physical denominator and window; r is calibration/recovery, a the independently measured active protein fraction, o the correctly accessible orientation fraction, and the c terms are same-molecule response ratios bringing **nuisance** conditions to the declared reference. Exclude the intended variable from the c terms. Do not assume separability: if temperature and pH interact, replace cT×cpH with a joint measured response ratio. Each absent measurement stays unknown; an unmeasured fraction is not set to 1.

For this paper's Fig. 3 contrast, the known arithmetic is `U×0.8333≈U−B`; it does not fill r,a,o or an intrinsic-molecular-turnover denominator. For a claim restricted to extra uptake per mg cellular protein, a and o need not be invented to create a different quantity: preserve that denominator and scope. For the purified preparation, 27.7 is an observed slope per mg preparation protein, not an orientation-corrected kcat.

A proposed **bounded measurement component**, when the raw data support the operands, is the conservative resolved fraction of gross signal:

`Qresolved = max(0, [U−B−z*u_(U−B)−b_unresolved]/U)`.

This combines background isolation and precision by subtraction, not by arbitrary category weights. `b_unresolved` is an experimentally bounded non-transport contribution in slope units, not a guessed penalty; z is an explicitly selected uncertainty coverage quantile, not fitted from the paper's significance stars. Without that bound or SE the result is unknown. This is a measurement-quality component for the selected molecular inference, not an overall annotation score. A kinetic nuisance correction changes the slope and uncertainty together; applying another isolation penalty after this numerator would double count. The current source cannot fill Qresolved completely.

When repeat preparations measure the same standardized quantity, combine them as `Jbar = (1'Σ^−1 J)/(1'Σ^−1 1)`, with `Var(Jbar)=1/(1'Σ^−1 1)`. Shared empty controls and shared K_m/calibration terms must appear in Σ. Between-preparation inconsistency belongs in a measured heterogeneity variance, not a paper-count multiplier. This statistical combination is proposed, not adopted policy.

Source-to-target relevance requires an explicit response relation for whichever correspondence supports the target inference. The paper does not calibrate such a relation for the project's target; sequence identity alone must not fill it. The BtsT source activity can be supported without claiming a numerical transferred-target score. A final A105 contribution remains unresolved until the common sheet defines this interface and missing inputs are handled honestly.

## Weaknesses, complementary evidence and source issues

The reconstitution experiment removes most cellular metabolic routes and addresses isolation beyond the intact-cell contrast. It is complementary evidence, not an independent copy of the same intact-cell initial slope. Empty-vesicle quantitative slopes would estimate passive entry. Quenching/washing or entrapped-volume controls would address surface/trapped external label. Orientation measurements and active-protein quantification would support molecular turnover. Time-resolved gradient probes would test gradient loss, which can produce a falling accumulated endpoint despite initial uptake.

Three biological replicates are stated for Fig. 3; Fig. 5 explicitly says three **technical** replicates, while the general Methods claims all experiments were repeated with independent samples and error bars SEM. Preserve the panel-specific definitions and flag the conflict; do not promote Fig. 5 n=3 technical measurements to independent preparations or treat its SD as SEM.

The counterflow Results calls 0.28 a value with rate units, but Fig. 5C's y-axis is accumulated nmol/mg and its point at 1 min is approximately 0.28. Do not use 0.28 as an initial slope without resolving the mismatch from raw data. The sodium-gradient Results description also differs from Methods buffer E: Methods specifies pH 5.8 plus 50 mM NaCl. This does not isolate sodium from a proton gradient. These ambiguities remain explicit rather than silently corrected.
