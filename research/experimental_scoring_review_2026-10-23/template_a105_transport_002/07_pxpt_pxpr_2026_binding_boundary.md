# A105: PxpT transport remains unmeasured; PxpR binding is measured

Research proposal, 2026-10-23. Sundermeyer et al., *Growth of Corynebacterium glutamicum on 5-oxo-L-proline (pyroglutamate) as a carbon and nitrogen source requires the PxpR-controlled pxpTABC genes*, [AEM 92:e02181-25, DOI 10.1128/aem.02181-25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13274462/). ## Experimental definitions and A105/A106 boundary

C. glutamicum ATCC 13032 and in-frame ΔpxpT, ΔpxpABC, ΔpxpTABC and ΔpxpR derivatives were tested in separate cultures. Plasmid pPREx2 expresses the respective deleted genes for complementation. A WT and mutant protein are not necessarily coexpressed just because the paper compares those strains; the genotype/plasmid must be resolved for each culture. Catabolic PxpABC and other cellular proteins coexist with PxpT in wild-type cells. Changing pxpT, pxpABC, or pxpR is an intended genetic perturbation. Supplementing whole operons can change both transport and metabolism, while preculture adaptation and glucose change regulatory state.

Growth used CGXII with 12.9 g/L (100 mM) 5-OP as carbon/nitrogen source, or glucose/ammonium/urea controls. BioLector backscatter at 620 nm monitored growth at 30°C, 1,200 rpm, initial OD600=1. This is **not** a 5-OP uptake trace. No direct extracellular depletion, intracellular labeled accumulation, purified PxpT transport, flux, kinetic affinity, orientation, gradient-response measurement, or surface-binding subtraction is reported. PxpT has 436 residues, 11 predicted transmembrane helices, and database matches in a family commonly associated with divalent-metal transport; the authors explicitly do not exclude that alternative. The knockout/rescue/growth evidence stays A106. A105 transport cells are unknown rather than numerically inferred from growth rate, lag time, or sequence identity.

The same paper does contain direct A105 **binding** evidence on a different molecule: C. glutamicum PxpR with a C-terminal Strep-tag-II, expressed from pPREx6-pxpR-Strep in E. coli BL21(DE3), then purified by StrepTactin affinity and size-exclusion chromatography. This is purified PxpR, not PxpT, and no untagged WT PxpR is reported as a coassayed reference protein. Any contaminating proteins or inactive PxpR fractions remain quantitatively unresolved.

## Reported PxpR numerical evidence

ITC Methods: 40 µM nominal PxpR-Strep, 150 µM ligand, 40 mM HEPES pH7.4/100 mM NaCl, 25°C. Protein was dialyzed overnight; ligands were prepared in the same buffer and adjusted to pH7.4. An initial 0.4-µL injection was followed by 36 injections of 1 µL. Ligand-to-buffer titration controls were performed. Concentration used A280 with predicted extinction coefficient 22,920 M−1 cm−1. A 300-µL filling volume is reported; it must not be equated to the instrument's thermodynamically active cell volume without confirmation.

Results/Fig.6 and cited Table S1 report five determinations:

| Parameter | Reported value | Meaning/denominator |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| KD | 726 ± 23 nM | Equilibrium dissociation constant fitted to the heat isotherm |
| Binding stoichiometry n | 0.324 ± 0.004 | Moles ligand per nominal mole PxpR monomer, under a one-site-per-monomer model |
| ΔH | −98.7 ± 1.0 kJ/mol | Enthalpy per fitted binding event |
| −TΔS | 63.6 ± 1.1 kJ/mol | Derived entropic free-energy term |

The main text identifies these as five determinations but does not specify independent protein preparations or the precise ± statistic. Do not manufacture five biological preparations or a confidence interval. No heat traces for L-glutamate, L-aspartate, L-glutamine, or L-proline binding are shown; these are reported nondetections under the tested conditions, not KD=infinity.

## Conversion into the user's numerical components

**Experimental comparability.** Ligand identity is the intended variable in specificity comparisons. Exclude that identity difference from nuisance penalties. Buffer, pH, temperature, protein preparation, concentration, dilution heats and ligand chemical integrity are nuisance inputs. Matching buffer and pH addresses real heat artifacts, while measured ligand-to-buffer controls permit numerical correction `q_binding,i = q_protein+ligand,i − q_ligand+buffer,i`. Exact blank heat values and uncertainty are unavailable, so neither a background fraction nor perfect comparability is assumed. The apparent stoichiometry also depends on concentration calibration and competence; SDS-PAGE appearance is not a numerical purity or active-fraction assay.

**Isolation and active fraction.** If there is exactly one physical site per monomer, concentrations are accurate, all binding heat arises from PxpR and ligand is competent, then `f_active = n = 0.324` and `P_active = n × P_nominal = 0.324 × 40 = 12.96 µM`. Holding nominal concentration exact gives a propagated stated error 0.16 µM. These are **conditional derived quantities**, not independently measured active fraction. A different oligomeric stoichiometry, concentration error, partial ligand competence, or heterogeneous binding can also lower n. Therefore `1/n = 3.08642` must not be used as a universal correction to KD. The fit already estimates KD and n jointly; penalizing its measured affinity again by 0.324 would count the same amplitude issue as a separate confidence loss.

**Signal/identifiability component.** The dimensionless ITC concentration parameter is `c = n P_nominal/KD = 0.324×40/0.726 = 17.85124`, whereas omitting n gives `40/0.726 = 55.09642`. This describes the experiment's binding-transition regime and potential parameter identifiability, not annotation confidence. Converting c into a weight requires fit covariance or empirical recovery of KD under that design; no invented “good c” score is supplied. A proposed fractional KD precision cell is `u_KD/KD = 23/726 = 0.0316804`, retaining the unresolved error type. Fit covariance, concentration uncertainty, dilution-blank variance and between-preparation variation must be combined before interpreting this as total uncertainty.

**Response relation, rather than raw condition ratio.** Under the fitted independent-site model and specified **free** 5-OP concentration L, `θ_active(L)=L/(KD+L)`. For an explicitly proposed condition L_free=1 µM, `θ_active=1/(0.726+1)=0.5793743`; occupied sites per nominal monomer would be `nθ=0.1877173`. The 1-µM condition is an illustration, not measured intracellular concentration and not the ITC titrant concentration. If only total ligand is known, solve the ligand/protein mass balance instead of substituting L_total for L_free. Neither occupancy predicts PxpT flux or the degree of transcriptional derepression without another measured relationship.

**Thermodynamic consistency.** Calculated `ΔG = ΔH + (−TΔS) = −98.7 + 63.6 = −35.1 kJ/mol`; independently evaluating the algebraic relation `RT ln(KD/1 M)` at 298.15 K gives approximately −35.04 kJ/mol. This verifies compatible rounding and unit conventions. It is not independent evidence because −TΔS is itself derived from the fit's KD/ΔH. Adding “consistent thermodynamics” as a separate independent probability would double count the isotherm. Variance must include covariance: `u_ΔG² = u_ΔH² + u_(−TΔS)² + 2cov`.

**Specificity and nondetection.** No alternative-ligand detection limit, fitted enthalpy bound, or raw trace is provided. An ITC nondetection may mean weak binding, small/zero net binding enthalpy, or inadequate dynamic range. A numerical KD selectivity ratio `KD_alt/KD_5OP` or lower bound needs an actual alternative KD limit under an appropriate model, so it stays unknown. Four reported unshown nondetections do not multiply into four independent exclusion scores. Complementary binding readouts could resolve thermally silent alternatives; this review does not invent their outcomes.

**Explicit combination.** Correct individual injection heats using measured blanks; fit the mass-balance binding isotherm jointly for KD, n and ΔH with concentration/blank errors; propagate the joint covariance into the desired occupancy at a known free concentration. If genuinely independent preparations have a common log(KD), combine with generalized inverse-covariance weights rather than counting injections or derived ΔG as replications. Condition-response corrections and active-site amplitude enter once in that model. There is no justified product of this PxpR affinity with a PxpT growth ratio to form a transport score: the molecules and endpoints differ.
