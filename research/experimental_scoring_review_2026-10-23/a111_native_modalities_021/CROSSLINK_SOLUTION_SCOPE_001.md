# Bounded cross-link / solution-contact scope assessment

Goal: determine whether the measurement belongs in this same source-observation→mapped-target-feature chain without assigning a fictitious experimental value. This is a scope result, not a claim that a general XL-MS scoring method was completed.

A residue-localized contact observation can fit A111 when the experimental question, source molecules/constructs, state, endpoint identity and a chemically meaningful contact constraint are specified. The existing G then evaluates that exact spatial/stoichiometric constraint, and T evaluates its preservation in the actual target mapping. An assigned intermolecular NOE with native experimental distance bounds and known averaging uses the defined NMR path in this packet. A source-to-target structural alignment alone remains A102. Binding affinity is A105; selective mutation/restoration is A106; coexpression is A107. A measured contact cannot be relabeled as any of those readouts.

For a cross-link, a numerical upper distance must come from the actual stated linker/chemical model and its admitted conformational assumptions; it is not supplied by this document. Retain endpoint atoms, attachment chemistry, intra/intermolecular ambiguity, construct state, cross-link identity and experiment identity. A statement of captured proximity has a narrower meaning than native stable occupancy, affinity or catalytic function. Choosing a rigid distance bound or a physical population from an unspecified cross-linking yield would invent an observation.

PSM score, peptide-pair FDR, residue-pair FDR and a quality-control pass label are distinct inputs. A set-level FDR does not mathematically identify the error probability of a particular required endpoint pair. For example, two hypothetical links with errors [0,.10] and two with [.05,.05] share mean error .05 but differ for either particular link. Thus `D=1−set_FDR` is not justified as per-feature support. Multiple spectra of the same link also do not become multiple independent biological contacts.

If an experiment actually supplied a calibrated error probability p for this **fully localized contact**, an explicitly proposed conversion could be `D=1−p`, domain p∈[0,1]; p∈[p_L,p_U] gives D∈[1−p_U,1−p_L]. That equation is stated to distinguish an absent input from an undefined transformation. No such measured p was established here, no new method selector or production reader is declared, and no generic PSM score is treated as p. If only set-level FDR is available, the particular-contact D point remains null [0,1] under this proposal; the chain remains correspondingly bounded. This is not a zero score.

Purely synthetic operand illustration of that conditional arithmetic: q=.5, p=.08, G=1, T=3/4, C=1, I=.8 yields w=.5×.92×1×.75×1×.8=.276. The same row with p unmeasured has a null point and [0,.3] bounds. A one-cluster synthetic sheet would have P=.276, O=0, Z=A=.276. This is an algebraic feasibility example only, not a published XL-MS measurement or a third demonstrated native method. The .08 is explicitly invented; a 5% FDR report cannot fill it.
