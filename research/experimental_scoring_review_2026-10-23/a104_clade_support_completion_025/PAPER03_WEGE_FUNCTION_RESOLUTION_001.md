# Paper 03: measured ion-preference change without a new organismal lineage

[Wege et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-313X.2010.04288.x),
publisher full Results, Methods, Table 1 and figure/support captions inspected.

In Arabidopsis clca-2 knockout protoplasts, separately expressed AtCLCa WT and
P160S were compared by whole-vacuole patch clamp. They did not coexist. The
intended change was residue 160; same-batch protoplasts received equal plasmid
amounts, which does not measure equal transporter abundance. Small endogenous
currents remained in untransfected controls. Table 1 reports current ratios at
−77 mV: changing cytosolic 19.2 mM chloride to 19.2 mM nitrate, with 200 mM
vacuolar nitrate, gave WT 4.0±1.1 and P160S 0.90±0.09. The ratio-of-ratios is
4.4444 (derived, no uncertainty inferred without covariance/raw replicates).
Anion/proton coupling remained supported for both; ion preference changed.
Figure S2 is a sequence alignment, not a supplied organismal comparison.

**A104 conversion proposal:** these assays populate function-resolution and
construct-identity cells. “Anion/proton exchanger” is shared; an exclusive
nitrate-transport label would overstate the measurements. The mutant is not
a second natural lineage reference. Thus the natural reference count is one,
not two, and neither clade coverage k/n nor F, J, O or a final A104 score can
be filled from this comparison alone. A single engineered change demonstrates
functional sensitivity, not a natural evolutionary rate. Whole-plant rescue
is distinct A106 evidence, not an extra phylogenetic replicate.
