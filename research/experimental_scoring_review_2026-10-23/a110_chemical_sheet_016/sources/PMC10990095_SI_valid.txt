Synthesis of C4[18O]-Shikimate


The synthesis of C4[18O]-shikimate was performed in five steps, starting with commercial
shikimate (Figure S4). The general strategy was to protect all the reactive functions except the
alcohol at position 4, in order to label it selectively with 18O, and then to remove the protective
groups. The first step consisted in protecting the carboxylic acid by an esterification reaction.
This was carried out using methanol and p-toluenesulfonic acid (APTS), which acts as a
catalyst. In a second step, the two alcohol functions at positions 3 and 5 were selectively
protected with a silyl group. The tert-Butyldimethylsilyl group was chosen for its bulky nature
and stability. The synthesis was carried out according to a slightly modified procedure
previously published (43) by substituting tert-Butyldimethylsilyl chloride with tert-
Butyldimethylsilyl trifluoromethanesulfonate (TBDMSOTf), for its high reactivity towards
hindered alcohols. In a third step, the hydroxyl function at position 4 was oxidized to the
corresponding ketone, using the Dess-Martin Periodinane (DMP) reagent, known for its gentle
and selective conversion of alcohols into carbonyl compounds (44). In the fourth step, the
ketone was labelled with H218O in an acidic aqueous medium (CH3C18O2H) based on previously
published work (45). In the fifth and final step, the selective reduction of the labelled ketone
was achieved with NaBH4, leading exclusively to the desired diastereomer. Finally, all the
protecting groups were removed in the presence of APTS and H2O to give the C4[18O]-
Shikimate.
All solvents and reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used without further
purification. Dry THF was purchased under argon from Sigma-Aldrich. Reactions were
monitored by thin layer chromatography (TLC) using precoated silica gel plates from
Macherey-Nagel (DC-Fertigfolie POLYGRAM SIL G/UV254) followed by phosphomolybdic
acid and heat as developing agents. Centrifugation was performed on Eppendorf Centrifuge
5424 R.

Synthesis of methyl (3R,4S,5R)-3,4,5-trihydroxycyclohex-1-ene-1-carboxylate (1):
Shikimate (1g, 5.7 mmol, 1 equiv) and methanol (25 mL) were added to a 100 mL round bottom
flask equipped with a stir bar and a reflux condenser. The mixture was heated to reflux and
stirred to ensure dissolution of shikimate. After complete dissolution, p-toluenesulfonic acid
(APTS) (50mg, 0.26 mmol) was introduced as a catalyst (cat) and was heated to reflux for 2
days, after which 1H NMR analysis of a reaction aliquot indicated complete conversion.
Therefore, the mixture was allowed to cool to room temperature then the solvent was
evaporated under reduced pressure and the residue was taken up in a minimal amount of
methanol. The product was purified by pouring the reaction mixture drop by drop into excess
pentane under rapid stirring and the precipitate filtered off using a Büchner vacuum filtration
funnel to afford the expected product shikimate ester as a white powder (833 mg, 4.4 mmol,
77%).

A 250 mL round bottom flask equipped with reflux condenser and stir bar was charged with
dichloromethane (50 mL) and compound 1 (833 mg, 4.4 mmol, 1 equiv). The solution was
stirred and heated under reflux for 2 hours to dissolve the shikimate ester. After being refluxed,
the mixture was allowed to cool to room temperature then in an ice bath. The stirring was
continued in ice. 2,6-lutidine (1.28 mL, 11 mmol, 2.5 equiv) was added to the solution and

the reaction mixture was stirred overnight at room temperature. TLC analysis showed a large
proportion of desired double protected product (low Rf) and low level of triple protected product
(high Rf). The crude mixture was purified by column chromatography over silica gel using
dichloromethane as eluent to give 1.2 g (2.9 mmol, 65 %) of product 2 as a colourless oil. The

Synthesis of methyl (3R,5R)-3,5-bis((tert-butyldimethylsilyl)oxy)-4-[18O]oxocyclohex-1-ene-1-
carboxylate (4):
The previously obtained ketone 3 (100mg, 0.24 mmol, 1 equiv) was suspended in dry THF (0.8
mL). Then, H218O (0,4 mL) and acetic acid-18O2 (5 µL, 0.09 mmol), as a catalyst, were added
and the resulting mixture was stirred for 3 days at room temperature. The solvent was removed
by rotary evaporation. To avoid 18O/16O exchange during purification and to prevent the loss of
oxygen-18 labelling, the crude compound 4 was used without further purification in the
following steps.


Synthesis   of     C4[18O]   shikimate,   (3R,4S,5R)-3,4[18O],5-trihydroxy-4-cyclohex-1-ene-1-
carboxylic acid (5):
To a solution of 18O labelled ketone 4 (100mg, 0.24 mmol, 1 equiv) from the previous step in
THF (900 µL) and H2O (100 µL), was introduced NaBH4 (480 µL, 0.24 mmol, 1equiv). The
reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 1 hour. The solvent was evaporated and
the residue was suspended in H20 (2 mL) and THF (2 mL). To this solution was added APTS
(10 mg, 0.05 mmol) as a catalyst. The resulting mixture was stirred at 70°C for 4 days. The
crude was purified by HPLC (the expected product is eluted at 13.6 min) to obtain the final
product as a white solid (4.25 mg, 0.024 mmol). The yield for the last two steps is 10 %.
The 13C NMR spectrum of compound 5 shows two chemical shifts (71.30 and 71.32 ppm) of
the carbon at position C4, corresponding to C4[18O]-shikimate and C4[16O]-shikimate
respectively (superimposition Fig. S13 and S14). It has been reported that the chemical shift of
a carbon atom carrying an oxygen isotope can be slightly modified (46, 47).
