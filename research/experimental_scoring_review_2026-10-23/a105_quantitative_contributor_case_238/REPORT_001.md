# A105 quantitative contributor correction: Kaster et al. 2011

Research conversion of one retained case, `chem_mvh_hdr`, after a complete independent post-paper reset. Original paper: [Kaster et al., PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1016761108](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041090/). This is a source-grounded numerical proposal, not a new implemented grader or a claim of A105 adequacy. The permanent user goal and authority files are unchanged.

The useful contrast with the preceding HFP contamination case is a quantitatively represented correction to the observed reporter conversion, together with a separately reported numerical bound on an alternative substrate route. Neither should be overstated as a separately measured, matched contaminant-enzyme rate. In particular, this paper does not complete an unconditional molecular `q−g` example.

## Actual experimental arrangement

The studied material is native MvhADG/HdrABC enzyme complex purified from *Methanothermobacter marburgensis* DSM 2133. No engineered expression construct or replacement genotype is established. The six complex subunits coexist. The ferredoxin partner is purified from *Clostridium pasteurianum*; its heterologous origin is retained rather than treated as the native partner. The alternative MTZ readout uses metronidazole, an artificial electron acceptor, rather than a product-identifying assay for one isolated subunit. Evidence about complex activity does not isolate the proposed flavin-bifurcating site to HdrA.

The relevant Fig.4 experiment contains approximately 0.1 unit complex, 90 nmol MTZ and 72 nmol CoM-S-S-CoB in 0.75 mL, 1.6 M potassium phosphate pH 7, at 60°C, with 100% H2; the Methods describe sealed anaerobic UV cuvettes and H2 at 1.2 bar. Reduction is followed at 320 nm. The stated extinction coefficient is 9,300 M^-1 cm^-1. The source does not supply an explicit optical path length for this particular trace, so this packet does not convert the ordinate into a newly measured number of moles.

The intended variable in Fig.4B is limiting CoM-S-S-CoB amount. Contributors remain together: coupled enzymatic electron transfer; thiols, DTT and FAD from the enzyme stock; products that can regenerate CoM-S-S-CoB while reducing MTZ; and possible direct MTZ reduction by the reduced enzyme at potentials unsuitable for ferredoxin reduction. This is not replacement of one purified protein by another. Fig.4A is a single displayed time trace; physical preparation count and experimental covariance are not reported for that trace. A plotted series of points does not supply biological replication.

## Numerical contribution to the user's sheet

The paper explicitly subtracts a late, slow continuing component by extrapolation in Fig.4A and reports an approximately 0.6 MTZ/CoM-S-S-CoB molar ratio after that correction in Fig.4B. The displayed dashed line supplies the source's correction model rather than an independent no-enzyme control. Its validity requires that the extrapolated late component describes the additional contribution during the earlier reaction and that the fast component has effectively completed in the chosen late window. Chemical regeneration and substrate dependence make those scientific assumptions; a straight line does not prove them.

An additive **endpoint** decomposition is appropriate to evaluate here, rather than inserting an endpoint amount into the existing initial-rate `g` slot. For plotted ordinate A, initial value A0 at enzyme addition t0, later value AT, and extrapolated late baseline B(t)=alpha+beta*t:

`D_total=A0−AT; D_fast=A0−B(t0); D_tail=B(t0)−AT; D_total=D_fast+D_tail`.

The retained-signal multiplier is `F_tail=D_fast/D_total`. It evaluates how much of this selected observed reporter amplitude remains after this particular extrapolation. It multiplies the uncorrected amplitude, not an overall reliability or function probability. The complementary fraction is `1−F_tail`; it is not another independent score.

The small original raster permits a qualified demonstration. Reading original image coordinates gives initial trace y=39 pixels, the dashed intercept near enzyme addition y≈170, and the late trace at x=190, y≈181. Because the ordinate is affine in y, its unknown offset and scale cancel in the ratio: `F_tail=(170−39)/(181−39)=131/142≈0.9225`, with a complementary approximate contribution 0.0775. A deliberately retained reading enclosure y0 in [38,40], yB in [168,172], yT in [180,183] produces F in [128/143,134/142], approximately [0.8951,0.9437]. These are manual raster-reading enclosures, **not** experimental confidence intervals, replicate uncertainty or a probability of correct attribution. They do not estimate the uncertainty of the source's published 0.6 slope. The enclosure is conditional on the displayed baseline interpretation. No time rate, fitted kinetic parameter or new absolute molar result is extracted from these pixels.

Separately, the paper reports activity below 2 nmol/min in a 0.75-mL assay when the two homodisulfides coexist below 1 mM. Amount-rate divided by volume gives a strict concentration-rate threshold `<8/3 µM/min`. This is a measured/bounded alternative route, not merely a named contaminant. It must retain the substrate regime and endpoint ambiguity of the source paragraph. It is not automatically the `g` for the Fig.4 MTZ experiment: the endpoint and sample linkage require support, and substrate regeneration/synproportionation do not establish independent additive contributions when CoM-S-S-CoB is supplied. This bound therefore remains a native control observation. No matching q is invented, and this packet gives no numerical isolated-rate interval for Fig.4.

## Functional resolution, validity and complementary evidence

The source's corrected MTZ ratio, approximately 0.6, remains different from the idealized 0.5 four-electron-reporter ratio. Relative excess is approximately `0.6/0.5−1=0.2`, but that number describes a discrepancy from a named stoichiometric model; it does not identify which contributor caused it. Using the stated electron counts gives `H2/CoM-S-S-CoB=1+2r`, approximately 2.2 at r≈0.6. The source's condition-dependent 0.5–0.7 range maps to 2–2.4. This is an electron-account calculation, not a new H2 measurement or an error bar.

The ferredoxin experiment supplies a complementary endpoint and a lower-potential acceptor. The source reports near-one Fd/CoM-S-S-CoB coupling and notes uncertainty in confirming complete disulfide reduction at the necessary low concentrations. Fig.3 uses 40°C, whereas Fig.4 uses 60°C. These observations are not interchangeable same-condition repeats and do not justify averaging their numerical ratios. Viologen carryover can bypass CoM-S-S-CoB dependence, so cofactor dependence is a particular assay validity issue; the source's approximately 25 µM BV observation is not an error-rate estimate. The primary text is read; supplement Fig.S3 itself is not claimed inspected because the supplement fetch failed.

Additional purification, source MS identification and alternative reporter chemistry address different weaknesses. They do not measure every background contribution or establish statistical independence. The tail estimate and corrected amplitude reuse the same physical trace. Fig.4B's approximately 0.6 is an already-corrected result; applying F_tail to it again would repeat the correction.

## Current implementation and precise gap

The inspected assay reader already accepts source-bound q and g point operands, and the within-assay compiler already calculates q−g with calibration, amount and nuisance-condition terms. Its bounded/approximate operand guards explicitly withhold unsupported point compilation. The existing table-bound calculator preserves strict interval endpoints, but only for its defined v1 table extraction; this paper's prose bound and displayed endpoint extrapolation are not proved bound to that path.

This packet proposes fields for endpoint decomposition, selected late-baseline provenance, baseline applicability, shared-trace dependence and correction stage. It creates no production reader. A source-specific graph reading and deterministic ratio demonstration are present here, but installed selected execution, persistence, typed delivery and reconstruction for an endpoint-tail correction remain unverified. Any implementation should reuse suitable source readers and interval machinery and add only the missing scientific representation/calculation. A complete common reliability/validity/relevance compilation remains unfinished. Target transfer, W05 and final annotation credit remain outside this work.
