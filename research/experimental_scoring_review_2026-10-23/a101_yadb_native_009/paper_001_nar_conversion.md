## Definitions before scoring

The protein being investigated in this case is E. coli YadB. The sequence comparator is the catalytic core of canonical glutamyl-tRNA synthetase, GluRS. The paper reports 34% protein identity and absence of GluRS's anticodon-binding domain in YadB; it attributes this comparison to its reference 13, Campanacci et al. (JMB 337:273–283). It does not reproduce a protein sequence alignment or its parameters. Its docking reference is **T. thermophilus** GluRS, PDB 1N78; that is not sufficient to identify the exact comparator or denominator behind the reported protein-identity percentage.

This is a natural protein-to-protein comparison, not a mutant-versus-WT perturbation. No supplied target FASTA, target/reference sequence length, exact protein accession/version, aligned span, alignment method, score matrix, gap scheme, raw score, bit score, E-value, or protein residue correspondence is provided by this case assignment or reproduced in this article. There is no experimental condition matching coefficient to derive for that sequence comparison.

The paper also compares **RNA** regions: E. coli tRNA-Asp anticodon stem/loop with tRNA-Glu acceptor stem. Figure 4 maps base pairs (Asp 27:43, 28:42, 29:41, 30:40) to (Glu 4:69, 3:70, 2:71, 1:72), and Asp G39/C38 to Glu G73/C74. These are ten nucleotide positions, not ten protein residues. The comparison covers selected RNA regions rather than either complete RNA. Its matching residues cannot populate a YadB-to-GluRS protein identity, protein coverage, or a protein motif score.

Experimental facts establish what the characterized YadB does. Purified E. coli YadB and AspRS were tested separately and, in Figure 1, together against purified tRNA-Asp. The Q34-versus-G34 experiment used native tRNA and tRNA from a `tgt−` strain; Methods names SJ1505 for the `tgt−` strain but does not give all preparation genotype/construct details. Nucleotide TLC supported a Q34-only modification difference in that comparison. Standard reactions used the same stated buffer and temperature, with radiolabeled amino acid incorporation measured; exact enzyme amounts are described only as appropriate concentrations. These comparability and effect-isolation observations belong to the experimental chain; they are not new A101 sequence measurements.

Proposed future arithmetic for a **protein** alignment, without adoption: retain the native score `B` and define observed identity density `Dq = I/Uq` and target coverage `Cq = Uq/Lq`, so `Dq × Cq = I/Lq`. Similarly `Dr × Cr = I/Lr`. Here `I` is the number of exact amino-acid matches, `Uq` and `Ur` count unique represented nongap residues, and lengths refer to the selected complete input sequences. These products describe exact-match representation across each complete molecule. They do not estimate function probability, and must not be added to their own operands as independent evidence. A discontinuous alignment must use a union of positions, not a sum of overlapping spans. A sequence fragment needs a separately declared fragment denominator and cannot silently stand in for a complete target.

This paper cannot numerically fill those protein products: it supplies neither the integer counts nor input lengths and does not specify whether its 34% uses alignment columns, nongap pairs, or a domain-length denominator. Thus the bounded values of the unfilled density/coverage products are only the trivial **[0,1]**; unknown is not 0, 0.34, or 1. It would be unjustified to multiply 0.34 by an invented domain-retention fraction. The RNA calculation above demonstrates arithmetic on actual mapped positions, while being explicitly excluded from the protein row. Native-score calibration, how score and coverage combine into A101 annotation contribution, and how domain-specific evidence affects distinct functional claims remain unresolved for the parent sheet.

## What function can actually transfer?

Canonical GluRS activates glutamate and transfers it to a tRNA's terminal acceptor end. This paper characterizes E. coli YadB as glutamylating Q34 in the **tRNA-Asp anticodon**, rather than supporting ordinary translation by charging a canonical terminal acceptor. The paper's direct experiments remove the tRNA 3′ terminus, oxidize/protect acceptor groups, and compare Q34/G34 substrates. Table 1 reports YadB charging of native E. coli Q34 tRNA-Asp at **93%**, with `n.c.` for the other six substrate columns. `n.c.` has no numerical detection limit here and must not become exact zero. Figure 3B reports **Km = 0.19 µM** for Q34 substrate and **Ki = 0.19 µM** for G34 competitor; a Km and an inhibition constant are not automatically two equilibrium binding constants. The figure caption mislabels the Ki species as Q34, while the Methods, Results and experimental setup identify G34. Preserve that internal source discrepancy.

These assay observations ground the identity and functional limits of the characterized reference. They may populate A105/A106 as appropriate in separate work; the values **93%, 0.19 µM** do not become A101 score components. GluRS-like catalytic-core homology and a different tRNA-end/substrate specificity coexist. The supported sequence-level relationship therefore must stay separate from the wrong full-function transfer “canonical glutamyl-tRNA synthetase.” Absence of the anticodon-binding domain alone does not prove the novel Q34 function; the experimental comparisons supplied that discrimination.

The paper's manual docking uses PDB 1NZJ, 1EFW, 1N78, models unresolved YadB residues 224–238, and moves tRNA using Turbo-Frodo. The **15-residue** modeled loop length (`238−224+1`) and **1100 Å² versus 1600 Å²** interface areas are structural/model information. They are not protein alignment spans, similarity scores, or directly observed YadB–tRNA contacts. The proposed RNA mimicry likewise does not experimentally establish every named recognition nucleotide as necessary; targeted changes would address that limitation.

Native parser calculations then fill coverage and density products. No reader or prompt was implemented here. A function hypothesis need not pre-exist; the target can begin with supplied sequence/file/snippet, and the reference's experimentally characterized function can emerge during investigation.

Identity, similarity, raw score, bit score, E-value, coverage and residue retention from the **same alignment** are coupled descriptions, not independent experiments. Repeated Abstract/Introduction statements of 34% are one comparison. The 6/6 RNA window is within the 10/10 comparison. Multiple figures about the same purified materials do not establish independent preparations. A citation to an earlier protein comparison is not a fresh alignment observation. Discordance between homology and full-function transfer is retained as a limitation; it does not negate real family correspondence or generate an opposing protein alignment that was never measured.
