SCOPE: THE TEN RETAINED AXES

A101 — Sequence homology.
Literature can establish experimentally characterized reference functions, relevant residues, functional distinctions and reported comparison measurements. New sequence analyses belong to the tool-grader session.

A102 — Structural homology.
Literature can supply characterized structural comparisons and the molecular features that make a comparison informative. Keep reported comparison measurements separate from functional interpretation and from new structural calculations.

A103 — Genomic arrangement/neighborhood.
Extract relevant gene arrangements, experimentally established relationships, organism/strain context and reported quantitative neighborhood evidence. Do not convert a verbal statement of proximity into an invented association score.

A104 — Evolutionary history/phylogeny.
The user’s definition concerns agreement between the protein-family phylogeny and the organismal reference tree, together with placement within a clade of conserved function. Preserve characterized-function coverage, mixed-function clades, rapidly changing specificity, outliers and horizontal-transfer limitations. Frequent horizontal transfer can make broad organismal-tree correspondence uninformative; very local relationships may still be useful. Do not replace this with generic “evolutionary support.”

A105 — Experimental molecular activity.
Extract direct molecular activity and specificity evidence, including evidence measured on relevant homologs. Keep perturbation/restoration evidence in A106. Distinguish what the assay measured from what function that measurement establishes.

A106 — Perturbation and restoration.
Extract the actual perturbation, reference and restoration conditions, measured effects, controls, expression/activity amounts, experimental comparability and effect isolation. Relevant homolog experiments are included with an explicit correspondence to the target.

A107 — Required partner presence and coexpression.
Extract which partner molecules are required and the evidence defining that requirement. Preserve genomic presence measurements and coexpression statistics as separate quantities. A statement that a protein belongs to a complex does not establish that all required partners exist in the target system.

A108 — Organism and sample plausibility.
Extract the organism/sample conditions and requirements that make a proposed function plausible or implausible. Use measurable conditions where available. Interpretive judgments must address specific defined questions, not produce a blanket plausibility grade.

A110 — Biochemical/pathway consistency.
Retain this as a separate axis. Extract reaction, pathway, substrate/product, resource and conservation evidence needed by its existing sheet. Preserve the distinction between a measured biochemical outcome and assumptions about the rest of the pathway.

A111 — Experimental structural evidence and correspondence.
Extract what an experimental structure establishes about the characterized molecule and functional feature. Preserve the chain from that molecule to the annotation target through actual sequence, structural or other justified correspondence. Evidence on another protein is not a direct experiment on the target.

A109 is removed. Do not restore it. Do not merge A110 into A108.

Literature can populate relevant parts of every retained axis. Do not divide the axes into “literature axes” and “tool axes.” The distinction concerns how particular cells obtain their information.

THREE SEPARATE RESPONSIBILITIES

1. Investigation prompt.
This tells the episode what to investigate, what distinctions matter and what decisions it can make.

2. Numerical scoring sheet.
This contains actual numerical inputs and components that can contribute to annotation scoring. Units, denominators, uncertainty, missingness and relationships must be defined. A table of claims, citations and conditions is supporting recordkeeping, not a scoring sheet.

3. Extractors, graders and deterministic calculators.
These populate the specific cells in item 2. Design them from those cells backward. Do not build a generic literature summarizer and later try to reinterpret its output as a score.

Keep these responsibilities separate in code and presentation. A JSON file containing prose rules is not a substitute for executable calculations. Do not paste the complete scoring sheet or calculation implementation into an investigation prompt.

NUMERICAL AND SCIENTIFIC REQUIREMENTS

Use reported measurements and existing numerical scores wherever available.

For each numerical cell, retain:
- the measured quantity and actual subject;
- value and unit;
- numerator, denominator and normalization basis where applicable;
- conditions and endpoint;
- uncertainty type, magnitude and sampling basis;
- whether it is reported, graph-derived, calculated, proposed or synthetic;
- source location and calculation dependencies;
- explicit unavailable, conflicting, censored or inapplicable status.

Do not treat an unreported value as zero or one. Do not treat nondetection as an exact zero. Do not turn SD, SEM, confidence limits, detection limits and mathematical bounds into interchangeable quantities.

Keep the underlying measurements separate from score transformations. A reported ratio is not automatically an evidence score.

Implement component calculations as ordinary deterministic functions. Their operands, transformations, sums and multipliers must be explicit and testable. Preserve the existing proposals’ status; implementing a named proposed calculation does not silently adopt it as the default annotation policy.

Do not substitute “low/medium/high,” “weak/moderate/strong,” or similar categories encoded as numbers for actual component calculations. Finite categories may describe experimental arrangements or missingness; that does not make them numerical evidence.

Do not produce one intuitive “experimental reliability,” “relevance” or “transfer” number and call the task complete. Those quantities must be assembled from their defined components.

Where applicable, distinguish:
- experimental comparability: similarities and differences outside the variable deliberately tested;
- effect isolation: how specifically the observed outcome belongs to the intended molecule or change;
- relevance of the measured endpoint to the functional inference;
- validity and limitations of the experimental method;
- signal strength;
- consistency and dependence across observations;
- uncertainty;
- correspondence between the assayed subject and the annotation target;
- correspondence between the experimental system and the target system.

These are questions to resolve through the existing sheet, not permission to invent another generic list of score categories.

Establish the experimental arrangement before interpreting a number. For example, reduced abundance has different implications if a mutant replaces wild type versus being present alongside wild type. The mixture also creates a separate attribution problem. Do not assign the same generic penalty to both arrangements.

Evidence transfer is not restricted to sequence homology. Preserve whichever measured sequence, structure, functional-site, evolutionary, contextual or experimentally demonstrated correspondence actually justifies the inference.

Keep the finding on the assayed molecule separate from the relationship that makes it informative about the target.

WHEN INTERPRETATION REQUIRES A MODEL

Use deterministic parsing, unit conversion, validation and arithmetic whenever possible.

When prose requires interpretation, give the existing literature episode a predefined extraction/judgment prompt matched to the exact requested aspect and output fields.

The prompt must define:
- the concrete question;
- which source material must support the answer;
- the relevant experimental distinctions;
- allowed output meanings;
- what makes an answer unknown or conflicting;
- which sheet cells the answer populates.

The model may interpret an experiment or identify the appropriate measured operands. It must not invent missing measurements, choose an unexplained overall score or perform arithmetic that belongs in a calculator.

Where no applicable quantitative measurement exists and a numerical judgment is genuinely necessary, make the specific question, criteria and numerical meaning explicit for user review. That exception must not replace available measured components.

Validate extracted relationships as well as JSON syntax. A syntactically valid value attached to the wrong protein, condition, endpoint, table header or denominator is wrong.

Do not add a second model call merely to restate recorded facts or compose a parent report.

SOURCE IDENTITY, DEPENDENCE AND NEGATIVE FINDINGS

A paper, its supplement, a database entry and a later review can represent the same original experiment. Do not count those as independent evidence.

Do not infer shared experiment identity merely because two numbers match.

Distinguish biological replication, technical repetition, shared controls, reused observations, complementary endpoints and genuinely independent findings.

Preserve contradictory evidence and qualifications. Failure to find an experiment is not evidence that the experiment produced a negative result.

Preserve source-to-target correspondence and exact measurement identity across return, persistence and reconstruction. Do not preload source records from unrelated earlier runs.

Do not grade the reputation of a paper as a substitute for evaluating its actual experiment. Citation counts and publication counts are not automatically independent replication or evidence validity.
