# Two published cases investigated for organismal/physiological annotation support

The annotation-credit aim is to establish what a gene contributes to its actual organism under a stated condition. A useful outcome may narrow an existing role, correct a supposed requirement, or leave ecological significance unresolved despite demonstrated activity. These are known published cases. We inspected their evidence after publication; the questions connecting probes below reconstruct our present reasoning and are not an author's laboratory notebook or a blind discovery claim. All biological experiments described were performed by the papers' authors.

## TtrA/TtrRSBCA: the same respiratory capability has different contributions in intestinal and systemic sites

**Starting question.** Tetrathionate reduction was already a recognized Salmonella capability. What additional physiological annotation is justified if a systemic mouse model shows no virulence defect? Plausible explanations included an environmental role outside the host, an untested host niche, and a benefit masked by the wrong infection conditions.

**Actual first probe and observation.** We retrieved Winter et al.'s known 2010 primary article, switched from a blocked PMC page to its full primary XML, and read the Results, figure descriptions and Methods Summary. Its reported systemic-versus-intestinal contrast changed our question from “is this a virulence gene?” to “where and when is its respiratory resource available?”

**Two routes we actually followed in that article.** One route examined bacterial competition: ttrA disadvantaged bacteria in inflamed intestinal contents, not the systemic comparison; restoring the allele restored expansion. Removing the inflammation-inducing T3SS background removed the ttr-dependent benefit. The other route examined the resource: LC-MS detected tetrathionate in inflamed intestines when nonrespiring bacteria permitted accumulation. Cybb-deficient hosts lacked its production and the competitive benefit, whereas Nos2 deficiency retained a benefit. These complementary observations favor a host-oxidant-created respiratory niche. They do not make every Salmonella infection tetrathionate-dependent. [Winter et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2946174/)

**Changed next question: does that phenotype identify the selected molecular process?** We checked the older genetic characterization. Transferring ttrRSBCA into E. coli enabled tetrathionate-dependent anaerobic growth; ttrBCA without its regulatory system was insufficient in that recipient. This links the fitness perturbation to a defined respiratory capability rather than assigning catalysis from the infection phenotype. Subunit/cofactor assignments partly came from sequence comparison. [Hensel et al., 1999](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1365-2958.1999.01345.x)

**Changed next question: is the advantage simply more of a carbon nutrient?** We opened the ethanolamine follow-up and read its relevant complete Results. An eutC defect mattered in colitis but not the systemic model; ethanolamine growth benefited from tetrathionate respiration, and an eutC difference disappeared in a ttrA background. Measured colon ethanolamine did not rise enough to explain the condition dependence. This supports respiratory access to an existing nutrient. The follow-up adds a specific nutrient route, not a requirement that every Ttr contribution use ethanolamine. [Thiennimitr et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3198331/)

**Bounded annotation.** Ttr-dependent respiration contributes to Salmonella expansion in the tested inflamed intestinal niche. Its contribution differs across host sites despite the same encoded capability. Establishing this role required chemical, bacterial-genetic, host-genetic and animal experiments. We can inspect their records but cannot reproduce them here. Broader host/species extrapolation, exact in-vivo fluxes and all consumed carbon sources remain unresolved by this trace.

## XoxF: a missing environmental cofactor hid catalysis, and residual growth exposed another route

**Starting question.** XoxF resembles methanol dehydrogenase, yet earlier calcium-medium studies suggested weak catalysis and a regulatory role. Plausible routes were a different physiological substrate, a primarily regulatory function, or an assay/culture condition missing an essential factor.

**Actual probe and observation.** We opened Nakagawa et al.'s 2012 primary study and read its growth, purification, metal-analysis and mutant Results/Methods. Methanol growth with lanthanum in place of added calcium, plus restoration of methanol growth in a ΔmxaF strain, made “which factor enables alternative oxidation?” the next question. Succinate growth was retained across metal conditions, localizing the defect to the tested methanol-dependent physiology.

**Independent evidence routes followed.** Culture rescue alone could reflect regulation of some other protein. The purification route therefore mattered: the active protein's N terminus matched XoxF1; metal analysis found tightly bound lanthanum. Activity and protein identity supported a catalytic assignment beyond a growth association. The regulatory route used promoter reporters; that paper reported no lanthanum-dependent increase and interpreted activation mainly after transcription. That interpretation required separate verification. [Nakagawa et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3507691/)

**Changed next question: is XoxF the sole alternative, and was the regulatory inference robust?** We then inspected Vu et al.'s 2016 growth table/curves, null-mutant comparisons, reporter Results and methods. A ΔmxaF ΔxoxF1 ΔxoxF2 strain still grew slowly with lanthanum and retained low methanol-oxidizing activity. Thus XoxF was not the exclusive explanation. Larger promoter fusions showed reciprocal lanthanum-dependent xox/mxa expression, correcting the earlier reporter-based conclusion. The methods also reported variable trace-lanthanide contamination of glassware and a preparation that made growth/reporter measurements reproducible. These observations changed the next question to “which remaining protein supplies residual oxidation?” rather than declaring the unexpected growth background noise. [Vu et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4859578/)

**Actual follow-up probe.** We retrieved Good et al.'s primary ExaF study and read its mutant, rescue and purified-enzyme Results. Removing exaF from the triple mutant abolished the remaining methanol growth; complementation restored it. Loss of another candidate quinoprotein did not account for the growth. Purified lanthanum-associated ExaF supported oxidation, while kinetics favored ethanol over methanol. This resolves the residual phenotype through a second enzyme whose secondary activity can become consequential in a deletion background. [Good et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5075040/)

**Bounded annotation.** XoxF supports lanthanide-dependent methanol oxidation in AM1 under tested culture conditions and also participates in regulatory behavior. Calcium-only experiments cannot establish its complete physiological role. XoxF is not universally essential for methanol growth because other routes and supplied metals matter. This trace does not establish lanthanide availability or competitive contribution on natural leaves. Metal occupancy, activity, knockout combinations, expression and rescue are authors' experimental dependencies; no assay or mutant culture was run here.

## Actual read-only records and data availability

The biological observations above come from primary main-text Results, Methods, tables and figure descriptions, saved locally as research data. We did not invent cultures, LC-MS runs, kinetic fits or numerical curve reanalyses. The Ttr source's live sequence accession probe returned AJ224978.1 as a 12,121-bp LT2 genomic ttr/SPI2 record; it corroborates identity/context and is not a new function assay. The XoxF/ExaF sources supplied published curves, tables, methods and protein measurements; raw instrument files or numeric growth series were not reanalyzed.

As an additional collection check, the GcsR accession probe returned GSE76522: two wild-type and two deletion RNA-seq samples, linked to SRP068065. This verifies that underlying sequencing data are available; the differential-expression findings in cases.jsonl remain the authors' results.

Saved access: primary article XML/HTML and extracted text; Entrez summaries AJ224978-summary.json and GSE76522-summary.json. Retrieval used primary web pages, Europe PMC full-text access when available, and the installed Entrez script. The research-router and ncbi-entrez skills were read. No tool-library/design recommendations are proposed.
