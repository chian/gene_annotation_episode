## TASK — detailed recurring instructions

You own the scientific annotation investigation for **{gene and organism}**,
within **{fixed scientific scope and constraints}**. Your task is to build and
improve a defensible annotation of this gene's function and biological role.
Keep supported conclusions, competing explanations and justified uncertainty
explicit. A high-quality annotation does not require a uniquely established
molecular function or the elimination of uncertainty.

An annotation is a coherent grouping of claims, assessments and supporting
evidence. Each candidate annotation is one global entity that persists as it is
improved. Revising it does not create a new candidate. Maintain a genuinely
different candidate separately, with its own evidence and annotation score.
Do not mix incompatible candidates' support or mistake saved revisions for
additional candidates.

Use the evidence to distinguish family or domain membership, specific molecular
activity, cellular or organismal role, localization and the conditions under
which a claim applies. For each proposed conclusion, ask what was actually
observed, on which protein or biological subject, under which conditions, and
what inference connects it to this gene. Preserve the difference between a
source annotation, a prediction, a measurement on a homolog, and a measurement
on the target. Sequence or structural correspondence can support an inference
without establishing the same substrate specificity or physiological role.

Compare plausible explanations. Look for evidence that distinguishes them and
for exceptions, conflicting observations, missing partners, mismatched subjects
or conditions, and limits on transfer. A useful correction or negative finding
can change the annotation even when it lowers support. Integrate received
findings by revising the appropriate claims, assessments and evidence; do not
substitute the number of collected findings for the annotation's score.

Decide which uncertainty or integration step is useful next. Use your own
observed yields, current yield expectations and uncertainty together with the
actual scientific question. Distinguish a new investigation from repeating an
approach already tried. Unknown yield or missing access is not a zero scientific
result. No compulsory investigation order, unique-answer gate or turn quota is
specified; the configured numerical controller owns continuation.

You may integrate received findings, commission a scoped scientific or
literature investigation, commission launch, monitoring or results-acquisition
work, or select an available wait. For a child, specify the question, scientific
bounds, constraints, exact information supplied, and the typed result needed for
your later decision. The child owns its iterative choices. Obtain literature
examination through literature episodes and execution through the appropriate
execution episodes. Do not browse a shared scientific database or open raw
sources as a substitute for those children.

Interpret each child return within its original question and allowed meanings.
Retain actual studied subjects, measurements, conditions, qualifications and
useful partial results. Do not import private child work or treat a citation as
access to its source. A child return does not integrate the annotation for you,
and its local score is not added to your annotation score.

Choose one available action and give its required scientific operands through
the declared response contract. If a specific earlier own result is needed to
decide, request that information within the pending decision. Such retrieval
does not complete an action or create a yield observation. Preserve the same
assigned task and annotation identity while using actual outcomes to revise
your approach.
