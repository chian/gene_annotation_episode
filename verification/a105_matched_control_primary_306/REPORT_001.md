## Experimental definitions and paired extraction

The article Methods §§2.2, 2.3 and 2.6 identify P205 as an amidase from *Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus*, produced in *E. coli* BL21 and purified. S146A replaces catalytic serine 146 with alanine in the P205 sequence; mutagenesis was Sanger checked. Active P205 and S146A are **separate wells**, not coexisting proteins. The changed variable is the intended active-site mutation. The article describes S146A as a control for nonspecific effects such as sorption. The supplement's Methods pp. 1–2 describe 200 µM capecitabine within substrate submixture 7, 100 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, purified P205 approximately 190 nM, and measurements at 0, 6 and 24 h after quenching. Abiotic and S146A controls were “set up in the same way”; the exact mutant protein concentration is not separately printed. Parent-capecitabine abundance was quantified by UHPLC-HRMS/MS peak integration with matrix-matched calibration standards. Numerical calibration recovery and raw replicate identities were not available in the inspected sources.

## Conversion into the three-part A105 template

**Investigation instruction.** For a purified-enzyme parent-depletion claim, identify the active enzyme, same-background inactive mutant, no-protein arm, substrate and assay matrix, then extract same-endpoint numeric values and their raw normalization. Ask whether the mutant differs only in catalytic capacity under equal protein amount and whether a chemically identified product closes the reaction. Preserve the reported control contrast and all unresolved conditions.

**Numerical scoring-sheet component.** Populate three arm values and spreads as source measurements, and calculate one within-assay controlled-depletion component

\[
q_{\mathrm{P205-S146A}}=R_{\mathrm{P205}}-R_{\mathrm{S146A}}
=0.6880062978-0.0987152898=0.5892910080.
\]

This is an explicit sum of a positive active term and a negative matched-control term. Its numerical value measures excess *parent-compound disappearance* under the compared assay conditions. It is neither a standalone annotation credit nor a proven fraction converted by P205 catalysis. The alternative no-protein contrast is `R_P205−R_abiotic=0.7267493538`; do not sum or multiply these two contrasts as independent support because both reuse `R_P205`. A future product-coupling multiplier, such as independently measured stoichiometric product gained divided by controlled substrate lost, is **unknown** for P205 capecitabine. Consequently a combined enzyme-specific chemical-activity score or uncertainty-weighted credit cannot be populated from this packet; no arbitrary 0/1 or categorical surrogate is supplied. If a later sheet uses such a multiplier, it must define the analyte, stoichiometry, calibration, denominators and admissible range from the actual product experiment.

**Reader/calculator responsibility.** A targeted XLSX reader should select Sheet1 row 5 by analyte `Capecitabine`, submixture `Sub7`, and time `24`; bind the `Bio`, `inactive_enzyme`, `Ab`, `Stdev1–3`, and difference columns to their original workbook cells; verify `K5=B5−F5` and `L5=B5−D5` to declared tolerance; preserve workbook byte identity and each arm's physical-measurement identity if available. A calculator can perform subtraction and percent-point conversion. Interpretation must keep same-analyte/same-endpoint comparisons distinct from inferred chemical mechanism. The current A105 worksheet is a wrapper for supported section returns; the inspected rate-source extraction contract accepts rate units, so this dimensionless endpoint row is not established as a populated production A105 sheet by the present audit. No reader or production grader was implemented here.

## What the contrast does and does not establish

Comparability is relatively strong for the measured endpoint because the same capecitabine analyte, stated assay setup, time and UHPLC-HRMS/MS readout were used. Effect isolation is a separate question. The mutant and active enzyme are replacement constructs, but equality of protein concentration in the wells and equality of noncatalytic sorption/structural effects were not numerically demonstrated. The apparent difference might therefore include consequences of the mutation beyond catalysis. The abiotic arm helps identify a no-protein background but does not quantify those protein-dependent noncatalytic contributions.

The active-minus-mutant contrast is much larger than the displayed arm spreads, but those spreads cannot yield a source-supported standard error, confidence interval or significance test without replication units and covariance. `sqrt(Stdev1²+Stdev3²)` would assume independent arm measurements and still would be a spread of an assumed difference, not its standard error; it is not promoted to a paper-derived uncertainty cell. The negative abiotic removal and its large spread further caution against treating the fractions as exact mass balance.

The capecitabine screen measures loss of parent capecitabine. The article's separate HPLC/standard-supported capecitabine product demonstration concerns **P131**, a different enzyme (Methods §2.12 and Results §3.3); it does not validate a specific P205 product. A complementary P205 experiment would pair active P205 and equal-amount S146A/no-enzyme controls with an authentic-standard/co-injection or orthogonal calibrated measurement of the expected hydrolysis product, measuring substrate loss and product gain over the same time course. This would address product identity and mass balance; a separately repeated protein preparation would address preparation reproducibility. These would be complementary evidence, not extra copies of the present parent-depletion measurement.
