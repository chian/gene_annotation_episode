Determine whether the selected experimental estimates measure the same activity of the studied molecule under sufficiently comparable conditions to assess agreement. Start with the supplied source material and the actual measurements received; a candidate annotation is not required. Identify the organism, genotype, molecular construct, preparation, partners and experimental system of each estimate. Establish whether compared molecules coexist or replace one another. Preserve separate constructs, purification/reconstitution states and unreported preparation identities.

For each estimate, identify what was directly detected and what quantity was inferred. Retain the endpoint, its sign, units, denominator, time window, fit or averaging basis, and whether a reported value is rounded, approximate, bounded or censored. An identical unit does not establish an identical estimand. Saturation turnover, rate under fixed substrate conditions, terminal conversion, affinity and reporter response can have different meanings. If different detected species can be expressed as one reaction flux, identify the reaction direction, stoichiometric coefficients and experimental support for that conversion; do not silently divide by an assumed coefficient.

Assess comparability outside the intended change separately from effect isolation. Establish the intended difference and any additional changes in substrate concentration, partner abundance, redox state, regeneration system, temperature, pH, enzyme preparation or denominator. Determine whether the protocols support an estimate of one common-condition quantity, merely a comparison of source protocols, or complementary information. Do not treat a change required by a detection method as harmless without examining its biochemical consequences.

Examine each method’s validity for the particular endpoint. Identify interfering signals, incomplete reporter coupling, calibration and recovery measurements, controls, detection limits and unresolved error mechanisms. A positive control or a required-partner omission can establish activity dependence without estimating an additive background. Distinguish random variability from an identified systematic bias. A weak or biased quantitative estimate may retain useful evidence for existence or partner dependence. Do not upgrade or discard all assertions about the molecule together.

Identify the physical measurements underlying each estimate and comparison. Separate repeated representations, repeated time points, technical replicates, paired measurements and independent preparations. Different assay methods may share protein, standards or upstream corrections. Preserve what is known about shared inputs and covariance; different identifiers or different rows are not evidence of independence. Retain uncertainty labels, estimator meanings and sample hierarchy. A source SD, an unspecified error bar or an uncounted claim of repeated batches is not a known covariance of the selected estimates.

Select supported source spans and answer the declared aspect-specific relationship questions. Ask deterministic readers and calculators for the requested native values and eligible numerical comparisons. Preserve descriptive protocol comparisons separately from common-condition consistency results, and separately from conditional explanations of a discrepancy. Mark unknown and conflicting relationships explicitly. Do not infer a p-value, probability of function, universal reliability grade, target-transfer weight or annotation credit. A comparison, its ratio and its log are representations of one contribution; no additional evidence is created by calculating or reporting them.
