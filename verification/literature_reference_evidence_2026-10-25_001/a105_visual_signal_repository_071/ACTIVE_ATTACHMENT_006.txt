# Goal: Implement literature evaluation of experimental reference evidence

Build and verify a literature workflow that examines experimental papers, extracts the measurements and experimental relationships supporting reference annotations, and populates the appropriate numerical evidence components.

Develop literature extraction fields and scoring-sheet definitions together through concrete annotation examples. Revise the existing sheet proposals when experimental cases expose missing distinctions or inappropriate calculations.

Deliver executable readers, aspect-specific interpretation instructions, deterministic calculators, populated examples and integration through the existing LiteratureEpisodes.

## Scope

The larger annotation system retains ten axes: A101–A108, A110 and A111. This goal does not build ten literature graders.

The principal literature responsibilities are:

| Area | Literature responsibility |
|---|---|
| A105 — Molecular activity | Evaluate experiments establishing a reference molecule’s activity, specificity and functional distinctions. Populate numerical components describing that experimental evidence. |
| A106 — Perturbation/restoration | Evaluate what selective perturbation and restoration establish about the studied molecule’s contribution, including controls, amounts, comparability and effect isolation. |
| A111 — Experimental structure | Evaluate what an experimental structure establishes about the studied molecule, ligand, site or molecular arrangement, including measurement limitations. |
| A108 — Organism/sample plausibility | Extract and interpret evidence answering specific biological compatibility questions about the organism or sample. Avoid an overall intuitive plausibility grade. |
| A110 — Biochemical/pathway consistency | Extract experimentally supported reaction, substrate/product, stoichiometric, resource and pathway requirements. Distinguish measured outcomes from assumptions about the surrounding system. |
| Supporting partner knowledge | Extract experiments establishing which partners are required and under which conditions. Supply those facts for later A107 use; do not perform genomic-presence or coexpression grading. |

Exclude from this literature implementation:

- A101: sequence alignment, homology scoring, coverage and target-to-reference matching.
- A102: structural alignment and target-to-reference structural correspondence calculations.
- A103: genomic-neighborhood analysis and association scoring.
- A104: phylogenetic reconstruction, placement and tree-comparison scoring.
- A107 computational measurements: genomic partner searches and coexpression analysis.

A paper may report information relevant to those excluded analyses. Preserve necessary source facts without creating literature implementations of their graders.

A109 remains removed. A110 remains separate from A108.

## Keep reference evidence and target correspondence separate

The literature workflow must establish:

1. Which molecule and experimental system were studied.
2. What was measured.
3. Which functional assertion those measurements support.
4. How well the experimental design isolates and substantiates that assertion.
5. Which limitations, contradictions and uncertainties remain.

It must not calculate how sequence or structural similarity transfers that finding to another protein.

For example:

- A101 supplies the target-to-reference alignment evidence.
- A105 supplies the experimental evidence supporting the reference’s asserted function.
- Later work determines how those contributions combine into annotation credit.

Preserve reference identity, construct boundaries, functional resolution and experimental conditions so later consumers can connect the evidence correctly. Do not multiply alignment scores into literature reliability, invent a target-transfer weight or implement combined A101/A105 credit.

## Workflow: examine cases, then develop extraction and scoring together

Use relevant examples from the retained 142 case groups, applying their corrections. Search additional original papers or datasets when a concrete unresolved question requires them.

For each selected experimental case:

### 1. Establish the experiment

Identify the actual organism, genotype, molecule, construct, experimental system, conditions, intended changed variable, reference arm and controls.

Determine whether molecules replace one another or coexist. Identify the endpoint, units, normalization, comparison denominators, uncertainty and repetition basis.

Separate what the experiment measured from what the authors inferred.

### 2. Explain its bearing on annotation quality

Determine which functional assertion the experiment substantiates and at what specificity.

Distinguish:

- Comparability outside the intended experimental change.
- Isolation of the observed effect.
- Relevance of the endpoint to the asserted function.
- Method validity and specific weaknesses.
- Signal magnitude and uncertainty.
- Independent replication, shared measurements and complementary experiments.
- Contradictions and unresolved alternative explanations.

Explain why each distinction matters. Do not substitute a generic quality label.

### 3. Define paired extraction fields and numerical components

For every useful contribution, specify together:

- The exact information the reader must extract.
- Its source location and experimental relationships.
- The numerical sheet cells it populates.
- Their operands, units, denominators and calculations.
- What each number evaluates.
- How components combine through explicit sums or multipliers.
- Missing-input, uncertainty and dependence behavior.

The existing sheets are revisable proposals. Preserve useful components; revise inappropriate ones through additive, explained changes.

Print concrete examples, proposed extraction/judgment instructions and populated numerical cells in chat before broad replication. Do not stop at tables of claims or provenance.

### 4. Implement and integrate

Define shared interfaces and file ownership before parallel coding.

Implement targeted readers and deterministic calculators, then connect them to selected work inside the existing LiteratureEpisodes. Verify persistence and delivery through the ordinary typed child-to-parent boundary.

Continue using contrasting cases to test and refine both extraction fields and sheet definitions.

## Three separate responsibilities

Keep separate:

1. Investigation instructions: what to examine and which scientific distinctions matter.
2. Numerical scoring sheets: actual numerical components and their defined meanings.
3. Readers, graders and calculators: functions populating those components.

A source-claim table is supporting recordkeeping, not a numerical scoring sheet. Prose calculation rules are not executable calculators. Do not insert complete sheets into investigation prompts.

The controlling principles remain:

> “Graders/readers are what POPULATE the scoring rubric”

> “THE PAPERS DO NOT OVERRIDE THE TEMPLATE. THEY GET CONVERTED THOUGHTFULLY”

> “These individual scores need to be multipliers or sum in some way.”

## Measurement and calculation requirements

Retain each measurement’s actual subject, endpoint, conditions, value, unit, normalization, uncertainty, origin and exact source location.

Distinguish reported, graph-derived, calculated, proposed and synthetic values.

Do not:

- Convert missing values to zero or one.
- Treat nondetection as exact zero.
- Interchange SD, SEM, confidence intervals and detection limits.
- Treat technical repetitions as independent biological experiments.
- Treat repeated representations as independent evidence.
- Convert citation counts or publication reputation into experimental validity.
- Substitute numerical categories for available measurements.

Use deterministic parsing, validation, unit conversion and arithmetic wherever possible.

Where interpretation requires a model, define a concrete aspect-specific question, supporting source material, allowed output meanings and unknown/conflicting conditions. Models identify experimental meanings and operands; calculators perform arithmetic.

Preserve useful partial results. Missing scientific information is acceptable when returned correctly. Unimplemented required functions remain unfinished work.
