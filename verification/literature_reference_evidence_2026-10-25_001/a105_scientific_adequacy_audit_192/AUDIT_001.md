Remaining scientific responsibilities

1. Relevance/specificity needs an explicit supported relation from actual measured endpoint to the particular source functional assertion and alternatives. Current source assertions are retained as context; a corrected q/E alone does not evaluate that relation.
2. Method validity has useful local calibration/control components, but method-specific weaknesses must be connected to the experiment that addresses them. A source-supported control-role interpretation is required; quote custody alone is not semantic proof.
3. Comparability must preserve nonintended changes and their actual response effects. A condition response can correct only the particular supported nuisance relationship. Conditions with different detectors, substrate concentrations, constructs or retained amounts must not automatically become a common-condition comparison.
4. Isolation must distinguish blank subtraction, measured contamination, partners and coexisting forms. Purification and replacement labels are not measured zero contamination. Supporting context does not numerically resolve a missing contributor.
5. Dependence is explicitly retained in many individual paths. A scientific account still needs to say which complementary assay resolves which weakness, which observations share a preparation or readout, and when apparently different outcomes really conflict. Those relationships must be assessed at matching subjects/endpoints/conditions, not inferred from paper count.
6. Unfinished numerical functions must remain unfinished rather than being reported as a paper omission. Examples directly seen here include product-partition uncertainty and compiled partial-normalization/bypassed-stage covariance. Broader completeness is not asserted by this list.
