## 2. Functional resolution and scientific questions

Reference conclusion supported by the combined experiments: E. coli BtsT-6His contributes to pyruvate uptake in the tested intact-cell system and mediates pyruvate uptake/exchange in reconstituted lipid membranes; imposed proton electrochemical conditions stimulate uptake, supporting proton-coupled transport. Preserve the tested substrate concentration, temperature, tag and preparation. The paper interprets BtsT as a pyruvate/H+ symporter. Direct proton flux and exact pyruvate/H+ coupling stoichiometry were not measured; authors call stoichiometry unknown. This does not establish a numerical H+:pyruvate ratio, a Kd, complete exclusion of alternative substrates, or physiological contribution under every environment.

The specific comparison choices emerge after the investigation reads the experiments:

- E1 asks whether introducing BtsT increases retained pyruvate-derived tracer signal in a common deletion host.
- E3 asks whether specified compounds alter tracer-pyruvate uptake at stated concentrations, not whether each compound is transported.
- E5 asks whether uptake changes with imposed electrical/chemical proton-driving conditions in the purified preparation.
- E6 asks whether preload identity changes accumulation/exchange; it does not supply a proton stoichiometry.

## 4. Explicit numerical compilation within reference experimental evidence

Preserve the existing sheet's principle: correct identified contributions/conditions once and propagate the actual shared uncertainty; do not attach a second quality multiplier for the same correction. The following are **proposed transport-specific equations**, not adopted overall scoring or W05.

For E1 independent preparations i, first fit accumulation a_i(t)=α_i+v_i*t over a scientifically justified initial window (not through the plateau and not forced throughzero). Use each measured replicate, normalization denominator and background. Matched BtsT and empty-vector slope contrast is d_i=vB,i−vEV,i; its mean is Σd_i/n only when these are identified independent matched preparations. Variance uses the actual paired covariance, or s_d²/n for an actual independent paired sample. The reported sixfold currently fills native contrast diagnostics, but does not supply this raw sample/covariance. If an abundance-normalized BtsT effect is wanted, divide by measured membrane BtsT amount; total cell protein does not fill that denominator. No protein amount or control equality is imputed.

For E5 the protein-specific gradient response requires both protein-containing and empty-liposome observations in each relevant condition. Let x=[vB,G,vempty,G,vB,0,vempty,0], l=[1,−1,−1,1]. Then e=l*x=(vB,G−vempty,G)−(vB,0−vempty,0); Ve=l*C*lᵀ using the full measured covariance. This is a **sum of measured contrasts**, isolating the added gradient-associated effect beyond passive uptake and baseline protein response under the stated preparation assumptions. It is different from experimental comparability: if buffer/ionic changes outside the intended gradient alter transport, a measured same-preparation control response h=u/v could map that effect once as a=e/h; h is null until measured. The intended gradient itself never fills that h. All unavailable empty-liposome rates, matching covariances and h remain null here. Reporting “slight” does not numerically close e.

For E3 define preparation rates and corresponding background b_j; net r_j=v_j−b_j. For positive measured netrates, z_j=ln(r_control/r_competitor) describes suppression of tracer uptake. Its derivatives preserve the **same control** across every z_j, so Cov(z_j,z_k) includes the common-control variance and other actual covariance. The raw inhibition fraction already in the table uses uncorrected bars and cannot be relabeled a BtsT-specific z. Cold pyruvate's isotope dilution requires its own total/labeled exposure calculation before comparing chemical flux. Direct uptake measurements for alternative substrates would supply a different selectivity contrast; they are absent, not zero. A significant p-value/star does not supply missing variance, an effect size or independent sample identity.

For a sufficiently established effect e and actual known positive variance V, the current proposed signed sheet calculation S=e*abs(e)/(2V), B=tanh(S/2), can consume the contrast. An empirically estimated variance needs its applicable finite-sample model instead of being called known. Here the source model and covariance are insufficient: `source_profile_log_ratio` and `source_signed_index` are null. **They are not zero,1,or a subjective overall grade.** Useful native cells above still survive. A sum or multiplication of the sixfold signal fraction, competition reduction, gradient ratio, Km precision and preparation count would mix repeated quantities, different endpoints and unresolved uncertainty; it is not defined as a defensible reliability score.

If later a measured same-curve substrate correction is selected, h=St*(Km+Sr)/(Sr*(Km+St)) can divide an applicable source effect once, with full covariance propagated once. A numerical value from whole cells cannot correct purified-vesicle40µM uptake. The whole-cell Fig4C contains10 and40µM concentration points under one curve: a **within-E4 conditional comparison** yields saturations .37735849 and .70796460,h=.53301887. This is a declared model-based same-curve comparison, not an observed E1→E5 comparability measurement. Unknown covariance keeps its variance null. No Km-derived correction is needed just to score E1's matched10µM arms or E5's matched40µM gradient arms.
