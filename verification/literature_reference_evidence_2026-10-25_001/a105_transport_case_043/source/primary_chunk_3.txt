E. coli LMG194 cells were transformed with pBAD24- btsT ( 7 ), and BtsT-6His was produced as described above. At each step, the pellet was resuspended in 100 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.6) containing 5 mM MgCl 2 , 5% (vol/vol) glycerol, 1 mM DTT, 1 mM PMSF, and DNase. For BtsT solubilization, 1.5% (wt/vol) n -dodecyl β- d -maltoside (Glycon Biochemicals) was added during stirring on ice for 30 min, and the sample was centrifuged at 244,000 × g for 45 min at 4°C. Ni 2+ -nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) resin, which had been preincubated with buffer W (100 mM potassium phosphate buffer [pH 7.6], 5 mM MgCl 2 , 5% [vol/vol] glycerol, and 1 mM DTT), was then added to the supernatant, and the mixture was incubated for 1 h with gentle shaking at 4°C. Unbound protein was removed by washing with buffer W containing 30 mM imidazole. Subsequently, BtsT was eluted with buffer W containing 300 mM imidazole. Purified BtsT was reconstituted into preformed liposomes prepared from an acetone-ether-washed E. coli polar lipid extract as described before ( 42 ). Prior to reconstitution, the liposomes were destabilized by the addition of 0.12% (wt/vol) Triton X-100 and then mixed with purified protein at a 100:1 ratio. Detergents were removed by adding Bio-Beads SM-2 (Bio-Rad) at a 5:1 bead/detergent ratio. Fresh Bio-Beads were added after 1 h and 2 h, and the mixture was gently stirred at 4°C overnight. Bio-Beads were removed by filtration through glass wool, and the proteoliposome suspension was dialyzed twice against buffer A (100 mM potassium phosphate buffer [pH 7.6], 5 mM MgCl 2 , and 5% [vol/vol] glycerol), and centrifuged at 160,000 × g for 1 h. The pellet was resuspended in buffer A and stored in liquid N 2 . Proteins were visualized by silver staining, and His-tagged BtsT was detected by Western blotting as described before. After thawing, samples were extruded through a 400-nm filter (Avestin) to obtain unilamellar proteoliposomes of homogeneous size, and adjusted to 4 mg/ml of protein in buffer A. Aliquots of proteoliposomes were diluted (1:200) into buffers A to E containing 80 μM valinomycin and 40 μM [ 14 C]pyruvate (55 mCi mmol −1 ) to initiate pyruvate transport, and at defined times (25°C) the entire sample was filtered through 0.2-μm Millipore filters (buffer B [ΔpH]: 100 mM potassium phosphate buffer [pH 5.8] and 5 mM MgCl 2 ; buffer C [ΔΨ]: 100 mM Tris/MES buffer [pH 7.6] and 5 mM MgCl 2 ; buffer D [Δμ̃ H+ ]: 100 mM Tris/MES buffer [pH 5.8] and 5 mM MgCl 2 ; buffer E [Δμ̃ Na+ ]: 100 mM Tris/MES [pH 5.8], 5 mM MgCl 2 , and 50 mM NaCl). For the counterflow assay, proteoliposomes were preloaded with 10 mM pyruvate or lactate at 4°C overnight. The resulting suspension was diluted 200-fold into buffer A containing [ 14 C]pyruvate at a concentration of 4.7 μM (final external pyruvate concentration of 54.7 μM) at 25°C. At defined times the entire sample was filtered, and the radioactivity of the sample was analyzed as described above.
Statistical analysis.
All experiments were repeated at least three times with independently prepared samples. Statistical analysis was carried out using GraphPad Prism (version 5.03 for Windows). Data were assessed for significance between tested groups by using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's multiple comparison post hoc test. Error bars represent standard errors of the mean.
