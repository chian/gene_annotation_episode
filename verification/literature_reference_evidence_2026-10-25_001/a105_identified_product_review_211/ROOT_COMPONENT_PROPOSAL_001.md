## Actual system and inference

The source is Kim, Heo and Ortiz de Montellano2008, DOI10.1016/j.abb.2008.02.044,
PMC2577879. The assayed molecule is Pseudomonas putida P450cam/CYP101. Mutants
were expressed in E.coli and carry a C-terminal6His tag. The paper does not
establish that every wild-type/mutant preparation has identical tagging, active
fraction, concentration calibration or preparation history. Do not fill those
gaps from a shared protein name. Table1culture yields are not assay denominators.

Purified P450 forms are assayed separately; WT and mutant P450 are alternatives,
not a mixed WT/mutant molecular pool. Each coexists with electron-transfer
partners putidaredoxin and putidaredoxin reductase. Product assays use the stated
200pmol P450/1nmol Pd/200pmol PdR reconstituted system in phosphate pH7.4 at37C.
The product and NADH methods describe different sampling/timing procedures;
matching all detailed conditions or the same physical aliquot is unknown.
The source deliberately associates endpoints within each Table3row and reports
coupling from them, which supports a descriptive displayed-rate comparison.
It does not establish calibration of another assay or target-to-reference transfer.

The functional assertion is camphor hydroxylation to5-exo-hydroxycamphor.
GC-MS plus chromatographic/mass-spectral comparison with authentic standards
addresses product identity; NADH optical consumption measures a different
endpoint. It can include unproductive redox turnover. Neither product identity
nor this ratio alone quantifies an enzyme-specific contaminant subtraction:
a numerical no-P450/partner-only blank is not supplied here.

## Combination and evidence roles

For the same operands, rho*H=P; for nu=1, eta*H=P. This is a justified
multiplication linking a reporter endpoint to identified product formation.
It exposes how much observed cofactor consumption corresponds to the claimed
conversion. It is not independent verification of a product rate already used
to calculate rho, and it is not a probability that the function is correct.
Applying rho to a different reporter measurement needs the actual preparation,
conditions, normalization and calibration linkage and shared uncertainty.

For T252N versus WT, the product activity comparison factorizes exactly:

    92/206 = (216/289) * ((92/216)/(206/289))
            = 0.7474048443 * 0.5975368572
            = 0.4466019417.

This separates altered reporter turnover from its productive utilization for
the specified chemical function. All factors share original measurements.
They must not enter compilation as three independent pieces of support. This
decomposition is descriptive while between-arm comparability remains qualified;
it does not pretend that the intended mutation is the only other difference.

T252A's427min^-1 NADH consumption and negligible/nondetected product provide the
contrasting arrangement. A high reporter rate is inadequate support for camphor
hydroxylation. Its native0does not supply an exact physical product rate, an
exact zero productive fraction or a numerical detection bound. Camphor binding
(Table4) supports binding and cannot substitute for productive chemistry.

Peroxide is an additional measured endpoint with its own controls/limit.
The water column is calculated from existing NADH/product/peroxide quantities,
as the table footnote says; its apparent closure is not an independent chemical
mass balance. Table2fitted kcat values and Table3turnover values remain distinct
experimental parameter records. Fig4's displayed double-mutantV253N conflicts
with text/Table3V253T; keep the original panel identity unresolved rather than
silently assigning it to the table row.
