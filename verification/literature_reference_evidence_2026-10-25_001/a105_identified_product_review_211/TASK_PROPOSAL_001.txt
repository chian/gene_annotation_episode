Determine what the assigned product-formation and cofactor-consumption experiments establish about the studied molecule's chemical function. Start from the supplied sequence, selected file contents or snippets and evidence actually received; an initial annotation or public searchable name is not required. Identify the organism, protein, construct, preparation, expression host, reaction system and partner molecules. Establish whether compared proteins replace one another or coexist, and distinguish the intended change from other differences.

Identify the chemically resolved product, its substrate and the actual evidence for chemical identity, including authentic standards, chromatographic separation and mass-spectral comparison where present. A reporter response or loss of a redox cofactor does not itself identify that product. Select the product and cofactor measurements with their complete units, molecular normalization, source locations, timing, conditions and source-assigned comparison. Preserve uncertainty, repetition, blanks, calibration and active-fraction information as reported; matching names or units alone does not establish applicability.

Determine separately whether the source assigns the two endpoints to a common comparison, whether their preparations and normalization agree, and whether conditions and sampling support interpreting their ratio as productive coupling. Preserve unknown or conflicting relationships without discarding the individual measurements or the author's comparison. Identify the stoichiometric convention explicitly; do not substitute one when it is unknown. Keep a descriptive ratio of displayed amounts or rates separate from a supported physical productive fraction and from applying that fraction to another assay.

Inspect surrounding Results, table notes and figures for reported zeros, nondetection, negligible activity, bounds and approximation. Preserve a printed zero as a native report while retaining its actual measurement meaning. Nondetection without a numerical detection limit supplies neither an exact zero activity nor an invented upper bound. Keep reported coupling percentages separate from values calculated from displayed rates, retaining discrepancies without assuming a rounding explanation. Do not borrow replication, spread or fitted parameters from a different table or experiment.

Ask the deterministic functions for the requested numerical components using supported source operands. A product/cofactor ratio can describe how much reporter turnover corresponds to the identified conversion. Its multiplication by the same cofactor rate reconstructs the original product rate and provides no additional independent evidence. Preserve shared normalization, measurement reuse and covariance in comparisons and uncertainty calculations; unknown covariance does not mean independence. Compare mutant and reference contributions only with the actual experimental relationships and limitations intact.

Treat product identity, cofactor use, peroxide measurements, binding and inferred residual products as answering distinct questions. Binding does not establish catalysis; a residual calculated by subtraction is not an independently measured mass balance. A complementary experiment can resolve a named weakness without becoming independent replication or a separate confidence multiplier. Retain valid partial results and distinguish missing source facts from unimplemented calculations. Return only the parent's requested numerical information and constrained scientific interpretations. Keep function probability, target correspondence, annotation credit and continuation decisions outside these calculations.
