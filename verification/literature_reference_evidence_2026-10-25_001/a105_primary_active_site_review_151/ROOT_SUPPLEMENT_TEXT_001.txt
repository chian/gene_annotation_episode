2. Determination of Substrate Solubility

Substrate solubility was determined by the shake flask method using HPLC analysis. About 1 mg (substrates 2, 5) or
1.25 mg (substrates 3, 4) was weighed into a reaction tube. A volume of 1 mL (2, 5) or 250 µL (3, 4) of assay buffer
(50 mmol/L MOPS, 10 mmol/L NaCl, 1 mmol/L EDTA and 0.01% Triton X-100, pH 7.2) containing 5% (v/v) DMSO
was added. Subsequently, the suspensions were shaken for 1 h with 450 rpm and 37 °C and then centrifuged at 15,000
rpm for 10 min. Aliquots of 450 µL (2) or 50 µL (3, 4, 5) of the supernatants were removed and diluted with 50 µL
(2) or 950 µL (3, 4, 5) of acetonitrile to prevent precipitation of the dissolved compounds and to obtain sufficient
volume for the measurement. Substrate 2 was analyzed on a Poroshell 120 EC-C18 3 × 50 mm, 2.7 µm column with
an injection volume of 20 µL and a flow rate of 0.7 mL/min. The substrates 3, 4 and 5 were analyzed on a Nucleosil
EC 4 × 250 mm, 5 µm column with an injection volume of 10 µL (3, 4) or 20 µL (5) and a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min.
Elution occurred within 12 min (2) or 15 min (3, 4, 5) in a linear gradient between eluent A consisting of 0.1% TFA
in water, and eluent B consisting of 0.1% TFA in acetonitrile. The starting concentration of eluent B was 20% (2) or
30% (3, 4, 5) and the final concentration was 80% (2) or 95% (3, 4, 5). UV detection was carried out at 270 nm
(2, 3, 4) or 490 nm (5). For the determination of the sample concentrations, calibration lines for each substrate were
obtained. The respective compound was accurately weighed and a DMSO stock solution of 1 mg/mL was prepared.
The solution was diluted with acetonitrile to yield six different concentrations between 1 and 300 µg/mL, which were
analyzed by HPLC. The peak area was plotted against the calibration solution concentration and thus sample
concentrations could be calculated under consideration of the dilution of the supernatants with acetonitrile.

3. X-ray Crystallography

E. coli strain BL21(DE3) was transformed with the sequence-verified SARS-CoV-2 Mpro construct as previously
described1. Transformed clones were pre-cultured at 37 °C in 100 mL LB-medium with ampicillin (100 μg/mL)
overnight. The next morning, the incubated culture was inoculated into 2 L of LB-medium supplied with
100 μg/mL ampicillin. 1 mmol/L isopropyl-D-thiogalactoside (IPTG) was added for induction of the Mpro gene at
37 °C when the OD600 reached 0.5. After 4 h, cells were harvested by centrifugation at 10,000 × g and 4 °C for
10 min. The pellet was either frozen at -80 °C or directly processed further. The pellet was resuspended in 20 mL
of buffer A (20 mmol/L Tris, 150 mmol/L NaCl, pH 7.8; pH values of all buffers were adjusted at room
temperature) and then lyzed by repeated sonication. The lysate was centrifuged at 48,000 ×g and 4 °C for 30 min.
The supernatant was loaded onto a HiTrap Talon crude 5 mL column (Cytiva) equilibrated with buffer A. The
column was washed with 10 column volumes of buffer A to remove nonspecifically binding proteins, followed
by elution using buffer B (20 mmol/L Tris, 150 mmol/L NaCl, 500 mmol/L imidazole, pH 7.8) applying a linear
gradient of imidazole ranging from 0 to 250 mmol/L within 30 column volumes. The fractions containing the
target protein were pooled and mixed with human rhinovirus (HRV) 3C protease (ThermoFisher) at a
concentration of 5 U/mg protein and dialyzed into buffer C (20 mmol/L Tris, 150 mmol/L NaCl, 1 mmol/L tris(2-
carboxyethyl)phosphine (TCEP), pH 7.8) at 4 °C overnight. The HRV 3C protease-treated Mpro was again applied
to a connected HiTrap Talon crude 5 mL column (Cytiva) to remove the His-tagged HRV 3C protease, the His-
tag, and protein with uncleaved His-tag. The His-tag-free Mpro in the flow-through was subjected to buffer
exchange with buffer D (20 mmol/L 2-[4-(2-hydroxyethyl)piperazin-1-yl]ethanesulfonic acid (HEPES), 1 mmol/L
TCEP, pH 7.4) using Amicon Ultra 15 centrifugal filters (10 kDa molecular weight cut-off, Merck Millipore) at
5000 × g, and 4 °C.

Freshly prepared protein (as described above) at a concentration of 3 mg/mL (around 100 µmol/L) was mixed
with compound 1 (dissolved in 100% DMSO) at a molar ratio of 1:2. The mixture was incubated at room
temperature for 1 h and then centrifuged at 12,000 × g. Subsequently, a basic screen with the commercially
available Morpheus HT (Molecular Dimensions) was performed by using a Formulatrix NT8-Drop Setter
employing the sitting-drop vapor-diffusion method at 18 °C. Protein solution (0.15 μL) and 0.15 μL of reservoir
were mixed to equilibrate against 40 μL of reservoir solution containing 200 µmol/L of titrant 1. Crystals started
appearing after 48 h applying the following conditions: 0.1 mol/L HEPES/3-(N-morpholino)propanesulfonic acid
(MOPS) (pH 7.5, 20 °C), 30 mmol/L sodium fluoride, 30 mmol/L sodium bromide, 30 mmol/L sodium iodide,
12% (v/v) polyethylene glycol (PEG) methyl ether 500, 6% (w/v) PEG20000. Subsequently, crystals were
harvested and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen without additional cryo-protection.

X-ray diffraction data were collected at 100 K at beamline P14 at the DESY synchrotron in Hamburg, Germany
(Table S1). The diffraction data were indexed, integrated and scaled with XDS2 and STARANISO3 as
implemented in ISPyB4 at DESY. The obtained crystal form has so far only been observed for a complex structure
of SARS-CoV-1 main protease (PDB ID 1UJ1)5, which was used as a starting model for refinement in Phenix6.
Coot7 was used for model building. The structure contains two copies in the asymmetric unit, which were refined
with non-crystallographic symmetry restraints on the torsion angles. The difference density indicated binding of
the ligand in both proteins of the asymmetric unit. Stereochemical restraints for ligand refinement were generated
                                                          S10
using grade (https://grade.globalphasing.org). In chain A of the asymmetric unit, the average B-factor of the ligand
(54.0 Å2) was significantly higher compared to chain B (23.6 Å2). A similar difference was observed for the
average B-factor of the surrounding amino acids for chain A (42.9 Å2) and chain B (26.0 Å2). The average
B-factors of the complete proteins were similar with B = 32.4 Å2 for chain A and 31.9 Å2 for chain B. The lower
B-factors of the ligand and its environment in chain B is likely a result of crystal packing interactions of the
Cbz capping group of the ligand and residues of the neighboring loops. This is in contrast to chain A, where the
protease active site and the ligand are situated at more than 8 Å distance to symmetry mates in the crystal structure.
Nevertheless, a superposition of both chains shows very similar binding modes of the ligands in the two chains
(Fig. S10B). The terminal benzyl moiety of 1, part of the Cbz group, lacks sufficient electron density and is
presumed to be flexible. Overall, the B-factors and the electron density of 1 suggest full occupancy of the ligand.

4.1. General Procedures and Instruments

Chemicals, reagents and solvents were purchased from abcr GmbH (Karlsruhe, Germany), Sigma-Aldrich Chemie
GmbH (Taufkirchen, Germany), TCI Deutschland GmbH (Eschborn, Germany), BLD Pharmatech GmbH
(Kaiserslautern, Germany), Fisher Scientific GmbH (Schwerte) and JULI.O GmbH (Jülich). Thin-layer
chromatography was conducted on silica gel 60 F254 aluminum sheets (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt) and detected at
254 nm. Retardation factors (Rf) are quoted. Melting points are determined on a Büchi (Essen, Germany) M-560
apparatus. 1H NMR (500 MHz) and 13C NMR (126 MHz) spectra were recorded on a Bruker (Rheinstetten, Germany)
DRX 500 MHz Avance and 1H NMR (600 MHz) and 13C NMR (151 MHz) spectra on a Bruker Ascend 600 at
300 – 303 K. Chemical shift values δ are given in ppm and are referenced to solvent signals, DMSO-d6 2.50/39.70
ppm. Flash chromatography was carried out at Interchim SAS (Montluçon, France) puriFlash XS520Plus. LC-MS
analyses (method A) were performed on an Agilent (Santa Clara, CA, United States) HPLC 1260 Infinity II using an
EC50/2 Nucleodur C18 Gravity 3 µm column (Macherey-Nagel, Düren, Germnany) coupled to an Agilent (Santa
Clara, CA, United States) Infinity Lab LC/MDS-system with ESI-source. The column temperature was 40 °C. The
samples (1 mg/mL) were dissolved in methanol containing 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 2 µL of this solution
were injected. The method was a run with a gradient from 90% 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate in H2O to 100%
acetonitrile within 10 min and consecutively a flush with 100% acetonitrile for 10 min with a flowrate of 0.5 mL/min.
The UV absorption was detected from 190 – 600 nm using a diode array detector (DAD) and purity was determined
at 220 – 600 nm, if not mentioned otherwise. Positive and negative total ion scans were recorded from 100 – 1000
m/z or more if necessary. Otherwise, LC-MS analyses (method B) were obtained with an HPLC HP1100 (Agilent)
using an EC50/2 Nucleodur C18 Gravity 3 µm column (Macherey-Nagel) coupled with an API2000 mass
spectrometer (ABSciex) with ESI-source. The column temperature was 25 °C. The samples (1 mg/mL) were dissolved
in methanol, containing 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate, and 8 µL were injected. The method was a run (0.3 mL/min)
with a gradient from 90% 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate in H2O to 100% 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate in methanol
within 10 min and a subsequent flush with 100% of 2 mmol/L ammonium acetate in methanol. The UV absorption
was detected from 190 – 900 nm using a diode array detector (DAD) and purity was determined at 220 – 400 nm, if
not stated otherwise. Positive and negative total ion scans were recorded from 150 – 800 m/z. HRMS spectra (method
C) were measured on an LTQ Orbitrap XL (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) with HPLC autosampler.
Samples were dissolved in acetonitrile. Positive or negative full scans were observed from 200 – 1000 m/z or more if
necessary. Alternatively, HRMS data (method D) were obtained using an HPLC Dionex Ultimate 3000 (Thermo
Scientific) using flow injection mode coupled with a micrOTOF-Q mass spectrometer (Bruker) with ESI-source. The
method run (0.3 mL/min) was an isocratic flush of 0.1% acetic acid in acetonitrile. Positive or negative full scans
were recorded from 50 – 1000 m/z (or more if necessary). Sodium acetate was used as calibrant.
