We purified CsiD and demonstrated by NMR (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Figures 1, 2) and LC-MS that it indeed hydroxylates GA to 2-hydroxyglutarate, while the co-substrate αKG is converted to succinate (SA) (and CO2), as is common for this enzyme class10,11. By derivatisation of the product with diacetyl-l-tartaric anhydride12 we demonstrated that L2HG is produced in a highly stereospecific manner; no D-2-hydroxyglutarate was detectable (Supplementary Figure 3). This finding is in accordance with the reported specificity for the L-enantiomer of the subsequent LhgO-catalysed reaction8. When the reaction was measured with a Clark O2 electrode, we determined a specific activity of 53+/− 3 µmol min−1 mg−1 and an apparent Km = 650+/− 20 µM for GA and Km = 100+/− 7 µM for αKG (Fig. 2b, c). Other dicarboxylic acids (oxalate, malonate, SA, adipate, and pimelate tested) were not converted. The physiological role of CsiD as glutarate-metabolising enzyme in the stationary phase of E. coli was confirmed when we tested its csiD knockout strain (ΔcsiD) by LC-MS of small-molecule extracts: with carbon starvation and entry into the stationary phase, the intracellular concentration of GA accumulated to much higher levels in the ΔcsiD strain than compared to the wildtype (Fig. 2d).
