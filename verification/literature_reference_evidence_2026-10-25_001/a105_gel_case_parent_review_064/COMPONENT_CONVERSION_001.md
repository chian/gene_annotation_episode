## How amounts can enter a quantitative experimental component

An amount calibration would turn signal into an applicable molecular amount
ratio only if the source establishes the assay's response/linearity and matching
sampling basis. That ratio can enter a matched direct-rate comparison once,
under a supported linear amount-response model. For replacement arms:

    amount_ratio = amount_variant / amount_reference
    rate_ratio = corrected_rate_variant / corrected_rate_reference
    per_amount_response_ratio = rate_ratio / amount_ratio

Each corrected rate needs its own applicable background correction and matched
conditions. A synthetic rate_ratio0.20 and validated amount_ratio0.64 would give
0.3125. The amount-denominator multiplier is1/0.64=1.5625, applied once. It is
not a generic confidence multiplier. This paper's anti-His signal alone does
not license that calibration or correction; recovery lag is not a linear rate
operand for this formula.

For a separately established coexistence experiment under an additive rate
model, total relative rate can instead have the form

    total_relative_rate = WT_amount_fraction * WT_specific_response
                        + variant_amount_fraction * variant_specific_response

With reference-normalized WT_specific_response1, known fractions0.60/0.40 and
synthetic total_relative_rate0.68, variant_specific_response=(0.68-0.60)/0.40=0.20.
Those fractions require calibrated amounts and the additive model; they cannot
be inferred from YeaG's0.64 signal. This explicit sum isolates a different
question from replacement's ratio. Neither model calculates target transfer or
W05 final annotation credit.

No squared-deviation aggregation or new overall comparability weight is
