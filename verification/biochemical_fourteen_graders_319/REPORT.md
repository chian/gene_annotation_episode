# Audit of the fourteen biochemical implementation entries

The fourteen entries are **fourteen reader entrypoints with associated calculation code, not evidence of fourteen distinct assays or fourteen complete assay graders**. Several explicitly share their calculator. Several are different measurements from the same experiment. Others specialize a published workbook layout. The implementation does not support the claim that all fourteen needed separate assay graders.

This report audits the exact fourteen entries requested. It does not change the agreed experiment-type rows or count these entries as additional subscoring sheets. Each may supply information to the **same biochemical-assay subscoring sheet**, whose common scientific questions concern the reaction, preparation, detection system, controls, assay regime, conditions, uncertainty, and applicability to the annotation claim. It is possible to retain useful measurement-specific calculations within that common assessment without declaring every calculation or parser a new assay type.

The comparison standard is the first subagent's [guide report](/home/chia/repos/gene_annotation_episode/verification/biochemical_assay_guides_318/REPORT.md), including [STRENDA](https://www.beilstein-institut.de/en/projects/strenda/guidelines/), [Assay Guidance Manual enzyme-assay basics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK92007/), [enzymatic versus protein purity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK91995/), and [optical interference](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK343429/). A source quotation establishing what was reported is different from a calculation evaluating its implication. The audit distinguishes those explicitly below.

## Direct answer: why did all fourteen need separate graders?

**They did not all need separate graders, and the code does not show that fourteen separate complete assay graders were built.** Concrete evidence is stronger than a general objection:

- Entries 2 and 3 call `populate_rate_contrast` through two different input paths in the same storage module. That is direct evidence of different readers for the same calculation.
- Entries 1 and 10 share `populate_corrected_activity`. Entry 10 adds applicability checks and compilation of existing quantities, rather than introducing an assay.
- Entries 5 and 6 use different measurements from the same HFP paper. Endpoint completion time and equilibrium product composition need different calculations, but neither measurement changes the experiment into another assay type.
- Entries 7 and 8 distinguish a workbook's reported entries from observations with actual sampling-unit identities. That difference matters for valid uncertainty estimates. It is a distinction in data structure and statistical support, not a biochemical assay taxonomy.
- Entries 13 and 14 explicitly encode original workbook layouts. Entry 14 also implements substantive stoichiometry and calibration-range checks. The layout alone cannot justify a new grader.

Some separate calculation functions are warranted. Completion time cannot silently become initial rate; disappearance cannot silently become product identity; redox coupling cannot silently become molecular attribution; graph digitization uncertainty cannot silently become experimental variance. Preserving those distinctions does not require a separate subscoring sheet for each paper or parser.

The common biochemical sheet can receive all of these outputs because the measurements supply different parts of the **same finer-grained biochemical assessment**: what chemical transformation was measured, whether the detected signal represents it, whether the catalyst/preparation and amount are appropriate, whether controls exclude spontaneous or competing chemistry, whether concentrations/time/conditions support the claimed interpretation, and how uncertainty and repeated observations should be represented. Assay-specific applicability remains necessary; a retained statement about 90 °C heating or 60 °C operation is not yet a judgment about its effect on validity or relevance.

This audit found useful implemented calculations and safeguards. It did **not** establish that the common biochemical reliability/validity/relevance assessment is complete. Nor does a missing selected operand show that the paper failed to report it: `not_selected`, `unknown`, unsupported parsing, and explicitly unimplemented analysis must stay distinct.
