## What the focal validation establishes

Focus: purified **E. coli DeoA**, uridine plus orthophosphate, assayed *in vitro*. Article XML `Sec8/Par20–21`, main `Fig5c–d`: the paper proposes phosphorylytic uridine cleavage, and Figure 5c plots **uridine ion depletion** together with **uracil** and **D-ribose-1-phosphate ion accumulation**. Main Figure 5c also shows the corresponding heat-inactivated DeoA control traces. Both proteins do not coexist as active/inactive molecules in one vessel: these are separate assay arms. The intended changed variable is DeoA catalytic competence after heat treatment; heating may alter other protein properties, and the source does not establish a matched protein-free blank or a protein-purity-specific contaminant exclusion. Product labels are proposed molecular assignments from accurate mass and reaction context, not unique chemical identities established by standards or orthogonal structure-sensitive measurement.

Article XML `Sec24/Par50`: validation reaction described as 50 µg/mL enzyme in 150 µL, 10 mM Tris·HCl pH 7.5, 1 mM MgCl2, 10 mM **each substrate**, 37 °C; 10 µL aliquots quenched in 30 µL cold methanol; at least duplicate experiments, at least two independent enzyme purifications; heat-inactivated control held at 95 °C for 60 min. `Sec23/Par48–49` describes affinity plus anion-exchange purification, fractions selected by SDS-PAGE. The paper does not show a source table mapping the plotted main Figure 5c points to preparation identities or pairing active and inactive replicates.

**Condition conflict:** Supplement PDF, `Supplementary Figure 6` caption (printed pp. 7–8), says 50 µg/mL enzyme and **1 mM each substrate**, with mean ± SD of **3 replicates**, for its DeoA validation panels. Its panel c contains uridine, deoxyuridine, and sodium phosphate. The 1 mM caption conflicts tenfold with the main Methods 10 mM statement, and that SI panel's mixture differs from the main Fig. 5c description. Do not assign n=3 or the SI substrate concentration to main Figure 5c without a source link. Treat concentration as conflicting and the displayed main-Fig5c replicate count as unknown, while retaining the Methods lower bounds separately.

`Sec17/Par36` describes online **mass-axis** correction, which is not an analyte concentration calibration. `Sec18/Par37` annotates ions by accurate m/z within **0.005 Da**, assumed adducts and KEGG masses. `Sec3/Par7` explicitly notes isomer and in-source-fragment limitations. `Sec19/Par38–40` defines the separate screen's relative abundances and normalized Z scores. The Figure 5c y axes are **ion intensity (counts)**, with different scales across panels. Neither the figure nor the inspected methods/supplement supplies analyte-specific response factors, authentic-standard recovery, unique product confirmation, or absolute molar concentration time courses. The chemical reaction drawings in Fig. 5d and KEGG reconstruction in `Sec7/Par18` are hypotheses, not a measured stoichiometric balance.

**Numerical combination relationship:** the within-channel controlled contrasts are operands for one source-experiment activity component. Chemical identity validation and molar coupling would constrain the interpretation of that same component. They are not independent papers or extra additive credits. A valid future assay-specific product-coupling component could combine `Δn_S` and `Δn_P` through the ratio above, with uncertainty propagated from the **joint** substrate/product/control measurements. Here that component remains null because its operands are unavailable. No proposed weights or final arithmetic can be source-grounded from this paper.

## Implications for current implementation

The current one A105 worksheet (`gene_annotation_episode/literature_a105_worksheet.py`) can link multiple numerical source sections to an assertion and track reused quantities. It does not itself extract a general multi-line, multi-panel MS time series. `literature_endpoint_signal_inputs.py` and its calculation module handle a particular three-point single-trace baseline-correction geometry; they must not be misapplied to Figure 5c's active-vs-inactive **multiple ion channels**. A future targeted graph/time-course reader could populate within-channel control contrasts with digitization bounds. It would still leave molar substrate/product coupling unavailable until calibration and chemical identification are supplied. This case therefore reveals a concrete A105 assay-reader gap without changing the parent sheet or claiming completed calibration/stoichiometric grading.
